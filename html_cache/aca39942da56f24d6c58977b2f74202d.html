Hi Vladik,

It's seems like you've already decided but remember it's good to try different models on and find the one you like the best.

Some skaters in my club are in the Electra model (I think that's what you are in now). My impression is that this is a medium-low support boot (they are listing it for doubles likely for lightweight skaters), and moving up to the Royal Pro, which is a medium-high support boot, would be a big jump. By bad luck, I am currently in a too stiff boot and it really negatively affects my skating as it does not allow me to get a full ankle bend in the boot, and even attaining a moderate bend requires a lot of muscle action from the shin which is super tiring. I can lace them more loosely, however they then start to become unstable around the ankle. I will be replacing them as soon as possible. So if you go with the Royal Pro, make sure you feel comfortable with its support level when you try them on in the shop.

For figuring out Jackson boots, just download their catalog as it describes all of them pretty well. One of our coaches, who is strong and big-boned, skates in the Debut and hasn't broken it down even after using it for a few years already. I consider it to be a good mid-level boot that would likely suit you well. You could also try their Premier model, which is the next one up in support, and see how you like that.

Compared to the cost of lessons and ice time, boots are not that expensive -- it's just that you have to pay for them all at once! So it can be tempting to buy a high level boot with the goal of having it last a long long time, but it is a problem to get something that is too strong. So keep that in mind and try on a few different models to see which is best.


Mark

I would suggest lacing and relacing a few times before you get on the ice and making sure that you tie them up to the top as I have seen people who only lace up to the second last hook when they move to lacing all the way at the top their tongue has practically been destroyed and has no elasticity so almost sits open too much, this may make the boots feel too loose vs doing them to the top and simply skating in them.

With my latest boots, I have found that almost trying to break them in evenly is what is working best. So for example say you predominantly skate on your right inside in regards to turns or pushes you would want to try and split your time between all different edges and directions giving more time to your weaker ones so that you still force the boot to conform for that. If you aren't even with your time you can cause the boot to twist in ways you don't want which can make getting onto certain edges harder.

Lastly focus on knee bend, focus on pressing your knees over your toes against the tongue and getting proper bend, if you don't do this you won't ever break them in properly, only the padding will go.

@Vladik , @Mauve , @emilinkaa . Hope you find this useful after you get your new skates. If you need any clarification, please ask.

Initial Break-In of New Figure Skating Boots at Home

(1) Extra Supplies Needed

(a) Laces. Assuming the laces that come with your new boots are the right length for the way you prefer to tie them (sometimes they are too short or too long), buy a pair of laces that are about 10 to 12 inches longer for break-in. Even with initial heat molding, new boots won’t fully conform to your feet at first; longer laces will make it easier to tie at first because the spacings across the rows of eyelets and hooks before break-in are larger than they are after break-in.

(b) Blade Guards. If you normally use expensive blade guards (such as Rockerz or Edea E-Guard), buy a less expensive pair for break-in. You will be breaking in the boots while pressing the blades down in the blade guards. You want new blade guards free of abrasive grit. You don’t want to excessively cut into expensive blade guards.

DO NOT buy a pair that uses plastic straps such as

https://www.ar-sports.com/figure-bladegards/figurebladegards_gold/

or

Centipede Figure

Guardog™ #8 Centipede Figure is Guardog's most poplar skateguard for recreational figure skaters. Available in a fun array of colours for skaters of all ages.
jacksonultima.com

Make sure they have metal springs and fit snug on the blades. You want something like these (local brands will vary):

SolidZ

Available in Red, White and Black. - Jackson Ultima Skates
jacksonultima.com

(c) Floor Mat. You want a floor mat with an anti-skid rubber backing. The top can be rubber or carpet. Examples are door mats, bathtub mats, or yoga mats (but don’t use an expensive one because you’ll be wearing ruts into it).

(2) Workspace

The best place for break-in exercises is an interior single-width doorway, preferably without a raised threshold on the floor (but a raised threshold is OK as long as it’s not too wide). The doorway should not be located near stairs. You place the floor mat on the floor in front of the open doorway. You want to be able to stand in front of the doorway and spread your arms, with your left hand pressing against one side of the door frame and your right hand pressing against the other side. This gives you full support to prevent you from slipping. There must be enough open flat floor space in front of the doorway for you to stand there with your skates on.

(3) Lacing Up

Put your blade guards on before you lace up. Make sure the heel of your foot is fully seated in the heel pocket of the boot. I personally don’t like to bang the heel of the blade against the floor, like many skaters do; I prefer to press the blade guard against the floor; grasp (using both hands) the exterior sole and heel of the boot; and wiggle (up/down and side/side) the heel of my foot until it’s fully seated. Make sure the tongue is straight.

Lace up snugly through to the top row of eyelets. Some boots have offset eyelets near the flex notch. If yours do, I consider that to be the top row. At the top row, loop the laces around themselves 3 or 4 times, depending on the lace. This is what I mean: When you normally tie your shoes, at the top row you loop your laces once before you tie the bow. But instead of looping them once, you loop them 3 or 4 times. You should still be able to pull the laces tight; but once you let go, the tension along the eyelets should stay snug.

Check the laces along the eyelets so far. If there are loose spots, snug them up, and pull the laces at the top row to take up the slack.
Now lace up a bit looser along the hooks. That’s the purpose of the 3 or 4 loops at the top row of the eyelets: it allows you to maintain higher tension along the eyelets and lighter tension along the hooks. Many skaters don’t do this; and of course, if the laces around the hooks are looser, the laces along the eyelets will eventually get looser too. Then you have to stop and re-tie all the time.

At first, don’t lace all the way up; i.e., don’t lace the top row of hooks. Just below the top row, loop the laces around themselves 2 times and tie your bow the way you prefer.

(4) Break-In Exercises

You are now ready to go through several sequences of break-in exercises. It’s best to spread them out over several days to let your feet and boots both recover. To maximize the service life of your boots, you want to break them in slowly and gradually, to allow the material to stretch and deform, rather than tear or crack. This also reduces irritation to your feet.

(5) First Sequence of Exercises

(A) So now (a) You have your blade guards on, (b) You’ve laced up, and (c) You’ve got your floor mat placed in front of the doorway. Double check that the floor mat does not skid.

(B) Face the doorway as if you were about to walk through it. Stand there, blade guards on the floor mat. Both skates firmly planted; legs just spread slightly like you’re normally standing; boots should not touch each other; weight should be on the flats of the blade guards, not tilted. Spread your arms to the side; elbows at shoulder height; hands raised up, palms forward; your left palm pressing lightly against one side of the door frame and your right palm pressing lightly against the other side. You should feel secure, but don’t press too hard: your palms should be able to slide up and down along the sides of the door frame.

(C) Do light up-down knee-and-ankle bends; knees straight over toes. Maintain proper posture; keep your back and shoulders straight. Keep your palms pressing lightly against the door frame and let them slide up and down as you do your up-down knee-and-ankle bends. Repeat 20 up-down knee-and-ankle bends. Not too quickly, about 1 up-down knee-and-ankle bend per 2 or 3 sec or so.

(D) Then sit down and check your laces. If there are any loose spots, snug them up.

(E) Repeat Steps (C) and (D) four more times (total of 100 light up-down knee-and-ankle bends).

(6) Second Sequence of Exercises

Now repeat Step 5 with medium up-down knee-and-ankle bends (total of 100 medium up-down knee-and-ankle bends). Remember to check laces after every 20 bends, and snug up if needed.

(7) Third Sequence of Exercises

Now repeat Step 5 with deep up-down knee-and-ankle bends (total of 100 up-down deep knee-and-ankle bends). Remember to check laces after every 20 bends, and snug up if needed.

(8) Fourth Sequence of Exercises

Now re-lace the boots, this time using all the hooks. Repeat the entire sequence of exercises in Step (5) through Step (7): 5 series of 20 light up-down knee-and-ankle bends; followed by 5 series of 20 medium up-down knee-and-ankle bends; followed by 5 series of 20 deep up-down knee-and-ankle bends. Remember to check laces after every 20 bends, and snug up if needed.

(9) Fifth Sequence of Exercises

The boots are now laced up all the way. Back to the doorway as before. Do a medium knee-and-ankle bend. Hold that position. Press your palms firmly against the door frame. Now flex your boots to the left and then to the right, slalom style. You will now be tilting the blade guards left and right. Repeat 100 left-right flexes.

(10) Sixth Sequence of Exercises

Repeat Step (9), except initially do a deep knee-and-ankle bend, then 100 left-right flexes. Remember to press your palms firmly against the door frame.

(11) On Ice ... Finally!

You are now ready to hit the ice. Proceed slowly. In between ice sessions, it’s still helpful to do up-down knee-and-ankle bends and left-right flexes off ice until you’re comfortable with the boots.

To maximize the service life of your boots, you want to break them in slowly and gradually, to allow the material to stretch and deform, rather than tear or crack. This also reduces irritation to your feet.

(5) First Sequence of Exercises

(A) So now (a) You have your blade guards on, (b) You’ve laced up, and (c) You’ve got your floor mat placed in front of the doorway. Double check that the floor mat does not skid.

(B) Face the doorway as if you were about to walk through it. Stand there, blade guards on the floor mat. Both skates firmly planted; legs just spread slightly like you’re normally standing; boots should not touch each other; weight should be on the flats of the blade guards, not tilted. Spread your arms to the side; elbows at shoulder height; hands raised up, palms forward; your left palm pressing lightly against one side of the door frame and your right palm pressing lightly against the other side. You should feel secure, but don’t press too hard: your palms should be able to slide up and down along the sides of the door frame.

(C) Do light up-down knee-and-ankle bends; knees straight over toes. Maintain proper posture; keep your back and shoulders straight. Keep your palms pressing lightly against the door frame and let them slide up and down as you do your up-down knee-and-ankle bends. Repeat 20 up-down knee-and-ankle bends. Not too quickly, about 1 up-down knee-and-ankle bend per 2 or 3 sec or so.

(D) Then sit down and check your laces. If there are any loose spots, snug them up.

(E) Repeat Steps (C) and (D) four more times (total of 100 light up-down knee-and-ankle bends).

(6) Second Sequence of Exercises

Now repeat Step 5 with medium up-down knee-and-ankle bends (total of 100 medium up-down knee-and-ankle bends). Remember to check laces after every 20 bends, and snug up if needed.

(7) Third Sequence of Exercises

Now repeat Step 5 with deep up-down knee-and-ankle bends (total of 100 up-down deep knee-and-ankle bends). Remember to check laces after every 20 bends, and snug up if needed.

(8) Fourth Sequence of Exercises

Now re-lace the boots, this time using all the hooks. Repeat the entire sequence of exercises in Step (5) through Step (7): 5 series of 20 light up-down knee-and-ankle bends; followed by 5 series of 20 medium up-down knee-and-ankle bends; followed by 5 series of 20 deep up-down knee-and-ankle bends. Remember to check laces after every 20 bends, and snug up if needed.

(9) Fifth Sequence of Exercises

The boots are now laced up all the way. Back to the doorway as before. Do a medium knee-and-ankle bend. Hold that position. Press your palms firmly against the door frame. Now flex your boots to the left and then to the right, slalom style. You will now be tilting the blade guards left and right. Repeat 100 left-right flexes.

(10) Sixth Sequence of Exercises

Repeat Step (9), except initially do a deep knee-and-ankle bend, then 100 left-right flexes. Remember to press your palms firmly against the door frame.

(11) On Ice ... Finally!

You are now ready to hit the ice. Proceed slowly. In between ice sessions, it’s still helpful to do up-down knee-and-ankle bends and left-right flexes off ice until you’re comfortable with the boots.

* It's hard to remove grit once it's been embedded in plastic. Washing with soap and water only gets rid of superficial dirt. Initial Break-In of New Figure Skating Boots at Home

(1) Extra Supplies Needed

(a) Laces. Assuming the laces that come with your new boots are the right length for the way you prefer to tie them (sometimes they are too short or too long), buy a pair of laces that are about 10 to 12 inches longer for break-in. Even with initial heat molding, new boots won’t fully conform to your feet at first; longer laces will make it easier to tie at first because the spacings across the rows of eyelets and hooks before break-in are larger than they are after break-in.

(b) Blade Guards. If you normally use expensive blade guards (such as Rockerz or Edea E-Guard), buy a less expensive pair for break-in. You will be breaking in the boots while pressing the blades down in the blade guards. You want new blade guards free of abrasive grit. You don’t want to excessively cut into expensive blade guards.

DO NOT buy a pair that uses plastic straps such as

https://www.ar-sports.com/figure-bladegards/figurebladegards_gold/

or

Centipede Figure

Guardog™ #8 Centipede Figure is Guardog's most poplar skateguard for recreational figure skaters. Available in a fun array of colours for skaters of all ages.
jacksonultima.com

Make sure they have metal springs and fit snug on the blades. You want something like these (local brands will vary):

SolidZ

Available in Red, White and Black. - Jackson Ultima Skates
jacksonultima.com

(c) Floor Mat. You want a floor mat with an anti-skid rubber backing. The top can be rubber or carpet. Examples are door mats, bathtub mats, or yoga mats (but don’t use an expensive one because you’ll be wearing ruts into it).

(2) Workspace

The best place for break-in exercises is an interior single-width doorway, preferably without a raised threshold on the floor (but a raised threshold is OK as long as it’s not too wide). The doorway should not be located near stairs. You place the floor mat on the floor in front of the open doorway. You want to be able to stand in front of the doorway and spread your arms, with your left hand pressing against one side of the door frame and your right hand pressing against the other side. This gives you full support to prevent you from slipping. There must be enough open flat floor space in front of the doorway for you to stand there with your skates on.

(3) Lacing Up

Put your blade guards on before you lace up. Make sure the heel of your foot is fully seated in the heel pocket of the boot. I personally don’t like to bang the heel of the blade against the floor, like many skaters do; I prefer to press the blade guard against the floor; grasp (using both hands) the exterior sole and heel of the boot; and wiggle (up/down and side/side) the heel of my foot until it’s fully seated. Make sure the tongue is straight.

Lace up snugly through to the top row of eyelets. Some boots have offset eyelets near the flex notch. If yours do, I consider that to be the top row. At the top row, loop the laces around themselves 3 or 4 times, depending on the lace. This is what I mean: When you normally tie your shoes, at the top row you loop your laces once before you tie the bow. But instead of looping them once, you loop them 3 or 4 times. You should still be able to pull the laces tight; but once you let go, the tension along the eyelets should stay snug.

Check the laces along the eyelets so far. If there are loose spots, snug them up, and pull the laces at the top row to take up the slack.
Now lace up a bit looser along the hooks. That’s the purpose of the 3 or 4 loops at the top row of the eyelets: it allows you to maintain higher tension along the eyelets and lighter tension along the hooks. Many skaters don’t do this; and of course, if the laces around the hooks are looser, the laces along the eyelets will eventually get looser too. Then you have to stop and re-tie all the time.

At first, don’t lace all the way up; i.e., don’t lace the top row of hooks. Just below the top row, loop the laces around themselves 2 times and tie your bow the way you prefer.

(4) Break-In Exercises

You are now ready to go through several sequences of break-in exercises. It’s best to spread them out over several days to let your feet and boots both recover. To maximize the service life of your boots, you want to break them in slowly and gradually, to allow the material to stretch and deform, rather than tear or crack. This also reduces irritation to your feet.

(5) First Sequence of Exercises

(A) So now (a) You have your blade guards on, (b) You’ve laced up, and (c) You’ve got your floor mat placed in front of the doorway. Double check that the floor mat does not skid.

(B) Face the doorway as if you were about to walk through it. Stand there, blade guards on the floor mat. Both skates firmly planted; legs just spread slightly like you’re normally standing; boots should not touch each other; weight should be on the flats of the blade guards, not tilted. Spread your arms to the side; elbows at shoulder height; hands raised up, palms forward; your left palm pressing lightly against one side of the door frame and your right palm pressing lightly against the other side. You should feel secure, but don’t press too hard: your palms should be able to slide up and down along the sides of the door frame.

(C) Do light up-down knee-and-ankle bends; knees straight over toes. Maintain proper posture; keep your back and shoulders straight. Keep your palms pressing lightly against the door frame and let them slide up and down as you do your up-down knee-and-ankle bends. Repeat 20 up-down knee-and-ankle bends. Not too quickly, about 1 up-down knee-and-ankle bend per 2 or 3 sec or so.

(D) Then sit down and check your laces. If there are any loose spots, snug them up.

(E) Repeat Steps (C) and (D) four more times (total of 100 light up-down knee-and-ankle bends).

(6) Second Sequence of Exercises

Now repeat Step 5 with medium up-down knee-and-ankle bends (total of 100 medium up-down knee-and-ankle bends). Remember to check laces after every 20 bends, and snug up if needed.

(7) Third Sequence of Exercises

Now repeat Step 5 with deep up-down knee-and-ankle bends (total of 100 up-down deep knee-and-ankle bends). Remember to check laces after every 20 bends, and snug up if needed.

(8) Fourth Sequence of Exercises

Now re-lace the boots, this time using all the hooks. Repeat the entire sequence of exercises in Step (5) through Step (7): 5 series of 20 light up-down knee-and-ankle bends; followed by 5 series of 20 medium up-down knee-and-ankle bends; followed by 5 series of 20 deep up-down knee-and-ankle bends. Remember to check laces after every 20 bends, and snug up if needed.

(9) Fifth Sequence of Exercises

The boots are now laced up all the way. Back to the doorway as before. Do a medium knee-and-ankle bend. Hold that position. Press your palms firmly against the door frame. Now flex your boots to the left and then to the right, slalom style. You will now be tilting the blade guards left and right. Repeat 100 left-right flexes.

(10) Sixth Sequence of Exercises

Repeat Step (9), except initially do a deep knee-and-ankle bend, then 100 left-right flexes. Remember to press your palms firmly against the door frame.

(11) On Ice ... Finally!

You are now ready to hit the ice. Proceed slowly. In between ice sessions, it’s still helpful to do up-down knee-and-ankle bends and left-right flexes off ice until you’re comfortable with the boots.
