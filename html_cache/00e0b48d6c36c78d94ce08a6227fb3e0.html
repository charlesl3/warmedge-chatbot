Just a couple of corrections:

"Team Great Britain And Northern Ireland", please.

We may be small and far outnumbered by our counterparts from the island next door in all the sports at the Olympics, but we are still included in the team's name (and, for that matter, the country name - the "United Kingdom Of Great Britain And Northern Ireland")

Sorry for being pedantic on that. It just winds me up the way the BBC keep "forgetting" that Team GB also includes us, and only mention our athletes if they win a medal for Team GB (and, conversely, ignore them if they win a medal for any other country).

Secondly, although Northern Ireland skaters are automatically eligible to represent either Team GB or Team Ireland, that doesn't mean everybody from other parts of the UK (or even the world) is not eligible to represent Ireland. All you need is an Irish passport, and you can get one of those if you have just 1 parent or grandparent that was either born on the island of Ireland (so from North or South of the border) or had Irish citizenship.

That is why there has been an explosion of applications to the Irish passport office from people born in Great Britain ever since the Brexit referendum.

Since we are talking Scotland, a large proportion of people from Glasgow would be eligible for an Irish passport, for example. Or if we were talking England, the same would be the case for a large proportion of people from Liverpool.

You know, I have always thought it strange the way some sports are devolved and others aren't. And my view has always been that they should be devolved, as by definition it gives more athletes the opportunity to compete for their country.

Oh, I would LOVE to see the Winter version of the Commonwealth Games come back. But, as I said the last time it was discussed, it would quickly become the Canada show.

Let's face it, Canada would win nearly everything that was going. Because it is the only Commonwealth country where winter sports are part of the culture. Scotland and New Zealand might get a look in here and there, but Canada would dominate.

That said, figure skating is one sport where we do see quite a few Commonwealth countries competing in. And we could realistically see medals for the Home Nations.

It's just a pity that Hong Kong and Southern Ireland are not in the Commonwealth any more. Because those are two countries I would love to see in a Commonwealth Games figure skating competition.

The problem is where to hold them. I know that the original run was held in Switzerland. But, if the CGF insisted on holding them in a Commonwealth country if they came back, then the only real options for host cities would be in Canada. Although, New Zealand could also be a possibility if some new ski jumping hills were built (there used to be 2 hills, but they are long gone).

And therein lies the problem. Ski jumping hills are expensive to build, and expensive to maintain afterwards. For goodness sake, the hills from the Calgary Olympics are due to be demolished this week. So, the only realistic option is to hold the Games in a city that already has all the facilities in place. And Canada is the only Commonwealth country that fits the bill. Although New Zealand has hosted winter multi-sports events, they haven't included the full range of sports.

Maybe it will happen in the future. But, given the problems the CGF have getting bidders for the Summer Games (which don't require as many expensive facilities to be specifically built), I doubt it.
