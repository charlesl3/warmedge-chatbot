What type of skates are you using? Rental skates? Your own? If you have your own skates were the blades pre-mounted? Sometimes you will have trouble holding edges because the blade is not mounted correctly for your feet. When you start trying to hold edges on a circle, blade alignment becomes very important and it could be a valid excuse as to why you are having more trouble holding edges on one foot over the other.

I know if I had blades mounted directly in the "center" of my skate in the traditional position it would be completely wrong for my feet. Just something I thought I'd mention. May or may not be your problem.

Regarding strength training for your legs, there are many options you have. Too many to fully list in this post to be honest. Do you have access to weights or will you be mainly looking to train at home with body weight exercises? For general leg and quad strength you cannot go wrong with squats. One leg, two legs, with weight or without, squats are the simplest and best lower body exercises for you if you want to build general leg strength.

Try following along with the exercise in this video. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I8bo2InRYI4
They can be done at home with just a chair, and it has the added benefit of helping toward sit spins.

Your ankles could also be a weak point since you are just starting out. I find skating really taxes my ankle muscles when I'm trying to hold those deep edges. Those are harder to train off ice, although it can be done. Probably the best way would be with resistance bands.

Its really hard to make a short post regarding strength training and fitness. So many options and different things that you could do that would work.

Rental anything can be dodgy at best. If you find that the boots are tied up snug but your ankles are flopping inwards then the skates are shot, maybe ask for a different pair.

Like anything else from drawing a horse to shooting a basketball, everyone has a dominant side. It's not so much that your left side is flimsy and weak, it's that you just don't have enough experience and confidence to ride your edges on that side. If you can stand on the floor on your left leg, then it's strong enough to glide on!

Your ability to glide longer, straight or curved will come with practice.

Tips:
• Skate faster, not out of control, but faster. Like riding a bike, it's easier to stay upright with some speed than going very slowly.
• Get your feet together. If your feet are apart, you'll tip over or curve towards the side that you're lifting.
• With your feet together, do a two foot glide before lifting into a one footer. The sensation of gliding won't be so abrupt compared to just stepping into it.
• Keep your arms out. This will help widen your center of balance and you can make small adjustments to counter balance if you tip to one side.
• Be soft in your skating knee. A slight knee bend will make things easier than a peg-leg!
• Measure your glides. Use the lines, advertising on the boards, or just count in seconds. Start with a solid 1 second glide then work your way up to higher counts.

Again, I wouldn't be concerned about how strong your muscles are until you want to start increasing your speed and height. If you can walk, you can skate.

Bit of a duplicate post, but having correctly fitted skates with good support for the beginner level is really make or break for many new learners in basic skating.

Here's an image that shows what good vs poor support looks like when you stand on your blades...https://www.wtsc.ca/sites/files/skate_fitting.png

If you are rolling inward or outward, don't just assume that it's a matter of strengthening your ankles or legs. Your stronger side may be better able to compensate, but if the skates don't support you appropriately, it will be an issue at every level. And you are at risk of injury if you don't have good support.

It may be that you need an insert support for a high instep, for example. Or it could be that the balance line of the skates is off. The blades might need remounting.
