As a career you are probably too late. Those on the path to making skating a career at your age are already doing double and triple jumps and are spending hours everyday at the rink + off-ice training. 12 is the latest I've heard career skaters start and even then that is extremely rare (I think Johnny Weir started at 12, but he managed to get his axel on the first week of lessons). Even those that show a lot of talent and started at 4 never make it because of time, travel and financial constraints - it costs a lot and only the top of the top skaters could be considered to be running a surplus in the money department. There is absolutely nothing wrong with learning how to skate for fun though and there are a lot of competitions for all ages and abilities! You will probably never be an international skater but there is nothing to stop you getting out there and enjoying it.

Are you too old to be a nationally competitive skater. The answer is unfortunately probably yes. The amount of time, money, and natural skill involved precludes even most people who start as young children from that level.

That said, skating can still be a big part of your life from learning new skills and taking tests, competing (while senior nationals is probably not happening you could definitely go to adult sectionals or nationals someday, there are even international adult competitions in Vancouver and Germany- and in The meantime there are tons of non qualifying competitions you could do with all sorts of events from technical to artistic and dramatic programs. And maybe someday you could teach learn to skate classes and even become a judge.

The majority of skaters are never going to be the ones you see on tv and that's okay. The important thing is to love skating and work towards your personal goals. Good luck!!!!

One further thought,

It's not my job to tell people what they should and shouldn't dream about. Dreams are beautiful, but it is important to think about why we want something. Chasing fame or glory in skating will never make you happy- and there isn't much of that to go around anyways. But chasing something because of how it makes you feel, whether it's learning something new or mastering a skill, or skating a program to the best of your ability, that will never let you down.

Skating can be all of these things. It can make you happy and it can make you miserable. I skated as a child and quit after I turned 18 and the last year I was skating I hated it. I was too caught up in the goals I would never reach and how negatively I compared myself to everyone else. As an adult I no longer care what other people think, my skating is for myself. And it is a lot of fun reaching goals you didn't think you could reach.

If skating doesn't make you happy on a 5 am freestyle, or when you don't have money to go on vacation because you had to buy new skates, or when you get hurt, or when you struggle on something for a year without progress then it's not going to make you happy at a big competition. You have to love it or else you resent it.

Keep in mind also that skating competitively is not a "career" in the sense of a way to earn a living. For all but a very few stars, it costs more to train to elite level than the skaters can ever earn back through skating-related income. That includes most world-level skaters.

Most Olympic sports are expensive hobbies even for the most accomplished, and with few exceptions that's as true of figure skating as it is of, say, luge.

If you mean career in the sense of a sports career as an ongoing hobby developing skills and entering competitions over the course of many years, then sure. Take lessons, practice a lot, pass tests if you live in a country with a testing system, and enter competitions appropriate to your age and skill level as soon as you feel ready. If you stick with it for years, your competitive level will increase.

As others have said, if you want to earn a living in a career related to figure skating, then learn to skate at the highest skill level you can get to, test if you can, compete if you want to, and then look into teaching skating, choreographing, performing in shows (e.g., Disney on Ice or theme parks etc. -- you're not likely to make it to shows that showcase elite medalists), etc.

But first, start learning to skate and see how you like it.
