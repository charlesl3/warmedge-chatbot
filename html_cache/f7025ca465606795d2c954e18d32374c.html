Realistically, you're working at a PreJuvenile level on jumps and a below pre-pre level on spins. It's not unimpressive for someone who has been skating for 2 years, but it's not realistic to shoot for the Olympics. Even assuming you do pretty much nothing else for a couple years, you'd struggle to find the time to take and pass the 16 tests necessary to skate at that level. There is a lot to skating outside of the Olympics -- even the thousands of 8-10 year old girls skating at and above your level who have been skating for 5+ years and have time to get to the Olympics know they are probably not going there, ya know?

None of us have seen you skate (that we know of) so it's hard to say. Just going off what you're saying, when it comes the Olympics, if you are in the USA, your chance is pretty much none, sorry. But it sounds like you're tremendously talented and you could totally work towards a near-term goal of competing at Novice Mens Regionals, with the goal of making it through to Sectionals and Nationals. To do this, you will probably have increase your training time, and work pretty hard to pass moves up to novice in a short period of time - I've seen other really fast learners do it in 1.5-2 years - it could be even less than that if you've been working on the specific patterns for each test a lot already. The freestyle tests won't be as difficult, but if you've never been a performer before, expect to work on your performance skills a LOT.

I honestly don't know whether it's a pro or a con that you've never taken private lessons before - It's possible when you start, you'll realize that there'll be a lot of bad habits to fix from all that time learning on your own, or alternately, the fact that you've done so much even without them means you have even more untapped potential. You won't know until you start.

I think if you're able to take more lessons, and step up your training time, reaching Novice sectionals is totally an attainable goal. Being a man, and the fact that there are often under 4 novice men at each regionals, many of them only doing doubles, is what makes it realistic. For anyone else reading this after the fact, it would NOT be realistic for a girl. For ladies at novice levels its much more competitive.

Whether it's realistic to ever make nationals as a senior, would be how quickly you pick up your jumps, and how natural and musical you are as a performer. Those are kind of impossible to know at this point. But the competition for men REALLY picks up at juniors and seniors.

If you're planning to go to compete, can I recommend that you sign up to take your first couple moves tests as soon as you can, like next month soon. I don't care how good you are as a skater, if you've never tested before it will take you a while to pass intermediate and novice moves tests, and there's not always a plentiful amount of high level test sessions. You want to get the easier ones out of the way asap. I've seen boys who start mid-late teens who can do doubles and triples really quickly, and it's always the moves that hold them up timewise. Make sure you know the deadlines for regionals and when you have to pass the tests by.

You say "I have basic skating skills and edge requirements to novice or possibly intermediate tests."

Does this mean you have worked on your own on all the moves required for the Intermediate and Novice Moves in the Field tests?

Have you observed coached skaters at your rink working on the moves for these tests and have a sense that you are skating the same moves at a similar skill level?

If so, that's terrific! Now start taking lessons to polish them up to the level needed for testing. Pay even more attention to the Pre-Preliminary through Juvenile test moves at first, because you'll need to pass those tests before you can move on to higher levels.

If you have not tried any of the patterns at all yet, you may be overestimating your current skill level and underestimating the amount of work it requires to pass the tests, let alone to be competitive. Once you start taking lessons, you may get a big reality check as to what your current level really is and how long it will take to improve to a level that would allow you to compete in qualifying competitions (or internationally for Philippines).

For example, overrotating double salchows and flips is not a sign that you should start working on triples. First you need to be able to control the landings of the doubles.

The fact that you are already able to rotate doubles and appear comfortable with jumping is a plus. So are your dance background and passion for performing.

Now start working with a good coach on controlling your blades on the ice, in edgework/stroking/Moves in the Field and in spins and in jump takeoffs and landings. The sooner you start refining your technique with good instruction, the more you will be able to achieve.

In order to pass a Freeskate test (those you see competing on TV/World Championships/Olympics have passed the highest or senior test), you first have to have passed the corresponding Moves in the Field test. These MITF patterns teach proper turns, stroking, flow, power, and edge quality. If you go to the USFS rulebook (http://www.usfsa.org/story?id=84114, hopefully the link works) the patterns for the first test, Pre-Preliminary, start on page 321.

I imagine that you would be able to pass the first few tests without too much of an issue, but the higher ones, even for experienced skaters, take practice to perfect and get them to a passing standard for judges. There are youtube videos of test-takers, but you need a coach for USFS testing (someone correct me if I'm wrong).

For singles, the levels are as follows:

Pre-Preliminary
Preliminary
Pre-Juvenile
Juvenile
Intermediate
Novice
Junior
Senior

So, for example, to pass the Intermediate Freeskate test, you need to have passed all the MITF tests through the Intermediate Level (though you can pass higher before testing Freeskate). A lot of skaters will be several levels higher in MITF than Freeskate.

I'm sure others can also give much better information than I can!

My skating club does not "allow" contingency testing and I believe that most others have a similar rule. Where such a rules exists, you would not be able to do back-to-back tests of the same type. However, you would be able to take two different tests (pre-pre freeskate and a preliminary "moves") on the same day. My daughter has done that.

Somewhere I remember reading that if you fail a test, the minimum wait time before you can re-take a test is 30 days. We never pushed that and I would have read that years ago. Not sure if a similar rule is still in place.
