Hey everyone,

So, here's my story: I skated competitively from age 9 until 18. I was competing at the Junior level with all of my doubles (working on a double axel and triples). The last time I skated hardcore was in 2006, so it's been about 12 years. I've skated for fun off and on since then, the last time being about three years ago.

Since then I've put on a lot of weight, and I picked up skating again as a way to exercise and also because I really miss it.

Bottom line: I feel like I am at square one. Everything is just so different... my balance, everything. It's rough because in my head, I know how to do everything but my body just isn't there. I'm not expecting to be brilliant overnight but I also didn't expect to find back crossovers difficult either.

I'm gonna keep working at it but in the meantime, I was just curious if any of you adult skaters are/were in a similar boat and what you've been able to realistically accomplish! Not sure what my goals or expectations should be at this point.

Thanks in advance for the support/advice.

First off- welcome back to skating!

I think that getting your doubles back is a realistic and attainable goal. I am a 30 year old adult skater who also skated as a child, although not nearly as high level as you (and I was in an in an ISI club which is more recreational). Speaking for myself, I have been able to learn things I couldn't do as a younger skater. I didn't skate for about 7 years in between quitting and restarting, with the exception of going skating with friends for fun where you can impress by doing a waltz jump. The only place I feel limited is working on harder jumps- I never did doubles as a kid and my body doesn't have the natural muscle memory. (I feel like it took me 85 years to consistently rotate the axel, it will probably take another century to make it pretty).

One of my skating friends is probably a better example for you. She skated up to Novice level as a kid and has been back to skating seriously about a year(ish). She landed her double loop this week- which she had not landed in years. It just took a lot of repetition and practice. Both of us have been able to learn a lot of IJS spin variations we didn't do as kids (but she is doing level 3 and 4's while I work on level 2's!). I would give yourself a grace period- I think you just need time to get your body used to skating again.

The great thing about adult skating is you see all levels- from those who never skated as kids, to those like me who skated recreationally as kids, to those like you who skated competitively as kids. I went to adult sectionals for the first time this year and the level of skating I saw across all levels was really impressive. If you did want to compete in the future, your level would be Masters Junior-Senior (they group Intermediate-Novice together and Junior-Senior together). At those levels of competitions you generally see doubles.

Good luck!

Welcome back to the rink! For most adult skaters who take a break from their younger years, their body certainly isn't what it used to be (myself included)! I know my core and leg strength and physique aren't the same as when I was a teen, and getting back on the ice was a struggle at first. I wanted to be able to doing 2A/triples again right off the bat, but even mind over matter couldn't help that. However, in the process of returning to the ice, I gained an appreciation and a yearning to develop aspects of my skating that I didn't really focused on as a teenager (I only liked to jump and wasn't competing under IJS) - like transitions, and more expressive skating. e.g. if I could only do a single axel instead of the double, I would try different transitions leading into it. I would experiment with spin positions I hadn't tried as a kid (albeit not the more flexible ones... never gonna get my Biellmann back! LOL). Since coming back to skating, I now try more unique movements, I try to mimic movements or footwork I've seen skaters do on Youtube which wasn't available back then. Of course, I've also tried to develop better technique in my jumping too - paying attention to make sure my edges on takeoffs are correct (in case the more nitpicky Golden Skate folks show up at my rink! ) and jumps are fully rotated.

After 5-6 years back, I finally got my double axel and triple toe back about a year and a half ago... but depending on the session even a single axel I have trouble with (usually after Xmas time!) - which is pretty much adulthood/metabolism coming into play... but it's best not to worry about things like that. There's something kinda liberating about not having to compete anymore so I can just focus on my skating as a whole, and not try to match or compete against what I was, but rather see if I can become things that I wasn't.

There's a lot of maturity and novelty you can bring to your skating as an adult, and I think that there is a lot of joy that can be derived from starting from the ground up, in that it's a chance to become a different type of skater than you were in the past. Take heart in the fact that you've had the skills previously so you know what it takes to do certain movements and the muscle memory takes a while to re-engage. Also one more thing - in terms of programs and whatnot, when you're younger, usually a coach and choreographer set stuff out for you, but when you're an adult it's more carte blanche and that can be very interesting and exciting.

Good luck to ya!
