I've been using a Lululemon bag that I got on sale for <$50. I used to have a $25 skate bag from Amazon but hated the way the shoulder strap was designed in a way that caused me shoulder pain. It did have separate compartments for each skate which I miss in the Lululemon (fingers crossed for the day that Lululemon designs a skate bag lmao). It's the Hot Mesh bag (http://www.eu.lululemon.com/p/women-bags/Hot-Mesh-Tote/_/prod11540243) -- I chose it because it was conveniently on sale lol, but also huge and because half of it is made from mesh, it would help air out my skates after skating. Since it's so large and un-structured, it easily becomes a black hole bag. I'm used to navigating blackhole bags though, since my most-used bag is my Longchamp Le Pliage. I find just having my skates on the bottom and having everything else in pouches and smaller bags within help with keeping all my stuff organized in the bag.

I mainly chose to not use a Transpack because I usually skate before or after work, and since I work downtown, I needed a work-friendly bag that looked semi-professional and not take up too much room on my subway commute. Just wanted to add my two cents for those who also are considering a more aesthetically pleasing skate bag.

I used a wheelie backpack for years. It was perfect for skates plus all the other stuff I bring skating. It was similar to this, but a different print:
http://www.zappos.com/p/jansport-wheeled-superbreak/product/7372933

Eventually, the wheels stopped working well, and I was going to buy another, but I had bought a used Zuca on Ebay for cheap, intending to sell it, and when my wheelie backpack stopped working, I decided to use the Zuca instead. To be honest, it's not as good as a good wheelie backpack - it doesn't hold as much.

A lot of skaters at my rinks use cool hard sided wheeled suitcases, which you can get pretty cheap if you have a Marshall's or TJ Maxx or similar near you. You can sometimes also find the Jansport wheelie backpacks there - that's where I'd gotten mine.

For me, priorities in a skating bag are that it have wheels, so I don't have to carry it, and that it have some pockets, so I don't have to have everything in one big compartment (another thing I don't like about the Zuca - the pockets are either tiny or exposed (like the exterior mesh pockets where everyone can see all your stuff and then you tear the stupid mesh in week 1 and your stuff falls out.) The Jansport had an exterior pocket that was much bigger, and zipped closed.)

When I travel and bring my skates, I normally bring them in a normal backpack. So really, you can use whatever you'd like and feel suits you re: usefulness, transportability, budget, etc.

It should stink up anything that's in the bag with the skates, and the bag itself. Are you interested in deodorizing your skates? If so, you may have success if you take the skates, and put them in a sealable plastic container (like one of these tub things people use for storage https://www.amazon.com/IRIS-Quart-S...498766990&sr=8-3&keywords=plastic+storage+bin) with a good amount of charcoal (for example, enough to fill a cereal bowl) or baking soda (a box like you'd use in the fridge.) To me the biggest thing about bags are 1) Ease of moving it 2)It holds what you need, and keeps it protected 3)Ease of airing out your skates!

My husband uses ahttps://www.amazon.com/5-11-Tactical-Patrol-Ready-Black/dp/B000AL9CUI/ref=sr_1_1?ie=UTF8&qid=1498883948&sr=8-1&keywords=5.11+bag

This is a shoulder bag/hand carry with tons of pockets, lightweight and very easy to unzip and flip open when we get home to air out his skates, drag out dirty socks and cleaning towels etc. Plenty of room to hold gaurds, skates and basic equipment as well as some additional clothes.

I have a Zuca. They are plenty easy to find cheaper if you check ebay sellers, but a decent frame with insert will cost at least a hundred bucks. I like that I do not have to carry it, can wash the insert, can sit on it, can easily air out all of my protective gear in the netting areas and also easily air out my skates by just unzipping the door. I also like that I can carry my little rinkside bag on top with my water, coffee, journal, kleenex etc. I use every pocket in the zuca, it is perfect if you really like to organize. At the end of skating I put everything in a pile and then put everything quickly in it's place.

The transpak is too small for me and I do not like to heft bags around on my shoulders anyway. A suitcase is not as easy as a Zuca to air skates out and not nearly as organized. Most of the suitcases I see at the rink are messy and a bit smelly, it looks like people just don't open them up on a regular basis. Plus they take up more room to open on the floor

I've repurposed an old diaper bag. Any bag that can hold your skates and other stuff you need at the rink will do the job. For me key factors are: big enough but not too big or heavy, holds shape, water-resistant. Skates are massive and weird shape, not every backpack will hold them. Generic soft backpacks with skates in them don't sit comfortably on my back. Having said this, all you really need for skating is skates, sooner or later it's worth investing in a good new pair, a little (but not a lot!) stiffer than your level. If you are an adult who doesn't skate a whole lot, and your feet are unlikely to change size/shape, boots and blades last for years. Everything else is optional and is only needed for your comfort, pleasure and safety, so it doesn't need to be expensive.

I used a bag with a very fine mesh - much smaller than skate hooks. But I haven't recently been able to find that style bag. They use to make that sort for carrying groceries. They could easily carry about 70 pounds without breaking.

Of course, any bag that is open at the top probably has pretty good ventilation.

But that might not be ideal for carrying your boots in the rain. I may not have a good answer for you. You maybe need the mesh on the bottom rather than the top. Or - would an umbrella keep the rain out. (Might not work in high winds.)

A lot of stores that carry watersports equipment (e.g., swim suits, canoes and kayaks) have breathable bags and backpacks. But they aren't always cheap.

If you aren't carrying your skates far in the bag, e.g., just to your car, and from your car to your home, when it is raining, you don't need a breathable bag. Just take them out once you get them inside the car and home.

My area - near Washington, DC, getsrain too. (It rains nearly every summer afternoon.) But it is such a short distance from the rink to my car, and from my car to my home, that I don't use a bag. After all, the boots are exposed to moisture from the ice and sweat. I do sometimes wait for the rain to slow down a bit.

I get why some people, who have fancy skating outfits, need bags - for the outfits, not for the skates. And I've seen hockey skaters use bags that could easily fit the skater inside, because they carry a lot of protective equipment.
