Ah, let's break it down then. For the most part, this is what you're looking for in ice dance:

- How close the couple is together especially in hold, not separated often
- How effortless are they going from hold to hold, and are there difficult connections and holds throughout
- How deep are the edges, the curves on the ice, and how fast are they going across the ice in general
- How well-timed are the skaters to the chosen music, emphasizing the beat if there is one or the lyrical phrasing

The twizzles and the pattern dances are a bit more particular. Level 4 twizzles basically need four very clean rotations on each foot to count with various arm/leg positions, and should be done with a lot of speed, ice coverage, and precision. The pattern dances have three key points where the edges and the edge changes need to be SUPER clear for the tech panel for them to count for a higher level.

Hahaha. Ice Dance scoring certainly is complex, as everybody (fans, casual viewers, even judges) mixes up personal preference for a programme with the points it deserves. I've been a fan of ice dancing for many years (these dances are just so wonderful to watch) but I never get into the maths. I can see whether a dance is difficult with intricate foot work, speed, well executed twizzles, special lifts, the sense of togetherness, and the synchronicity (it's like skating with three legs as some say), and whether it's executed looking easy but in the end it's the scores that count - however incomprehensible they may be to some. It's probably there's more "was robbed" here than in any other discipline. Just enjoy, and don't get too frustrated when the dance you favour (for whatever reason) doens't get the marks you wanted it to get. I won't (I hope).

Belbin has chosen to focus on the Choctaw as a way to give an example of a technical aspect of the scoring. You really have to know Choctaw , three turn , rockers, counters, brackets etc etc because so much of ice dance is steps! Step sequences pattern dances all have steps and even if you are seeing all the steps can you judge both skaters doing them so quickly? That’s why when I was wondering why what I thought was great was level one because one skater did not hit any edge correctly! I was very disappointed to learn that the skater just did really look alike moves! Then you had other couples where one was great at one kind of step and the other was weak on another but their step sequences still included both and they depend on friendly tech panels to call them being equally done by both but they really aren’t!
