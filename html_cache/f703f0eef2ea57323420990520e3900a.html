Skate guards have lots of channels and crevices to trap water. To speed up drying, you want to tap the guards to dislodge as much of the trapped water as possible. With the one-piece guards, it's easy, you just clap the bottoms together many times. With two-piece guards, you have to be a little bit more careful to avoid stretching the springs. Hold the guards near the center, on both sides of the springs. Hold one guard upside down (open channel down, bottom up), and tap along the bottom repeatedly (around six times or so) with the bottom of the other guard. Repeat with the other guard held upside down. Then let air dry as Ic3 wrote.

Realistically, it doesn't matter if there is water inside. You obviously don't leave them on when your skates are in your bag. The only time they are on is when the blades are going to be wet either way. For that initial 5 seconds that it takes to get on the ice in the beginning of the session, they are dry. But during the session, they will be wet. It doesn't *really* matter if they are wet for the initial 5 second walk to the ice. The most important thing is that they don't have debris and dirt inside that could scratch or damage the blade. Rinsing them out before using them is more beneficial than drying them.

I wonder if blade type has an affect on propensity to get excess water in the rockers. I was taking off my Rockerz and noted that because they are Matrix blades, the Rockerz fit very tightly, so water does not drip past the top of the Rockerz.
