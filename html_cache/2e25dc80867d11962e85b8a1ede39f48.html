Yes, it is possible to figure skate as a boy lol. I skated from age 5-13 as a boy and there were a few other boys my age at the rink I went too aswell.

Men skating is of course not as common as women, but you will still find a few men skating at a typical rink. Really depends on what rink you go to. The rink I go to now is a smaller one and there are only 3-4 guys that skate. My old rink when I was little had 10+ men/boys skating at different times.

To go to the Olympics in the sport of figure skating you need the perfect mix of natural talent, work ethic, time, desire, and proper coaching. Unfortunately starting at 15 puts you at a significant disadvantage. The skaters you see on ESPN have spent their entire lives since they were ~5 years old dedicated to skating. Their entire life has revolved around the sport. You could become a very accomplished skater by starting at 15, but I think the Olympics are probably out of your grasp. Sorry. The only well known male skater I know who started later was Johnny Weir. He started when he was 12 years old.

Is figure skating a "gay" sport? All I'll say is that men skate with many different styles. Some more feminine and some more masculine. Your style is entirely up to you.

"Is it possible to figure skate as a boy" Yes.

"Is it common do a lot of boys/men figure skate" I don't have any statistics (haven't searched for any either). From personal observation over the decades at various rinks, however, I would say that females (girls and women) far outnumber males (boys and men). I skate during public sessions, and I'm either the only male figure skater or one of a few male figure skaters. I also frequently observe freestyle sessions (before or after my session). Very few males (often none; but none of the rinks I've gone to are elite training centers). Also, at pro shops that do carry inventory, the selection of white skates for females far outnumber the selection of black skates for males. Skate techs tell me the vast majority of their customers are female. Also at college I took freestyle and ice dance classes; there were far more females than males. Again, these are limited personal observations, and I'm talking about the more general population of figure skaters and not the elite competitive population of figure skaters (as another poster commented, in the competitions shown on TV, half the skaters are male).

"I am almost 15 am I to late to figure skate and become good/pro" Addressed in other concurrent threads. Depends a lot on your criteria for "good/pro", your athletic ability, how hard you're willing to work, how many hours you can devote, access to good ice and good coaches, and how much money you or your parents have.

"Last but not least (this may hurt but I don't mean it to) is it a gay sport" I don't know whether there are any statistics comparing the percentage of gays among male figure skaters with the percentage of gays among the general male population (haven't searched for these statistics either). I don't know the rationale behind this question. I'll take a stab and offer these comments. My initial reaction was similar to TontoK's. You are at a vulnerable age in which you are dealing with difficult issues of self-esteem, peer acceptance, and sexuality. There has been much social progress over the decades in eliminating "male" and "female" labels from specific activities, but biases and prejudices remain (depends heavily on your local community and individuals; and I don't know what country you're from). Generally I would say that most people do not consider figure skating as a macho sport, and certainly there are people who think that real men play hockey, not figure skate. At work, I always got funny looks when I said, "I ice skate", and co-workers asked, "Oh, you play hockey?", and I answered, "No, I figure skate." There are few rink facilities dedicated to figure skating. At most rink facilities, the figure skating crowd will intermingle with the hockey crowd to some extent. And, yes, as a male figure skater, I've had to deal with some crude and cruel comments from macho hockey players, and their equally macho coaches and parents (including moms). Fortunately, they've been rare. But don't be surprised if you do hear them. Just dismiss them, and continue with the joy of figure skating [I know, not easy at your age].

Within the US, I would estimate that approximately 15% of the figure skaters I see at local rinks or who compete at regionals and large club competitions or adult competitions are male. The distribution would vary by location. At a training center especially one that specializes in pairs or dance, the numbers of boys/men will be higher. At a small rink in an isolated location, there may be very few figure skaters in total, and all of them young girls.

At competitions that skaters have to qualify for (e.g., US sectionals and Nationals, or the championship events at Adult Nationals), the numbers of males and females will be more similar because only a limited number of skaters in each discipline can make the cut -- a lot higher percentage of girls and women get left home.

I don't have a sense of what the numbers are like in other countries. It would be interesting to know.

Hi Soelie...

There are lots of male/boy skaters out there....but I suspect that not every skating club is a great one to be a boy at...

If you are lucky enough to live in an area with several clubs, try to get down to some of the sessions of mid level skaters to see if there are a good proportion of boys/men or not. Our kids skate at a club that's known to have a lot of boys, and a good proportion of the coaches are male too. From what I see, the boys train a lot together and don't seem to feel exceptional.

However, when we go to competitions in our region we see that most of the boys competing are from just a couple of the clubs, including ours. So, it seems that boys feel more comfortable and are more successful on average in clubs that have a more balanced gender ratio...

One thing that has been noted is that being in a skating club is a great place to have make good solid friendships with both genders, whatever your orientation. It's one of the very few sports where boys and girls, and men and women team up together to compete...

And, I should note that if you are interested in a partnered discipline, as a male you will find it easier to find a partner just because males are less common. [Remember 3 out of 4 main disciplines require a male skater.]

You may not be become a skater on TV, but there is lots of opportunity to grow in skating at your age. I certainly know of folks who make a living as coaches who started much later than you.

Lastly, in the words of one blogging parent [Skatersdad.com], if he'd only knew that being a teenaged boy would mean being in a club with lot of beautiful and fit teenaged girls, he wouldn't have waited until his daughters were skaters to learn figure skating himself. Suggest you take a look at some of his Qs&As, and share the site with your parents if they have doubts about whether skating is for you....
