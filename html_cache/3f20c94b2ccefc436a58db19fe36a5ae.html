I am new to skating, approaching a year and a half soon, and I am very anal retentive about caring for my skates, blades, etc. One of the more interesting things that I found, maintenance-wise, was the benefit of changing laces. For me, they seem to need a change about every six months (I skate 4-6 hours a week). I had noticed that the laces that came with my skates were giving me trouble at about the sixth month mark, I was having to re-tighten my laces at least twice during a practice session, had never had to do that before. Switching to new laces solved the problem. At about six months after that, I decided to replace again, even though I was not noticing very significant issues, and again, I noticed a difference with the new laces, my boots just stayed snugger, longer. Laces are kind of a frog in warm water thing, you don't notice that they are wearing out right away... the changes are so gradual. Anyway, laces are not expensive usually (I like the ones with the center crystals) they feel better, and are so easy to replace. Not to mention the look of fresh clean laces

Yes, that's normal, eventually you'll have callouses on your hands from lacing your boots. If the laces hurt your hands it doesn't necessarily mean they're low quality - they're low quality if you feel like you can't tighten them properly, or if they slip on the hooks, requiring re-tying. If it's a problem with regards to strength/pulling on them hurting your hands, you can get a lace hook that can help pull your laces tight without just using your hands. You can find them for 5$ at any hockey or skating store.

It's normal to develop some calluses on your fingertips if you skate regularly. The laces will bite into your fingers more right now because you are breaking in a new pair of boots. The boot eventually will mold to your foot, and grooves will mold into the tongue and around the eyelets and hooks to conform to the laces. Until they do, extra tension is required.

The degree of bite into your fingers also depends on (1) the thickness of the lace (the thinner the lace, the more bite; check the width of your lace, they should be ~1/4" (~6-7 mm) wide; I've seen thinner laces) and (2) material (of the common materials, all-nylon cuts the worse). Be careful at this stage; until you develop calluses, it's easy to draw blood.

yesienna said:
Do you think hockey laces would work for figure skates? I've seen some colorful hockey laces recently in the hockey store, long enough for my skates. Worth trying?
Click to expand...

Only in limited instances. Figure skate laces come in a range of widths and thicknesses; hockey skate laces come in a range of widths and thicknesses; and figure skate boots have eyelets and hooks in a range of sizes. There are "narrow" hockey laces that are about the same width and thickness as "wide" figure laces. Narrow hockey laces will work if the eyelets and hooks of your boots accommodate wide figure laces. Lace manufacturers generally list only the width, not the thickness, of the lace. Most hockey laces are spec'd in metric widths. A 7 mm wide hockey lace would be worth trying out. A lace with a width of 8 mm or more probably won't work.

Sounds good to me. I would add the following steps. After you’ve laced through the top set of eyelets, wrap one lace around the other 3-5 times (number of wraps depends on the lace) before lacing along the hooks. By wrap, I mean the same step you would do before tying a bow. You want few enough wraps to be able to still tug on the ends to take up any slack as you go back to snug up the laces along the eyelets. At the same time, you want enough wraps to hold the tension if you let go of the ends. The wraps allow differential tension. When you first break in boots, you will probably want some slack along the hooks. So the wraps allow the laces to be snug along the eyelets and looser along the hooks. Without the wraps, if the laces are loose anywhere, eventually they will work loose everywhere.
