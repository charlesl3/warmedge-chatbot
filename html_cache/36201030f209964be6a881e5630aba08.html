Ice time is less where I live but still, it adds up. Plus there's the wear and tear on boots and blades, which increases overall fees. (For instance, skating 2 hours a day 5 days a week means sharpening your blades about once a month vs skating 3-4 hours a day 5-6 days week could double the cost of sharpening).

And then also money for transportation to and from the rink. And the opportunity cost of the transportation time, too, if the sessions are divided and not done all at once.

FWIW, our coach (former elite skater) is of the opinion that its better to have shorter, fewer, and more focussed sessions than to have a lot of ice time that's not 100% productive. Coach says that especially for younger skaters, its a very "old fashioned" view that they have to skate every single day and for excessive amounts of time.

My skater is nowhere close to being elite, but we've reduced training time by 50 percent since leaving the old coaches and we've seen 200% performance gains. The coach jam packs the session, they work really hard, and then they're off the ice.

Of course, I'm sure our coach skated more than 4 hours a week (what we do now) during their elite years.

For instance, I feel like many skaters have very little endurance and I think that's from lack of doing serious cardio (which can be on or off ice - and since it can be done off, why pay for ice time for that cardiovascular training?).

My skater is 7 and started at the age of 3. For the first 2 years we just did seasonal Canskate (learn to akate) classes in a group format for 45 mins per week. The goal those first 2 years was just to learn how to skate, but we went from pre-canskate to beginning canskate 5. So not rapid progression but pretty good given it was fairly minimal ice time. In year 3 the interest picked up and so did the icetime, but we were still at maybe 3 hours per week during the schoolyear only. Got to STAR 2 that year. After that we were into a few weeks of skating camp and then 8 hours/week. There was literally NO progression during that time but part of that was due to the coaching relationship breaking down. We are making way more progress now even though my skater had to re-learn almost everything since January. Now we are working on doubles.

But my skater isnt hoping for Olympic glory and just wants to have the opportunity to be reasonably good at skating. If we had sights on the Olympics, we might be training more, I dunno.
