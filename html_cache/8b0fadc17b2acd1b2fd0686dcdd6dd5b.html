Hi all.

I recently purchased a pair of Edea Overtures and a Jackson Ultima Mirage blade.
My general shoe size is about a 10UK or 11US.

On proper measurement of my feet I found that they are about 287mm long.
When I asked the skate company which skate size to get they requested that I get either a 300 (10.5UK) or a 305 (11.5UK).
I went ahead and ordered the 305mm because according to the Edea size chart it seemed to be the correct choice.
I was worried that the 300 would be too small for me length and width wise.

I received my skates a couple weeks ago and have only skated in them about five times but on inspection of the insole it seems that there is a great deal of space between my big toe and the front of the boot.
Around 20-30mm which I know is way too big!

The reason I inspected the insoles length is because I felt when I was skating my feet would naturally fall inwards on the inside edge and cut an uneven cut in the ice, almost as if the weight distribution was off on the blade to ice.
I don't know if this is because the skates are too long but I feel as if the weight distribution is incorrect.
I feel as if I cant judge or trust my edges.

I'm not sure what I should do because I paid a lot of money for the skates including shipping and tax and so I'm in a bit of a predicament.
Could someone advise me as to what the issue could be or what I should do?
Are the skates too big? Is that why I'm having these issues?

Please assist!

Sounds like they are way too big, and therefore unsafe for you to use.

Most places will not take returns once the blades are mounted on the boots and the blades sharpened, so you are probably stuck with the skates. It is hard to sell used skating equipment, even just lightly used, so if you go that route, I wish you luck (selling boots and blades separately might make it less difficult).

This is why buying without a professional fitting is a really bad idea. Just yesterday, I heard a coach break the news to a mother that the daughter's new skates are too big and might make the girl get hurt.

Ah, that's where you messed up. You used the chart backwards from its intended purpose. The chart tells you the proper blade length to order for a specific boot size. That is, if you know what your proper boot size is, the chart gives you the length of the (outer) sole, and based on the length of the (outer) sole, what length blade you should use. Of course, it's more accurate to actually measure the length of the (outer) sole, once you have the boot in hand, and then order the blade. But if there is a long wait to order and get the boot and blade, this chart allows you to order the boot and blade at the same time ... assuming the boot size is correct in the first place.

You cannot simply use the length of the (outer) sole and the length of your foot (when measured on a flat surface) to determine the proper boot size. In most boots, the toe of the boot uppers overhangs the toe of the (outer) sole and the heel of the boot uppers overhangs the heel of the (outer) sole. The degree of overhang varies with make and model of boot. That means that the length of the uppers will be longer than the length of the (outer) sole. [Further complicating matters is that in many boots, the (outer) heel has a tapered profile.]

Also, your foot does not lie flat in a skating boot: a skating boot has a heel pitch (that is, the heel of the foot is raised with respect to the ball of the foot). The heel pitch depends on the heel pitch of the (outer) sole of the boot as well as the heel pitch of the (inner) sole of the boot. Both outer and inner heel pitches depend on make and model of boot. The higher the overall heel pitch, the shorter will be the horizontal projection of your foot. I don't wear Edeas, but I did talk to a skate tech about them. He told me that Edeas have a higher overall heel pitch than many other boots. The boot size also depends on the overall shape of the boot; e.g., a wide toe box may allow a shorter length than a narrow toe box.

A good fitter typically takes measurements and tracings of your feet on a horizontal surface. This info is sent to the manufacturer, who then knows the geometric correction factors to give the proper size. For example, when I was in a particular model of Riedells, I was a size 9N; in my current model of Jacksons, I am a size 8W [partly because the Jackson model has a wider toe box, narrower heel, and higher heel pitch than the Riedell model]. Because of all the other geometric parameters, you can't simply compare the horizontal length of your foot to the (flat) length of the (inner) sole, either.


ETA: If you simply don't have access to a good fitter, and must order by mail on your own, you might want to consider a foam-mold kit for your feet. These are typically used for custom boots, but the extra expense might be worth it in your case. Check with Edea if they offer this option. BTW, it's difficult to accurately measure your own feet by yourself; you should at least have someone else do it.
