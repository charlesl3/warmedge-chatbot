I've had the Overtures for about a year now and they're still going strong! They look brand new too, except for a smudge that snuck onto the left somehow. I skate about twice a week for 4 hours, usually. When I first got them I used a blow dryer and a golf ball to punch out the sides where they made my feet hurt. I also wore them around the house to help break them in. I don't remember how long it was until the breaking in period was over, but I don't think it was more than a few weeks.

I've heard that the brand has a higher heel than others, but these are my first skates so I wouldn't know. There is also room in the ankle for you to bend, which I didn't like when I was first starting out because it didn't feel secure. Now, I like having that space because I can get down into my knees. I have been toying with the idea of getting gel pads though, because there is still some side-to-side motion in the ankles. There is also a specific way for lacing an Edea boot that you can find on Youtube.

Overall I'm happy with my boots. They look nice and I'm comfortable in them. But again, these are my first skates so I don't know any different.

Skated Jackson forever, switched to Edea last year (Piano)

They are generously sized, meaning they feel roomy. I don't notice a heel height difference when skating. They fit me better out of the box than any of my Jackson boots ever did, but it's because they're so thickly padded. My heels slip around a bit if I don't lace right, meaning the Edea way, over-under the hooks. One of my ankles has a recurring injury and they are very supportive but not restrictive - lots more flex to get used to, but it's actually healthier for your ankles. It lets them get stronger rather than just taking all the slack up for them. My custom insoles for my super-high arches were a must, but I've always had to use those so it was nothing disappointing.

After a year in them, they are starting to feel a little worn (though they look awesome) and I think when I replace them I will go one (5mm) size down from what my fitter put me in this time, because I'd like them to be more snug.

Oh, the biggest difference I noticed when switching - they are crazy light. That took some getting used to. I had to relearn crossovers to get down into the ice the way I like again, really have to put some weight into it. Everything else is effortless because of how light they are.

mj elric4 said:
How do they feel - length and width wise (I've noticed that they measure in mm)? Looseness? Problems? Heel height and feel? Overbooting or underbooting issues?
Click to expand...

I moved to Edeas after having heel problems with my Jacksons. Compared to the tight lock of Jacksons', the Edeas do seem much more comfortable but also snug at the same time. The Edea rep that helped size me said that it's normal to have some toe-wiggle room in Edeas (unlike Jacksons where it's pretty much like being in a cast) which was an interesting experience and at first I thought it might be too loose but it's tight in all the right areas.

Initially everyone who fits me always thinks I need narrow-fit sizes but I've fit fine in my Edea's (going on 9 months now) normal C width. My other friends who use Edeas all have normal shoe-shape feet, although I think my preference to wearing 'thicker than tights' socks might be making a difference. If possible you should get your feet measured by a proper rep or at least get someone to explain how to do it properly. I did mine over email with a rep providing a sheet with all the measurements I have to give them. My length was roughly 223mm and my boot size is 230. The rep told me I could also do 225 if I wanted but since she said it's normal to have some toe-wiggle room she recommended the 230. Oh and before you think "wow but 7mm of space? that's a lot!", that's what I thought too but apparently she said about 5mm of that is padding so technically in a 230 boot you can have a 225 foot and 5mm padding.

The ankle height of the boot feels lower than my Jacksons and much more loose around that area. But like the others said, this is pretty normal (you can also see a video of how loose Patrick Chan ties his Edeas at the top, easily slipping 3-4 fingers in beside his ankle), and makes you work hard on building ankle strength when doing edges rather than 'relying' on the stiffness of the boot to do the work - something a lot of older skaters say. In a way, you can cheat by adding things like ankle bunga pads to take up the space, which is something I've seen people do.

Apparently Edeas are lower to the ice than other boots but I didn't really feel a difference. I did find it weird about the higher heel, it made me feel like I could face-plant a few times during my first few sessions on them. Another girl who also wore Edeas advised me to do some crossovers as it helps get used to them. It didn't take long to get used to it - probably fine within about 5 sessions.

I noticed that the Edea footbeds are really flat though, and though I have no foot problems, I would get pains in my arches after skating awhile on them - something I hear is quite common. I replaced the insoles with superfeet black (most people use yellow but black worked better for me) and since then no problems.

A lot of people say you can't really overboot in Edeas, but I can't really comment. I think because of the looser ankles that's probably true, compared to other brands where if you put a beginner in really stiff boots they don't know how to control it properly but you can control how loose/tight Edeas are based on your lacing technique. I can say that I know skaters using Edea Chorus (rated double to triple jumps) before they've even learnt waltz jumps, and a few using Concerto (rated triple - quad jumps) who are doing single jumps except axel, as well as 1 older lady using Edea Pianos who is still working on her 3-turns. They seem to like the boots so I guess overbooting might not be an issue?

I decided to weigh in on the Ice Fly experience, as I was so desperate to read as many posts as I could find before making the change. I'm a tall adult skater with single jumps and have been in Klingbeils, Harlicks custom and stock boots. My last experience with Harlick just didn't work out, and having wide feet, narrow heels, a high instep and tailor's bunions made skating for an hour painful. As a last resort I was recommended to an Edea fitter in the Boston area, and he was very knowledgeable about everything I was describing. The C width was necessary and he made a custom orthotic for my high arch to help with the fit. They actually felt very comfortable in the shop, and I was hoping that wouldn't change once the blades were mounted. The footbed profile works well with pattern 99's, and since my pattern 99 was actually too short for the boot I was in, I had to go up a size! ( usually people report having to go down a size). Also having had an orthotic in my Harlicks, I didn't notice a big heel height difference. The ankles do feel slightly loose and I added ankle gels and a lace strap to help with the feeling of stability. The first time on the ice I had to get used to the way they flex. I was able to be in them for an hour and forty minutes with no pain- I've never had that with broken in boots!
As others have said if you have a good fit with a certain boot brand be it custom or a stock boot you should stay with it. If you do decide to go with Edea go with a recommended fitter.

Good luck!

I have had my Edea Choruses for about a year. I had serious pain with them when I first tried to skate with them, and also was uncomfortable with the higher heel than my previous (used) Jackson Freestyles.

I had to get them 'punched' out (especially my left boot) 2 times before I could even do crossovers. During the summer I had to have them punched out again as my feet get bigger in the warmer weather.

Now I am very happy with them. I only do singles right now but am a bit on the big side (and a male), so the extra support hasn't hindered me. I am happy to know the boots should be good enough for doubles should I ever be able to achieve them.
