I can't speak to whether your boots are starting to break down. But if not, I do find it puzzling that the paddling has broken down - that would certainly suggest it is breaking down, if you mean it is not as stiff as it used to be. Quite possibly, the poor fit contributed to that break down. A good snug fit everywhere is one of the best ways to extend boot lifetime.

Heat molding causes some break down. But in many cases it is worth it. If I had heat moldable boots, I would probably do it if they weren't on the verge of breaking down, because it helps with fit so much. - but if yours are already breaking down, that might not be a great idea.

Punching is something you can do yourself - with a ball and ring pliers. But it usually works a lot better if you apply heat - e.g., with a hair drier.

If the heel lock you are looking for is at the bottom of your feet, neither punching nor heat molding can help with that. If it is higher up, it might help. However, there are simple things you can do with tape. E.g., if you put a good quality tape (e.g., a good cloth athletic tape, or a good foam tape, like good quality moleskin) on the bottom of the insole under the heel, and extend it a bit past the edge of the insole, so it comes up around the sides of the heel, that could help. Or if it is loose right at the back, you can stick the tape past the back of the insole. You can also stick moleskin to the part of the boot where it is loose.

And you can stick moleskin wherever it does not feel snug, and that will make it snug.

None of the things I suggested will fix break down - you would still feel a lack of support. AFAICT, break down occurs mostly because the stiffening elements that are between the layers of leather are breaking apart. But if your boots are just one layer of leather (or other material), the leather itself breaks down too - and single layer figure skating skate boots mostly do not last long in any event. All the better quality figure skating boots, AFAICT, have two layers of leather (or synthetic) surrounding stiffening layer(s).

Unfortunately, most of us lack the skill and tools to rip open the seams on those two layers, and replace the stiffening elements. People, and some companies do occasionally do that on very expensive boots.

There is a nearly universal recommendation in forums like Goldenskate, that makes sense, that you should find a really good skate fitter, and get new boots that fit right and are appropriate to your level to begin with (or after a heat mold). It is possible that you also need more expensive boots, that will break down more slowly, but we don't really have enough data in this case to be certain. But do I gather from your inability to get them punched, that you don't know of a really good nearby fitter?

Just curious: What brand and model of boot? What are your most advanced skating moves?

What part of the lining is getting holes and wrinkling? I.E., how far from the bottom of the boot?

Regardless, if the lining of the heel is getting holes and wrinkling, that suggests to me that it is too loose. Why not just add a little moleskin there. If you don't like it, it can be removed fairly easily. Does it make a difference? At the least, you may develop callouses on your heel, which may or may not bother you. If the looseness goes far enough, it may eventually create sores and abrasions, and maybe make you bleed there. But to a significant extent, the heel also helps control the movement of the skate. So, if you are looking to improve fine motor control, it is possible it would help if you got the heel more snug. Jackson Elles, with fancy spins and turns... And to do all that, I bet you put in a lot of practice time. The Elles have given you remarkably long use for what you do with them, and deserve an honorable retirement.

So it might not be worth snugging up your boot, unless you can't afford new ones, or new ones take a long time arrive. Though it only takes a few minutes to snug the heel with moleskin.

Sit spins force the boots to flex a lot. If you point your free toes, that bends them in the opposite direction. Likewise if you try to do deep edges by using a sideways ankle, as I was taught, that bends the boot too, in other ways. So you need boots with more durability (or, like hockey boots, a looser fit - but I think that would make all your fancy spins and twizzles harder to control) - i.e., higher end boots, even if you don't jump. I'm surprised they lasted you a year. I broke down a pair of low end boots - maybe comparable? - mostly doing ice dance in a month or two, a few hours or so a day, even though they initially fit quite well, and I wasn't able to do many of the moves you do.

Boots can be too snug, especially around the toes. I very possibly got my osteoarthritis (in my case metatarsal bone spurs) because my misfit boots were too snug there, causing the toes & metatarsals to rub together, and maybe I have bunions as well (my big and little toes gradually changed angles to point inwards), despite repeated heat molds and punches. I wish I had given up on my old boots a long time ago. I hope you are able and willing to upgrade.

Could someone with appropriate experience suggest what boots the o.p. might switch to, given all the pointing and flexing and bending they are subjected too? (I assume not super-high-level boots designed for triple or quad jumps, because they would be too stiff to allow all that. For that level durability, would good quality leather uppers be better than synthetics? Or do I have that wrong?)

Jackson Elles are nominally heat moldable. But the toebox is the least easy place to widen by heat molding and punching, because you have a complete arch over the foot, and because figure skates have to be relatively stiff there, from a basic structural perspective. I was unable to successfully widen the toebox of my old skates enough for comfort despite repunching (sometimes with heat) every few days. If you don't want osteoarthritis and/or bunions (or other damage, if the boots are too short), I personally think a good fitter is worth it, even if the travel costs are as much as the boots themselves.

Short answer:
1) a Jackson 5w fits me (more or less) directly out of the box with zero modifications
2) the boots and blades arrive mounted with the blade (more or less) correctly positioned
3) I can buy them online directly through Jackson ready to wear and don't need a fitter



Long answer:
The only fitter in my area everyone goes to and travels to misfitted me and confiscated (ie refunded) my skates after I went back to have them modified only for the modifications to make me feel more unsafe in those skates. As a result, that fitter/skate store will no longer fit me for skates, sell me boots or blades , mount any blades, or punch out boots. They only are willing to heat mold my skates and sharpen my blades, which works out well for both of us since while I don't think either of us wants to work with one another as fitter-skater again, she does a good job at sharpening especially given I let the hollow flatten out by extending the life of a sharpening with a honing stone.

And the thing is, Ive been in Jackson Elle's for 3 yrs and am on my 3rd set at the moment. I have loved these skates. I have hated these skates. I have been sent to the hospital by these skates. I have learned everything I know on these skates. I also rarely fall in these skates. Maybe since I don't try and work on basics so much that I was able to go from 2 foot spins to what I have now in 3-4 month after realizing that it's not my skates holding me back from these spins, it's me. Adding bunga pads to my ankles and cut up bunga gels to the top of my foot activated the spinning ability of the boots, and I guess I'm hanging on to this set since they've been really good to me. Each time I have a lackluster skate and say I want to retire them, I just skate in a way that makes me so happy the next day that it's like they're magically stiff again. Maybe I'm just getting stronger.........

But even though I've had really bad times on these skates, the good times outweigh the bad days by such a large amount that I can't hold it against them. They have given me so much- strength, conditioning , and the ability to endure the unendurable day after day after day without faltering. I've also started to just really trust and feel in sync with them over this last year. Especially since I can feel the entire blade with my foot and just have this sense of where the foot and blade are in space to the extent that I feel so stable and safe on that blade most of the time.

It's also hard to get rid of a skate that has really always come back from the dead to give me one more, and another and another and so on..... push. And im just so grateful to these skates. I love these skates and my body. Not once have they failed me as long as my mind has held solid. Even when I've injured myself on them- I know my mistake and have long since fixed it- and even then they've made me so insanely strong that I've been able to feel immense gratitude towards my boots by giving me this incredible gift of what I feel like are unstoppable spins . I may wait years to be brave enough to try a new skill and work on things I can fall on, but once I start attempting stuff I stay upright and make progress while still being actively offended at the ice when I fall since it's so rare.

After sticking with the Elle's for so long and getting close to year 4 and ossibly another pair of Elle's or freestyles and no coach since I feel like it's a waste of money given my current equipment, I've just resigned myself and given up all hope that equipment wise things will get better. I wish I was brave enough to use social media to share my skills in an effort to get a skate company or shop to gift me skates and help, but I'm not. One day I will be a force of nature to recon with but for now I'll endure and change the only thing I can- my attitude.

* I'm having a difficult time parsing what you wrote. What does "flattest" modify?

* Right now, it reads as if it modifies "8 ft main rocker". That is, you are looking for a blade with the "flattest 8 ft main rocker". In which case, that makes no sense. That is, all blades with an 8 ft main rocker have the same longitudinal flatness of the main rocker. There will be deviations arising from manufacturing inconsistencies, of course.

FWIIW, very simple modifications to the insole, that you can do by adding a little tape, or sanding, can have essentially the same effect as moving ("offsetting") the blade sideways a bit. Because all you are mostly doing is changing the weight distribution.

Re-mounting your own blades is a bit of a challenge, though some of us have done it.

I do not know enough about Jackson boots to say whether the fit is similar for other Jackson boot models. And it is true that fit tends to be more critical with stiffer boots, because they can't be modified as much. Nonetheless, minor modifications to the insole, as well as punches you can do yourself with a ball and ring pliers and a hair drier (after letting your pro shop do the heat molding) can usually improve fit - but as I said, the toebox is very hard to change much, especially on higher level boots.

It's a shame you don't have a better relationship with your local skate tech. But it is good that you like the job she does at heat molding and sharpening. If Jackson didn't accept the returned boots, or if she had to pay return shipping, she took a pretty big loss on that. But most pro shops, AFAIK, make most of their money on sharpening. So maybe she will make some of it back over time. And in all fairness, fitting skates exactly right seems to be very difficult, in no small part because boot makers aren't all that consistent in the way they make boots. What was the change she tried to make that she messed up?

Perhaps Jackson Elles fit you right in part because they aren't very high level - they are soft enough to conform more to your feet than high level boots would.

Are you using the hand hone to actually sharpen, or just using it to re-straighten the edges? (Edges tend to get knocked sideways (inwards or outwards, depending...), especially if you don't put on blade guards before stepping off-ice. I think a lot of the time people just need to straighten the edges rather than re-sharpen them.) I.E., what is your honing technique?

If you don't happen to use blade guards that way, it's a good idea, though I suggest you put on one guard, step with it off-ice, then the other, rather than trying to balance on the ice wearing guards on both blades at once, especially if using hard guards.) That way you need fewer sharpenings and honings. And again, I suggest you dry off your blades after you take them off, and don't store them in hard guards between skating sessions, because edges last longer that way too. Even a little moisture, or a little dirt inside the hard guard channels, messes up the edges.

Many skaters who don't have to travel great distances, and pay the costs of doing so, to find a great skate tech, don't appreciate just how hard it is to deal with that. I'm glad that you have found a practical way to do so. I don't know what to say about whether you should try to get them re-heat-molded. There is a risk that that will make them too soft, or widen them too much elsewhere. (AFAICT, heat molding on Jackson Freestyles only widens them - it can't make them thinner.)

Were I you, to make more space for the toes, I would try one of the things I suggested before - remove the insoles, trace them out on something a bit thinner, cut that something to the same outside shape, and use the thinner insoles you create that way in place of the old insoles. That just might make you enough space to help. But keep the old insoles, in case you don't like the results. And I admit it will create a looser fit elsewhere. (I assume you don't feel up to cutting something like a skin-safe closed cell foam in a three dimensional manner, thinning them at the toes, but not so much in the rest of the skate, so they aren't looser fitting there.)

However, that might not be enough. Especially if what is really happening is that the stiffening material inside your toeboxes is breaking down, and is pinching your toes when you put weight on your toepick.
