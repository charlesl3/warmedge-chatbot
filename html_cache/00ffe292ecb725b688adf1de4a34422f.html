Since I can't see you skate, this is all general. Please consult your own coach for specifics.

Try practicing making snow on the spot with each foot and both together. Make sure you have even pressure and the blade slides smoothly. You want to be pushing with an inside edge of about 45 degrees. Any smaller and the edge is going to catch. You also want to make sure you don't go past 90 degrees, which will also catch the edge and make it impossible to get the skidding motion.

As mentioned above, practicing at a slow speed can help with the confidence.

Make sure you have pressure into the ice, bent knees and that your weight is on the stopping foot, especially if you're trying a snowplow stop.

Hope that helps.

Since you're already stronger on your left foot, why not just do a one-foot snowplow stop (don't know if there's a different name for that) for now? At least then you'll know how to stop somehow.

In addition to what others have said... when stopping, your weight is on the stopping foot/feet. But that doesn't mean you are vertically above your feet; you will need to be slightly leaned backwards as you press into the ice, in order to not fall forward when you stop. (Think of how the Looney Tunes' Road Runner stops.) You should also try to feel how the ice is resisting your blade as you press into the ice. The resistance is what will make you stop.

Don't despair - one day it will "click" and you'll have lovely snowplow stops! I remember it taking me months to feel confident stopping when I first was learning to skate. Nothing about my technique changed, it was just gradually getting used to the feeling and how much pressure to apply. Maybe think about using the middle part of your blade (inside edge) and don't look down!

I am surprised to see so many people saying t-stops are easier for beginners! That certainly wasn't my experience. Good luck!
