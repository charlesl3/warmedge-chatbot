Hi! It's definitely never too late to start skating, but in terms of what you're asking really it's important to know what you mean by 'future in figure skating'. Absolutely you could get to a point where you can compete in adult competitions, but if what you are asking is whether you will ever be able to do what the Senior Ladies skaters do at their Grand Prix Events or at the World Championships, the honest answer is no, you are never going to be able to do that. With that said, you'll never know how far you can get until you start- I know of people who started at your age or later who did ultimately get some double jumps so that is definitely possible if you have the time, motivation, and aptitude.

First off, how exciting that you finally get to start taking lessons! I was in that same position a year ago and it was so nice to finally be able to do something I'd always wanted to do (fyi, it's taken me the equivalent of eight or nine months of skating to even get to Adult Basic 4). Realistically, I'm sure you know that you won't be competing internationally, so if by competing you mean competing with Ashley and Gracie for a podium spot at nationals, that's simply not going to happen. However, there are adult nationals, and collegiate competitions and such (aren't there even adult international competitions just for fun?). So if you mean could you get to adult nationals at a lower level (maybe bronze? I'm not super familiar with how adult nationals works) in five or ten years and enjoy skating a ton in the meantime, then yeah, that might be more realistic. That's assuming that you have 1) lots of money, 2) lots of time, 3) ability. Remember how many hours it takes to make a figure skater proficient and how many tens of thousands per year it takes even at, say, an intermediate level. Even if you have the money, I think it's highly unrealistic for you to expect to be putting in a ton of time as a new college student.

Personally, I'd say take lessons and enjoy the heck out of it. But keep your expectations very realistic. Very realistic. And I'm curious to hear what other opportunities there are for adults in the U.S., because I too am a new skater as an adult. I don't harbor any great ambitions, but it'd be nice to know what's out there, maybe find a good goal or something.

It's never too late to start skating. I know several adult skaters who started as adults and have some doubles and are really good skaters overall. Realistically you are not going to get to Senior level competitively. The skaters you see on television have been skating 10+ years, have spent countless hours a day skating and training, worked with high level coaches, and have a certain degree of natural talent. That said, there is adult nationals and adult international competitions. Adult Gold is the highest level in the US of the four adult track levels and getting to the championship gold event at Adult Nationals is a pretty big deal, and something that would definitely be possible for you someday.

I am really excited that you get to start skating. Skating is one of my greatest joys in life as a adult skater- it has it's ups and downs but it is definitely worth it. Good luck!

Like some others have said, it is a bit unclear if you are asking if you can start skating with Olympic aspirations or with the aspirations of basically skating as an adult. The Olympic thing is basically out as 18, but in my opinion, that is when the fun begins! I started skating seriously about a year ago at 41. I skated briefly as a child when I was about 5 and 6 years old, but even being that young, I remember the pressure on me to keep passing tests, and I grew tired of it and disinterested. Now, as an adult, I can test if I want, I can make progress if I want, or I can just skate laps and talk to friends. There are several people who come to our adult group to do just that, though most folks there want to make progress on their skating in some shape. I am actually looking forward to passing some of my lower level tests next year, I am polishing up my Beta skills for ISI right now and am extremely happy with where I am.

Skating as a child up through your teens is probably really exciting, you could be that rare gem that ends up very high level, but in all likelihood you will use it as an activity you enjoy, make friends, do synchro, local competitions, and you may even skate as a younger person in ice shows or at college. Getting some parents to understand the reality of skating as a young person can be difficult, many seen to dream of the medals more than their kids and make their kids feel guilty about the money spent if something spectacular does not happen. As an adult you control the money, so that aspect is gone.

Skating starting at 18 you are pretty much starting as an adult, so the same things are open to you in adult form. Adult synchro teams, competitions, ice dancing for fun or competition, the sky is the limit. The other thing is, you can get as into it financially as you want. If you have limited funds you can go to a slow public or coffee club session once or twice a week, skate in rentals or get basic skates for Christmas or your birthday. For a little more money you can do Learn To Skate classes. For even more you can do private lessons and skate more frequently, you can advance as slowly or as quickly as you wish.

The general rule of thumb out there is to not self teach. I agree with that to some degree, you need guidance to make sure you are not learning things incorrectly. I do think everyone is different in the way they learn and their confidence level. Personally, at this point in my skating life, I take an occasional private lesson(once every couple of months), I take group lessons in our Coffee club about once a week that usually focus on body position, power and edge work, and I work independently the rest of the time. I have made good friends who seem to enjoy skating with me and giving me pointers from time to time, and I see my coach all the time. She has encouraged me to talk to her if I am stuck with something but says she feels I am progressing nicely. I watch videos and read constantly, but putting things into practice takes just that, practice.

I hope you decide to give it a shot, skating is hard, harder than it looks, but be patient and persistent and soon you will be making it look easy.

I'm feeling really happy to see many fellow adult skaters here The first thing I've to say is you're lucky to be in such a country with various resources and rinks around !

I can't say that it is easy (to compete as an amateur adult or to be an adult skater), but it's not that impossible. A good coach, a strong will and an ambitious effort are really needed. As a college student, there will be plenty of things along the way. There were many occasions that I had to read exam textbooks in the warm-up room, stretching while listening to lecture's records or wearing skates while doing the research online by the rink. I had to get up earlier to make up the time for studying and sleep a little later because I join the evening session and my campus is 1-hour away from the rink. This was exhausting, but fun and worth it

I started skating five months ago at the age of 22 as a 5th year medical student who wanna vent out stress and tiredness. It came out that I was one of only few adults in the rink, stumbled around with little girls jumping and spinning all the way. I felt so embarrassed at first because I'm slow , prone to fall all the time and I was really far from being athletic. I'd never done any sports seriously before, to be honest, I'd nothing in my life apart from textbooks and exams.

However, thanks to my coach's patience who guided me to fight with my stupid flatfeet, my wobbly ankle and fatty leg. I had to do self-exercise off ice frequently. I'm not a quick learner and my coach tried to teach me with the kid's standard so I could get everything as perfect and correct, so I'm progressing slowly. I'm happy with what I am now. My coach and me set the goal together to get to ISI freestyle 1 in January and get the program for exam

Hope you find the way as an adult skater's life and enjoy !!! Figureskating is soooo addictive that you can stay on the ice for hours and miss it when you're not there !
