Hi guys! So my skates finally came! I felt like a kid on Christmas. I waited by the front door for hours until my mom got tired of seeing me check the window every time a car passed by so she made me to leave the living room.... so i went to her room because it’s closest to the front door [emoji23] I got my blades mounted same day but it took too long so I wasn’t able to skate that day.

Me and a friend of mine, who both quit skating at the same time when we were younger (and also are getting back into skating at the same time now. how funny), went skating yesterday. My first time back on ice in years. Even though I wasn’t able to do much, i felt so much better, i’m really so excited to skate again. Having new boots + new blades left me doing edge exercises and such, just to break in the boots + get used to the new blades. The cross cut toe pick will definitely take adjusting, but I hear good things about it so I am hopeful.

The only really discouraging thing is that my jumps and spins feel like they are on vacation. Like i understand fully how to do them in my head, except my feet just aren’t coordinating. I had a little tantrum moment on ice and kicked a chunk of ice (sorry to whoever skated over that) but it was really frustrating. I thought I would be able to be practicing on my own for a while before doing lessons but now I’m thinking I may just have to jump straight into lessons again because I feel so alpha-beta beginner level haha. My dad tells me not to be too hard on myself and give myself a handful more tries because it was only the first time but it’s hard cause it’s there, i just cant get my feet to connect with my memory.


Any returning skaters have similar struggles? Any tips and/or advice?

How long it will take to get your jumps back is based on several factors:

1. How solid a jumper you were before you stopped skating.
2. How long it's been since you've been on the ice.
3. What kind of shape you're in.
4. What kind of non-skating activity you were doing when you weren't skating.
5. Any injuries you might have.

If you've stayed in decent shape, then you should probably get your singles back fairly quickly. It'll take longer to get them to the quality you had them before. Higher level jumps are much harder, but not necessarily impossible.

Hi sk8chis

The no injuries is good. I quit skating because of every injury in the book and I'll be doing well if I get my singles back (minus axel) in the next 2 years. The lack of physical activity is probably what'll cause you the most difficulty. You'll need to get back the strength and awareness to regain the timing needed for free skate elements. You'll progress faster if you add some off ice workouts into you routine. Dance classes, swimming, Pilates, yoga, etc. would all help a lot. (You don't need to do them all. I personally don't like Pilates, so I avoid it at all costs.)

The actual time line is impossible to predict, but those things could help speed up the process. Just be careful to pace yourself and understand that you might not be able to return to where you were when you were younger. It's going to take time and you need to go at the pace your body wants, not that you want. Try not to be too hard on yourself. This is a crazy difficult sport.

It's easy to forget little things after awhile off the ice, and little things are what make the difference between being able to land a jump and not being able to land a jump. Where should your shoulders be? What is the exact timing? Same with turns, edges and spins. For this reason, I'd suggest starting in lessons ASAP. A coach will see what it is you're forgetting to do and correct it so that you won't be wasting your time and getting frustrated.
