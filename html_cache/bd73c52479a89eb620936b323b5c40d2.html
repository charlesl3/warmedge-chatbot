You've laid out your questions quite well, but we likely need a bit more info before giving some thoughts. It sounds as though your listening to others' experience, but sincerely want the best solution for your own needs.

Coaches love the Coronation Ace blade and equivalent in other brands for certain levels of training, but it's not clear from your post exactly what level you are at.

Depending on what federation you are in and whether an adult learner or a child/adolescent competition may start at different levels. So would need a bit more information than just that you are starting to compete.

The type of boot and blade needed will be determined by what kind of jumps you will be doing (assuming you will be competing in singles rather than ice dance), but also your weight and strength of jumps. Small, very young and light skaters won't need as stiff a boot for the same level of jump. Depending on this you may want to match a more advanced blade with a lighter boot. As well, the shape of your foot (e.g. high instep or flat foot) will impact whether a boot will work or not. The moulding process doesn't take care of all of this.

Has your coach made recommendations to you, either in terms of blades and boots or in terms of good places to shop?

There are shops, especially in cities with elite training centres, that really know how to not only fit your foot, but match blades and boots to your needs. One of our kids' coaches was very particular about where her skaters should shop, even though it was more than a couple of hours away in another city, and felt the same about where to get orthotics. It basically meant a day trip, but going there was the difference between being able to progress or not for one of our kids.

Hi Goldenfigureskater,

Coronation Ace blades or equivalent sound right.

If you are light and small you really don't want to overboot. Our kids' coaches advise to keep to less stiff boots at your level where possible in order allow the ankle and foot muscles to develop and strengthen. You don't want to get in the way of good ankle bends and deep edges. Going for the lightest boot that could last you to your first doubles is probably what you want to target.

This is a real issue for the boys at your level at our club as a number of the boot brands don't offer all the models in boys skates. So most of them are in Riedells, which offer all the their styles in black, in order to avoid jumping up too quickly. I'm getting the feeling that you are a girl, since you are comparing yourself to other girls, so you will have a broader range of brands and choices.

I find to be frank the whole concept of buying boots on-line to be concerning and surprising at your level. I don't actually know folks who do that where we are. It's a matter of going to a store close by or further afield to one that has an elite clientele. Perhaps there is no one reliable close by, but I would double check with your coach on that. Perhaps she/he might recommend support the idea of a trip to get skates if it would mean getting the best for you.

Boots are a significant investment. You may save a little buying online, but you'll lose out on a personal fitting. Our ideas about brands have changed as we've checked things out. And the brands are changing how they make the skates, so even folks who have been with the same brand for years are checking others out. It's great to do research as you are doing, but getting your feet into different models and brands might surprise you.

Different brands fit and work differently for different people even if you don't have significant issues. Edeas may be cool and lightweight, but without a fitting in store, you won't know if they are best for you. You'll likely be in the harness working on your axel and first doubles in these skates within the year. You really want them to be "right".

My daughter is 11 so 1 year younger than you are. If you are like by daughter, you won't like my advice.

When we purchase skates/boots for my daughter, my goal is for them to last one year. Why? her foot grows; she breaks down the boots; and as she gets better, she needs better boots. For us, the one year is a good guide but I know of other skaters who are lucky to get 6 months from a pair of boots.

Your coach has recommended Overture or higher. From the literature I read, Overtures are rated for singles including the axel. You are currently working on your singles and have not yet started the axel. My guess is Overtures work for you for at least another year. I see no reason to jump to Chorus since they are rated for doubles. No way should you even consider Ice Flys. Use the price difference between Overtures and Chorus and to get a good competition dress.

You state that some girls say EDEAs are "dream" boots. Some skaters love them, but not all. One girl at my rinks got them and wound up breaking her ankle the first week of wearing them. The girl's mom blamed the EDEAs so as soon as the girl's cast came off, the mom purchased a new pair of non-EDEAs. A couple of years back, EDEAs were all the rage out our rink - since them, a good number of those EDEA skaters have switched to a different brands. Then there are the logistics issues. Whenever my daughter gets new skates, we have to go back to the dealer at least twice for some fine tuning. If you were to get a pair of EDEAs', who would mount them and do all the other "stuff" described by loopy?

My daughter has tried Riedells. Risports, and Jackson. Jacksons work best for her. I agree that it is time for you to upgrade out of the Jackson Elles but from your comments, I believe the Jackson are working for you. As a result, I see no reason for you to switch brands. My guess is the Jackson Competitor would be a good choice.

You need to get both your parents and coach involved in this decision. Please let us know what you decide and how they skates work out. Good luck!

Hi again,

I agree with loopy's suggestions and questions.

Regarding concorde's suggestions, I agree with him that you can't expect to get more than a year out of your skates if you haven't finished growing already, and at your weight it sounds like you still have some time to go.

However, if you are looking to compete your flip soon, you may be starting more advanced jumps soon. Recognizing that there is a long way from starting to learn a jump to being ready to compete it, it would be reasonable for you to start to work on your axel and possibly a single jump within the year. While most get the axel first, I've noticed that some skaters seem to train the axel and 2S and 2T at once.

So again, it would be important to know your coach's plans for you over the next year so you have a more firm sense of your needs. 10 hours a week on the ice seems quite a lot at your level for the regular season. We would normally be advised to book 4 skates at 50-90 minutes each plus off-ice until you are through your preliminary test, but this guidance varies. So it looks as though you are in a push to make some progress.

I've looked again at the Edea website. I can see why you are concerned to look at the Chorus, as the Overture is only rated to axels. However, as I mentioned below, if you are small and light you may not need as strong a boot for those first doubles as the average the manufacturer is targeting with this model. This is where a good skate fitter who knows the brand can help you avoid overbooting.

As for injuries and brands, I've heard of problems with most brands as the techology has been evolving. Heels breaking off on landing big triples seemed to be an issue for many skaters a couple of years back. There were skaters hunting for used, old model, heavier skates with stronger construction as they felt the new technology wasn't meeting their needs. At your level, the injuries I've heard about are less easily attributable to a particular skate and have mainly come from a bad fit, poor mounting, or a skate that encourages bad habits.

Best of luck and kudos for researching and asking for input

Good luck Goldenfigureskater,

I understand concorde's concern about follow up, but it sounds as though you are at the level that it's worth the trip just to check out Edea to see if it is right for you. Perhaps any minor tweaks could be handled once you are home, but I'd recommend taking a break at the store [say go to lunch and come back once the blades are mounted] in order to have a chance to catch as many little fitting issues before you leave as possible.

Hopefully your parents have something else that they'd like to include on your day trip. I suspect if they are skating parents, an excursion to check out better skates for you is not such a huge request in the big picture of things. I suspect that they might be relieved that you are open to getting advice on what best fits your needs rather than thinking too much about what others are wearing.

I know that many have sworn by Jackson/Gams for years. And five years ago, it would have been hard to justify making a long trip to check out other brands. But they are definitely a very different skate than they were even 5 years ago. I had expected that our kids would be in them, but all of ours needed to switch brands after their first pair. Yet other kids do just fine in them, and many adult skaters still swear by them. So, IMO you are taking good care of yourself by asking to check out alternatives and see if they are better for you. The cost of a slightly more expensive skate or a day trip is, in comparison to your training time, a modest expense.

Enjoy your trip!:agree:
