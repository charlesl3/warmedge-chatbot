Looks like your first listing is worth a shot, but not your second one.


Here’s one of my previous posts that discusses various aspects of laces for figure skate boots:


http://www.goldenskate.com/forum/showthread.php?59239&p=1420653#post1420653

Updates and additions:


(1) Riedell has reformulated their laces again and no longer offers the combo cotton/poly laces that I preferred. Haven't tried their latest laces.


(2) Stay away from laces that are specifically marketed for shoes. You want a high-tensile-strength lace specifically marketed for figure skate boots. Most "shoe laces" are probably not strong eneough. And most “shoe laces” have a max length of ~72”; OK only for kiddie skate boots. There are “boot laces” for hunting boots that have a max length of ~100”; still a bit short for many adult skate boots. Laces for hockey skates and roller derby skates are often too wide to thread through the narrow eyelets of figure skates (the 1/4” width in your first listing should be fine).


(3) For cross-sectional profile, you want flat, or perhaps, oval. You do not want round. Years ago I tried round boot laces (I couldn’t find any long enough, so I used two shorter ones). Flat laces provide more contact area (lace-on-lace or lace-on-tongue); round laces provide less contact area. Eyelets on figure skate boots are round, so a flat lace ensures sufficient friction to keep the laces from slipping through the eyelets easily (laces stay put as you tighten them); round laces slide through round eyelets more easily. Also, tugging hard on round laces causes the laces to cut into the eyelets more, leading to more wear and tear on the eyelets than that caused by flat laces.
