I'm a guy guy, so I can't recommend products specifically for women. Here are some general tips though.

In one of your other posts, you mentioned you bought new Jackson Elite 4200s. I have the Jackson Elite Suede, which is roughly the men's equivalent (Jackson is somewhat odd. Most manufacturers have the same line for men and women, with a choice of different colors and sizes for men and women. Jackson has a slightly different line for men and women.). The Jackson Elite boots are lined with memory foam and microfiber. They are also initially sized for a very snug fit. As you break in the boots, the linings will compress and mold to your feet, and they will loosen up a bit.

I'm a bit surprised at how you were fitted. Typically, the tech will take tracings of your foot with what you plan to wear while skating (nothing, tights, socks, ...). Were you wearing your soccer socks when you were fitted?

At any rate, you're lucky you have a tight fit without soccer socks; otherwise, they would be too loose without soccer socks. You don't want to wear thick, heavily-cushioned sports socks for figure skating. They are too mushy, and your foot will wobble (even when the boots are laced tight), leading to a loss of fine control. I know some people do skate barefoot, but I wouldn't recommend it, both to increase the longevity of your foot and the boot. The linings of the boot (including the tongue and insole) can abrade your skin. And your sweat and skin oil can attack the linings. So you want some buffer between your feet and the linings.

I also agree with another post: avoid nylon and stick with cotton. Nylon doesn't breathe too well, doesn't wick away sweat, and is slightly abrasive. Cotton breathes well, wicks away sweat, and is soft (at least for good quality socks). The high cotton blends have better stretch and longer wear than 100% cotton; I would recommend a blend with 70 - 90% cotton. For men at least, the thinnest cotton socks are thicker than the thinnest nylon socks (I think that's because nylon is stronger than cotton, so you can make a thinner nylon fabric). But the thinnest is not necessarily the best: you want good wicking capability and good cushioning consistent with good control. So I wear high-cotton blend dress socks; they are thinner than athletic socks. The ones I wear now are slightly thicker than the thinnest ones I've tried previously, but I find they are a lot more comfortable and longer lasting, while not being mushy.

Before you proceed, make sure that the boot at least fits properly with no socks. If so, then start your break in with the thinnest socks, regardless of the material. For example, I break in my boots with nylon socks, because they are the thinnest. After a number of sessions (say 6 hrs worth; will depend on the individual), you should notice that the fit is not as tight as initially. Then switch to a slightly thicker high-cotton blend sock. It will feel too snug at first, but then loosen up. This two-step process reduces the amount of cramping during break in. Do NOT break in the boots with thick socks; in addition to potentially painful cramping, the linings will compress permanently to some degree; and they will not be snug enough if you should switch to thinner socks. Hope this helps. Good luck, and have fun!

I buy opaque microfiber socks at the grocery store (Jewel) and love them. They are basically just like opaque tights except they're socks. I think the brand is No Nonsense - will check next time I go to store. Anyway, they're cheap - about $2.00 pair - and wear like iron. I have had some pairs for a couple of years and they don't run or pill.

For reference, I really don't like thick socks because as Tstop4me says, you lose fine control. They are breathable enough for me to skate in for a couple of hours without discomfort or blisters.

Only downsides IMO: since they're cheap, they are not super fitted - just one seam at the toe; no shaping at the heel. Also, if you wear orthotics or insoles with a slick surface, the socks slide a bit. With Superfeet or the skate's own lining, there is no issue.

Ah, this is a good point. Especially for figure skating, you need to pay attention to details of fit and seams. You don't want a sock that will pinch, bunch up, or tear. Some socks don't have a shaped toe: the fabric at the tip of the foot section is just squeezed together and sewn. You either end up with pinched toes or a sock that's too long and a toe that bunches up. One of the worst socks I've ever worn had a separate shaped toe cap that was sewn onto the foot section. But the seam was a hard ridge that was placed very close to the ball of the foot: it would bite into me. Other bad socks I've worn had little contour and stretch at the heel: if I tugged a bit too hard, the heel would tear. On good socks, the seams are placed away from pressure points and are very fine; you can hardly feel them. I think the execs at sock manufacturers should all have to walk in them for at least a mile before they market them.

Speaking as a person who sews their entire wardrobe (including skate gear), I gotta jump in and say something: Cotton is NOT breathable, it is a natural fiber that is incredibly hydrophilic. It's great for wicking away water, sweat, so on, but once it's absorbed, it stays there.

This is why you do not see performance gear made in cotton, as it can chafe or cause blistering. Sportswear is commonly constructed with synthetics (think Nike Dri-Fit, swimwear, etc) because of its wicking and drying properties. And in the winter time, cotton is especially not recommended for sportswear as it soaks up water and can contribute to hypothermia (hence wool/wool blends for winter clothing).

If you want to go the natural fiber route, bamboo fabrics are an ideal alternative, otherwise, you are not doing your sweaty feet any favors with 100% cotton.
