Hi there! You have options. Borrowing of course helps, and happens in Juniors, and sometimes in the Senior level, too. You can also rent costumes from other skaters' families (yes, that is a thing), or buy some outright.

If you have somewhat standard measurements, you can buy less expensive dresses online that are new with a generic cut, and then stone them yourself for a custom look. (Look at lyrical costumes from jazz/modern/stage dance costume companies as well. Those are fairly reasonably priced and you can embellish them, too.)

Do not use hot-fix rhinestones. They fall off too easily. Find some tutorials online on affixing crystals and use the following glue:

E6000 Fabri-Fuse – Eclectic Products

eclecticproducts.com

I don't think there is a better one on the market that I have tried.

Hope this helps!

A good place to start is to get a Jalie pattern - you can look for them and buy them online. There are a few styles to choose from. Try making one exactly as the pattern dictates... later you will understand how you can change bits. You need a sewing machine that sews a zigzag stitch or a stretch zig zag... or better a serger (but absolutely not necessary!)

Lycia fabrics work the best. You might want a thicker fabric for the bodice and lighter fabric for the skirt. May want to double the panty. Chrisanne Clover has loads of colours. Check their online shop. They sell from UK, Poland and Japan I believe. Local markets also often sell lycra products.

Follow the instructions on the pattern. They are pretty easy to follow. Just remember that straight stitches don't stretch! Always use the zig zag. You can embellish with crystals, fringe, appliques from other lycra colours or lace motifs. Just keep thinking about when applying something, it will need to stretch when you put it on!

You can completely change the feel/look of the dress by clever embellishing. Have fun! Also... if you make a mistake, take a deep breathe, unpick your work and do it again! Sometimes it helps to handsew the pieces together before you machine sew them, they won't move as you sew then.

Good luck!

As a professional in the field, I can absolutely recommend the Jalie patterns to begin with. If you're wanting to make dresses, I would suggest starting with their "Tessa" dress pattern, as it's well-designed and easy to put together. The illusion neck is easily redesigned into other angles and shapes, should you so wish, and it explains the technique of doing the basic one very well.

Take your time and choose a Lycra with a good weight to it to start with, as the flimsier ones are more difficult to handle. Something over 200g/m is a good place to start.

Machine-wise, if you don't have access to a serger/overlocker, then use a zigzag stitch to start with. Some machines have an actual "Lycra stitch" but be aware that it is fiendishly difficult to unpick and has been the reason for many a garment flung in the corner and never finished! Practice with zigzag first. Unpick what doesn't work and try again.

Cutting out Lycra and mesh fabrics is much easier and more accurate with a cutting board and wheel.

Line up your seams and tack/baste them in place before stitching pieces together. It gives a far better result.

And remember that you can usually crystal over bits that haven't gone quite to plan...!

All of these are excellent suggestions. Used and buying plain dress "blanks" to embellish are the quickest way to more custom costumes for now.

Costume-building has different stages, and as you start learning, you might not enjoy all of them (I don't, and I've been sewing since I was 3). That's okay. When you get patterns, even the excellent aforementioned Jalie patterns (out of Quebec, CA - the can send you paper ones or you can buy digital PDFs), you still have to start out by adapting the pattern to your body. Often, that involves sewing a "toile" or "muslin" test fit of the bodice - but you will want to work with a stretch fabric that has the same amount of give as your final fashion fabric.

Sewing is another stage - sergers are great, but many people sew great skating dresses on conventional machines. (Look at online tutorials to learn the difference between such machines.)

Embellishing is another stage - glueing on rhinestones to create the "character" of the dress.

From what you wrote, you are at a stage where you want costumes that better suit the theme of your program, and that don't cost a lot of money. This may not mean making them on your own - or, it might.

I make this distinction because yes, you can get a used machine and view online tutorials on:

-how to alter patterns
-how to do a test fit
-how to sew stretch materials
-how to embellish costumes

and more. There will be an initial cash outlay (machine, cutting mat, scissors, rolling cutters, snips, plastic clips - no pins on stretch if you can help it, patterns, fabric, thread, elastic, etc.) If you buy a machine through a dealer, they often have lessons on how to use it included as part of the purchase and free maintenance for a period afterwards. (Big box stores don't do that, so not the best option for purchasing a machine.) If you buy used, have the person you bought it off of demonstrate how to use it (there is more inherent risk in this type of transaction though).

So why am I spelling all this out? Because costume work is a learned skill - one that anyone with drive can attempt to master. Sewing and fitting stretch is a learned process and it might take some time. Meanwhile, you still need something to wear. It doesn't have to be all-or-nothing.

But if you are motivated by being in custom costumes and saving money, and not intrinsically motivated by sewing itself, then your aims of 1. looking good and 2. doing it cheaply can still be accomplished by buying plain dresses to embellish. At least initially. You can even buy a well-fitting bodysuit and drape/tack stretch mesh in layers for a lyrical-style dress.

I do costumes myself (including all of my kid's) and I focus more on fit. I don't particularly like the stoning process. Some moms get dresses made for their daughters, and stone them elaborately themselves. There are many different approaches. You are the person to best determine what suits your likes/dislikes.

Look up the Dress #1 video from thisgirltash - she did a nice job of showing her process; watch it and see if what she's doing speaks to you. If you are motivated, no reason that costume-making can't also be part of your skating journey.

My mother made mine when I was little-and she had just basic sewing machine skills. I am a fairly good seamstress, but skating dresses are a special sort of challenge due to working with very difficult fabrics (Especially 'skirting' with chiffon etc.-there are some fabrics where you really cannot make a mistake and pull out the stiches). I would say that you need close to expert skills, but no reason you cannot attain those. Bear in mind there will be mucho frustration! Also it is much easier to make a dress for someone else (you can fit them) then for yourself. (hard to pin and fit yourself). There is a group on fb, 'buy and sell' figure skating and while most of the dresses for sale are for children, there are adult dresses as well.

Yes! Step away from the velvet. For the love of all that is holy.

I still have flashbacks to a few years back when I worked with velvet for the Jalie 2791 princess-style dress pattern. In my infinite wisdom, I used velvet for the fashion fabric. And I have this weird quirk where I insist on lining pretty much *everything* on a dress: briefs, bodice - even the sleeve ruffle was lined with coordinating spandex underneath. (Rinks can be cold and skaters need to be warm.) The seams, the seams ("The horror... the horror...") - I think I was hand-cranking 8 layers at the seams with my Babylock. Not to be repeated. I have stopped short of lining flesh-colored mesh and chiffon skirt layers, though...

This is why the tip of hand-basting layers together (so they don't distort and shift) is an important one!

As I was later told regarding the dress: "It doesn't need to re-enter earth's atmosphere..."
