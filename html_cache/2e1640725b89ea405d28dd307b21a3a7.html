Sorry in advance that this post is about feet!

The only skate brand I've ever worn is Riedell. They were popular in the midwest where I grew up and what I chose when I bought my most recent pair of skates (the only ones I've bought myself as an adult). As a high schooler I had very narrow feet and while my feet are still pretty narrow I've had to increase the width of my skates due to tailors bunions (these are bunions on the outside of your feet on the small toe). I have a regular bunion too but it doesn't cause any pain.

A part of the reason for buying my most recent pair of skates was tailors bunion pain, it improved when I got my new skates (which were heat molded and punched out) however was still causing some pain so I started using bunga pads for tailors bunions which has helped a lot. If I wear them I can skate indefinitely without pain. If I don't wear them then I have pain within an hour. My current skates probably have 6-12 months of life left so I am starting to think about their replacement. I am pretty indecisive so I usually give myself a long time to think about big purchases.

Does anyone else have experience with bunion pain skating? I've been doing some research on different skate brands and in particular brands that offer semi-custom skates. I've had several people suggest looking into Harlick's because of how customizable they are but don't have much experience with that brand. I'd appreciate any other skaters advice (I am also asking other adult skaters I know as well as both my coaches for their opinion).

I've had anything and everything a skater could get happening to their feet to mine in my career. I mostly get monster calluses, but I have gotten bunions, especially when I used to wear Riedells. When I switched years ago to Jacksons it eased the bunions (I have wide toebox with a narrow heel). I could never wear Harlick's for that reason because they catered mostly to narrower feet, I hated SPTeris, but I did get away with wearing Risports for my dance boots when I was still dancing.

I hope you are able to find a skate that works for you and you are relieved of your pain.

You may want to look at Jacksons. Relative to many other brands (such as Riedell and Edea), Jacksons have a more rounded toe box. The more advanced stock freestyle boots (those listed on the website as using the Elite last) also standardly come in split widths (toe is one width wider than heel), so you get an even roomier toe box while maintaining a snug heel. If this works for you, you might avoid customs. A good fitter will take measurements and tracings and send them off to Jackson for proper sizing and let you know whether stock is OK or whether custom is needed.
