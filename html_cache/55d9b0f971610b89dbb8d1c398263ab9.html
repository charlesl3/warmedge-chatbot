Hi all! I have a couple questions. I'm interested in Risport boots, I've heard from a handful of people in my local skating community that Risport is making quality product currently, while some other brands are not.

Not sure if you guys have ever used Edeas, but I noticed Risport uses the same sizing for foot length. Did you find that your size in both of these brands was the same? (I'm talking about the length, not width). I currently use Edea Ice Flys in a 245B, I like some things about them such as that they are light, the width is customizable, and they are easy to break in (there's never a too stiff feeling with these). What I don't like is that they cause me foot pain, despite that I use a different insole with an arch the matches mine and ultimately I worry that they are bad for my feet and body long term. I also think edea is having quality problems, as my newer pair are not exactly like my older pair and have problems that the older pair did not have.

I have very narrow feet and high arches, but I also have a heel that is narrower than the ball of my foot (I think this is considered split width?). I am trying to figure out if Risports are designed for my foot type or more for people with feet that are a similar width throughout. I do freeskate, so one of the most important things I look for in a boot is that it holds my heels down.

What is the difference between the Risport Royal line and the RF line? How do I determine which line is the best for me? I am currently on junior moves in the field, I do all types of spins, and I do double jumps, no triples. So the lowest model boot I think I could use is an RF2, but I am worried that maybe that wouldn't be stiff enough. My most recent skates are ice fly for like 2.25 years and before that I used Riedell 875TS for 3 years (I wouldn't go back to Riedell though, unless it was a custom since I can't get a fit I want from their stock boots). The Risport Royal Elite looks nice, but not sure if it would be too stiff....

I noticed that the Royal Exclusive and the RF Exclusive have a different design (mainly a different heel that looks plastic). The design looks similar to that of an Edea Ice Fly. Do these boot models have the custom shaping ability of an ice fly or is the only difference between a royal elite and a royal exclusive the plastic heel? What's the point? For looks? Is it lighter? I noticed the royal exclusive only comes in B and C width which also caused me to wonder if it is similar to the ice fly since that too is only available in limited widths and I know the two companies used to be under the same owner.

I'd appreciate any information and feedback that you guys have to offer. Unfortunately the Risports aren't as well known in the US, so less information is available. Even on the internet I can't find as much info as I can about Edea, Jackson, and Riedell. I always like to do research and get feedback prior to making the long trip to get a fitting and potentially purchase them. Thanks!

I've never actually skated in Edea so I can't say this with 100% certainty, but when I tried them on I felt like lengthwise they were the same fit as my Risports. Because the sizes are in millimetres, I doubt there's much difference between the two. Regarding the width, Risport do have multiple width sizes and go quite narrow, which is why I wear them, haha. They do have more of a similar width throughtout than Edeas do, so if the ball of your foot is snug in your ice flies, expect Risports to be tight.
IIRC, one of the main differences between the RF line and the Royals is the collar. The RF collar has more of a V shape and is longer so it wraps around your ankle, while the Royal one has a U shape and is shorter. Risport posted a graphic comparing the two on their Instagram page a while back I think. Not sure what's the deal with the heels on the Exclusive models of both line - I never really thought about it but I'm getting curious now so hopefully someone else will be able to give some more insight.

Hi skatespin, I wanted to give you an update. When about 4 pairs of boots from Edea all came in defective, my skate tech and I sadly agreed that there's a problem with the production in my size (250). When I got no response to my email to the company, I gave up and got myself a new pair of Risports instead. I chose the Royal Elite and was professionally fitted by my same skate tech who fitted me for the Edeas. I have skated in them twice so far, and I have to say that already I am totally in love with these skates. They may very well be the best skates I've ever owned. They fit me so well, and the very first practice I adapted to them very easily. Over the past 6 months, I have been in my original custom Harlicks with Gold Seal 9.75" blades, then the Edea Chorus with Gold Seal 9.25" blades (along with a brief stint on Maxtrix blades to try them out), and now Risport Royal Elite with Gold Seal 9.5" blades. I know there will be yet another adjustment to find the sweet spot on these blades for spinning after changing blade size so many times, and my next goal this week is to get most of my tricks back. I have not done any jumps or back spins in 6 months because of the defective Edeas, so I know I have my work cut out. But, I already love these Risport skates and I know this was the perfect decision for me. The heel height is not as high as Edea, and with my low arches, these work really well for me. I also got the narrow AA width, which locks in my narrow heels, and then had the toe area stretched out at the shop. With Edea, I could never really get used to the high heel feeling and the wobbliness. Also, my arches hurt like crazy in them until I figured out how to lace them properly. These Risports lace up easy with no special technique. Let me know what you ended up doing -- I'd like to hear an update.

The sizing is similar yes. The Edeas are not good for those with high arches so it's not surprising to me that they aren't working for you (they're more for flat feet).
You will need a split width and that's not uncommon in skaters. All the Risports you listed would work for you. The only difference is that the Royal Exclusive and the RF1Exclusive are made similar to Edea's. They are lighter weight etc.

I know this is strange, but I almost feel like we are using the same skate tech based on your descriptions. I also think we have almost the same foot size lol.

So I ended up driving to my old skate tech, who confirmed that my Edea Ice Fly heel/sole (not sure which of the two) was defective, by holding an unmounted blade to it to see that it would not naturally lie flat on both the heel and sole. He said that he has been seeing the problem, that he has had to order and then send a lot of boots back, and also mentioned an international pairs skater that skates at that rink has been having the same problem (the pairs skater and her coaching team were at my current rink for a while in the past). I needed skates badly and the Ice Fly situation seemed hopeless, so prior to going to there I had them order the RF1 Exclusive and the Royal Elite. I ended up getting the Risport Royal Elite in size 250 AA with a Matrix Supreme Blade, originally I tried the 245, which I would have preferred, but it just felt too tight at the toes. So with the 250's I had the toe box opened (via the hairdryer method) and then stretched when that wasn't enough. These boots are stiff, so they are not fully broken in yet, but they are getting there and as I have been breaking them in they start to feel wider and have some heel lift. Hopefully this can be fixed via the hairdryer shaping method with clamping, but I live far so I'll probably have to do it on my own. I have been unhappy with one of the mountings (blade feels wrong, difficulty getting on an outside edge), but I don't think it's the mounting/alignment itself but the sole so I am going to talk to someone I know who works with blades to see if he can remedy the problem, perhaps with a shim. So I'll have to reserve my opinion about the new skates until they are completely broken in and have some adjustments made. So far I have attempted MITF, all spins, all single jumps, and lower level doubles on them. There are a lot of changes from what I was using beforehand, so it's been quite an adjustment.

Risports are very solid boots. I have an old pair of super diamants from the 90's. They are tanks! Absolutely love them! I'm sentimentally attached but know I'll need new skates eventually.
