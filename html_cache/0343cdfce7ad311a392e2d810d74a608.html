The MK Pro has a 7' rocker, making it slightly more curved than the CA which has an 8' rocker. Others here are better equipped than I to explain what the descriptive term actually means -- I learned the terms when I was 5 and never asked" 7' or 8' what?"

The MK Pro is usually seen as better for spins and footwork, the CA for stability in jump landings, being somewhat flatter, but the difference is so slight as to not matter at the beginner stage. I've used MK blades my whole career, including triple throws as a pairs skater, their Pros up to the senior level in competition because there didn't used to be as much choice in blade models. Now I use the MK Phantoms which have a longer pick that gives more elevation in jumps. I've tried CA blades, but the 8' rocker made them feel clunkier, as if there was too much blade getting in the way in turns and spins . But you won't feel that if you learn on them from the start, just as I learned everything on the MK Pros.

I hope that doesn't confuse you and make you feel you shouldn't get the CA blades after all. Just trying to answer your question as to how the two blades differ. Good luck!

More specifically, they both have 7' main rockers: i.e., the radius of curvature of the main rocker is 7'. The main rocker excludes the spin rocker (and a transition zone, in some instances, depending on how you specify the blade geometry). Very approximately, the main rocker comprises the rear-most 3/4 portion of the blade.

* Blade manufacturers typically do not release detailed specs for their blades. So there are no primary sources to refer to.

* My tech tells me he finds significant variations (for new blades that he receives and sharpens) among the spin rocker profiles of JW and MK blades. So keep that in mind.

* Paramount did their analysis of various blade profiles. Google "paramount sk8s figure skate blade profiles video". As far as I can tell, many other websites have regurgitated their results without attribution. Caveat: Paramount doesn't state their sample size (i.e., how many units of each blade were measured, and what the variation was). Their measurements were done with a 3-pt radius gauge.

* If you really want to get into the weeds (deep, deep into the weeds), Google "Bill_S comparison between mk pro vs ace". This analysis was performed by a mechanical engineer, using more detailed measurements and analysis. Results differ from that of Paramount. But the same caveat: measurements were done on only a single sample of each. It's a long read, but contains comparisons of other features (beyond the rocker profiles), as well as a functional, on-ice comparison (again, valid for this one skater).
