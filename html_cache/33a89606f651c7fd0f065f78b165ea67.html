Welcome Lily!


Although you will progress faster with private lessons I do think you should finish out the Basic series.

Once you've passed Basic 6 you can start the Freeskate series.


Unless money is a challenge there is no reason you cannot do both group and private lessons! As you've gone through the Basic series you may have found a coach that you like and perhaps you and your parents can arrange a few private lessons.



Good luck!

If you want to advance, at some point you will need private lessons. An important role of a coach is to provide you with individual critique of what you are doing wrong, and tailor corrective actions. Unless you luck out and have a very small group and consistently have the same group instructor (and a good one at that), you won't get this individualized attention in a group lesson.

That said, private lessons are expensive. In order to get full value, you need at least one, preferably two or more, practice sessions spaced apart in between private lessons. This is difficult to do, if you can skate only on weekends. So before you take the plunge on private lessons, how much skating can you do through the week?

I think you need to talk with your parents and see what they are willing to support. Since they will be transporting you to and from the rink and paying for it, then what they decide is the path you are stuck following.

If they say they are all in, then I would recommend both a group and private lessons. With the group, you get instant "friends" and there tends to be some competitiveness between each other which pushes the group along. With privates, you get someone that focuces only on your technique. I taught swimming for 20+ years and noticed that students that had both group and privates made the most progress. Early on, I followed this philosophy with my daughter.

If you parents say only one (group or private), that is tricky. I would probably go with privates only since you get the individualized attention but if there was one instructor that I really liked teaching a group and that time worked, then the group may make more sense.

But the advantage of taking a bunch of group lessons is you get to try out lots of different instructors and from there, you get to determine which one you relate to the best.
