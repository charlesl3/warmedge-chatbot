Curious what the general consensus is on stretching skates. I'm not talking about punching them out a little bit, but really trying to get 1/2 to 1 size out of them. The type of stretching where you have to leave your skates for a day or two and come back for them.

Of course, when buying new skates, it is preferable to have them fit properly, but kid's grow sometimes in unpredictable ways, so stretching helps bridge the gap when they grow.

Our old coach was of the opinion that stretching the skates was a stop gap measure - if the kid's foot grew and they needed the next size up, you can stretch while you wait to get the new skates but then you switch over to the new size ASAP.

When we got my skater's Harlicks, the company said you can stretch them "up to a full size" which seems scary to me (leather can't stretch an infinite amount, but maybe it can go a full size???). So if you start in a Size 1, they say you can stretch it to a Size 2, so would you then buy as a your next skates a 2.5? or would you buy the 2?

I've asked Harlick to provide some feedback (still waiting for a reply) but also curious the thoughts from the group. These skates are leather....I think synthetic materials may have different "stretchability" but I could be wrong and would be interested in hearing about that for future reference.


Side note, my brain is complete mush today so instead of musing over work I'm inclined to think about skating (more fun). I'm totally procrastinating but justifying it because I do need to figure this out.

The stretching equipment has settings on it, for how much the boot is to be stretched width wise and for length-wise. Shops usually only stretch a slight amount when customers don't specify. More intense stretching is harder on the materials, but unless the skates are really cheap, the boot won't come apart. The boots need to be heated before stretching, and some shops don't do it (akin to heat molding). Also, they may have to be stretched multiple times, as there will be some spring back effect. Sometimes stretching just the width will make the toe box lower, so there is caution to be had if toes have gotten fatter requiring a higher toe box (I haven't had to worry about that, so I'm not sure if anything can be done about it... I doubt it). Also, at least on our equipment, the length stretching may make the heel pocket bigger, so there is the risk of heel slippage if it is over stretched. Keep in mind that stretching just length or just width may have the opposite effect on the width or the length, so make sure you know exactly what needs to be changed in the size, and if there is room for narrowing up the boot if you want more length (this is why it helps to have a fitter who maintains records of your past fittings).

I stretched the toe boxes of my old boots to at least a full width wider than they were, and it did not harm the materials or construction. However, I had to stretch them once a month to maintain that width. I also repeat-punched them out at those times, to maintain the space.

If the toe box width is especially a size issue, there are oblong punching bits that can be used to make slightly overlapped punches, that if done properly, will make the toe box wider than stretching will. It will look ugly on the outside of the skate, but the skate will be wider. Of course that will not help any with the length.

I interpret the comment as stretching to the feet, not machine stretch. So you purchase skates that fit your child now. Kids grow so those skates will "grow" with the child up to 1 size beyond the true size.

I know of 1 situation where the skater wore one size and when they went to get new skates, the replacement skates were two full sizes bigger.

On my skater - she had them stretched 1.5 sizes and when she got new skates she really needed 2 sizes up. Yes, they tend to curl the toes vs complain about small skates. I found when they start to complain, they are way too small.

If you keep your skates out of the car and heat, you shouldn't have an issue with them "shrinking".

I definitely say "uncurl your toes and tell me how they feel" (sidenote, my skater has claw toes so this is constantly a battle). I also try seeing whether I can stick a pencil or finger down the back of the heel with the toes uncurled and pushed to the front of the boot.

When my skater was really small, I used to just take out the insoles and see how the foot was laying on top of it. I discovered the skates were too small once when the foot was overhanging the insole by about a quarter inch (I felt so bad!). I find its easier to do the insole test when there is a heel cup in the back because its obvious how the foot should sit in it. the flatter insoles are more challenging for this test.

I also have a cheap plastic version of those foot measuring tools. It is not terribly accurate for sizing but it is helpful to see if the foot has gotten longer. I also find it easier to check the street shoes and determine whether they are too small - if we have to go up in street shoes, its usually a sign that we need to go up in the skates too.

Another clue for really small kids is frozen toes.
