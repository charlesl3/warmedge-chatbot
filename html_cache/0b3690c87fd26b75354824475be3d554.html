I agree with Ic3Rabbit - despite the "popularity" of Edeas (and they do have their positive points), they are NOT for everyone. If your daughter's foot is better suited to a different brand, the Edeas will only make her miserable. Even if the coach tries to get you to use Edeas, resist the urge to go with what everyone else is doing and see a proper fitter at a specialty figure skating store that carries a selection of brands and models. I would trust a respected fitter to advise what is appropriate for her foot shape and skill level.

Our old coach used to want all of her skaters in Riedell -turns out it wasn't the right fit for my skater and once we found something that *did* work all of the pain/fit issues/complaints went away. Our current coach is very adamant that he is not in charge of determining appropriate skates and asks the parents to take them to a figure skating store to be fitted properly.

Edea has this thing where they claim that you flat-out can't overboot with their brand. As someone who skates in Pianos, I'm... skeptical on that, to say the least, especially for young, just beginning, or very small skaters. I love my boots but I got them after doing triples for years. Lol. And I'm also a pretty tall adult (I don't have a petite body type.) The Ice Flies are rated just a smidgen under the Piano in terms of stiffness (Ice Flies are popular at my rink, and I tried them on when I bought new boots - the difference felt, to me, to be more in the shaping than the stiffness comparison.) I don't think a 9 year old would ever need an Ice Fly, honestly, let alone someone just starting doubles/axel. I don't think they're worth the expense for you. If her current setup works, I say stick with the setup. Especially with skating equipment, why fix what isn't broken? Your daughter will eventually get to a point where her equipment is not well suited to her skill level. That's when you reevaluate, not when you notice a trend. I think you're in the right to be skeptical here.

My daughter has moved through 3 different Edea models. We knew she was never going to be in a traditional boot again, so after using the lower 2 models for a couple years, we switched to Ice Fly just to minimize adjustment between models as she grows. I have watched girls go from boot to boot and blade to blade as they advance and each time they change sizes, models, and blades, it's a big adjustment with both boot/blade change and size change. In my opinion, it was worth the additional investment for a seamless switch every time she moves up to a new size. We know that she will never need to adjust to a new boot or blade since she is currently in the combo that she can use all the way through her skating years.
