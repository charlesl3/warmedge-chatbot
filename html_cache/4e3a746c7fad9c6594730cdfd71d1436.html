No, only for beginner skates. Usually if you have a skate shop recommended, they don't have your skate in stock. They measure, help you decide and order it for you with a deposit. Sarah, I really hope someone comes to your rescue with the skating emporium that you seek.
I remember when my SoCal rink stocked a large selection of SPTeri's and Reidell's but that was back in 70s.

I was recently in the market for new boots and happened to be traveling through Florida. None of the 3 rinks I visited had any better selection of figure skates and boots than my own rink - and they don't keep much actually in-stock but will place orders.

I knew I wanted another pair of Harlick's so I went to their factory here in CA - and they didn't even keep "stock" boots there!
I had to place an order. Of course their fitter takes multiple foot tracings and they keep them on-file. In fact, they had all my foot tracings for the past 30 years.

There are two online entities that seem to stock a lot of skating items: Kinsie's Closet and Jerry's Skating World. Perhaps you can call them and see if they have a physical location that actually stocks boots.

As far as bootmakers go, both Harlick and SPTeri make custom boots and you can go their respective factories to get measured up. Both are in northern California, about 1/2 hour south of San Francisco.

Sorry for not having responded to your original query. There are two well-known pro shops in the Chicago area: Rainbo Sports and Geppettos Skate Shop. Both are in the suburbs. I've bought skates at both of them and had good experiences. There are quite a few other skate shops around here, but these are the two I can personally recommend. Both have very good reputations, and both have competitive skaters and out of state customers among their clientele. Jason Brown and Kori Ade both used to get their skates at Rainbo (they may still but I don't know since they are now in Colorado).

What I would suggest is that you take a look at their websites, do a little preliminary research on skate brands and models, and then call to speak with them / make an appointment. You should explain you're coming in from out of town, be able to tell them what the problems are with your current skates, what your level is and how often you skate, and your street shoe size. You can ask if they will have different skates available to try on in your size; I'd say the chances of that are a bit higher at Rainbo, but at either store, you may try a lot of skates on, settle on the right size / model, but discover they need to order your skates, which means you'll need to return in a few weeks to pick them up, make sure they fit you, have them heat molded, and have the blades sharpened and properly aligned. The most important thing is to make sure you speak with / get fitted by an experienced fitter.

Rainbo Sports is in Northbrook, Illinois. John is the person most people go to for fittings. If Stephanie is still there, she is also very good. They used to have younger competitive skaters working there at times; not sure if they still do, but I would try to speak with and get fitted by a more experienced fitter. http://rainbosports-px.rtrk.com/?ut...CPC&utm_campaign=SEMRainboSportsFigureSkating

Geppettos Skate Shop is in Downers Grove Illinois. John is a very expert fitter. The other guy who works there, Mark, is very nice but I would suggest having John fit you. I don't think they have as many skates on display as Rainbo, but John is really good at getting skaters into the right make and size of boots, and if you need customs he will tell you. http://www.askmredge.com

FYI, most but not all skaters with a wide forefoot and narrower heel do well in Jackson's (that's what I'm in). Riedell often fits skaters with narrower feet and / or tapered toes. Both shops carry Jackson /Riedell. Geppettos is also an authorized dealer for Harlick; not sure about Rainbo. I'm not sure if either shop handles SP Teri or Edea.

LMK if you have questions.

You might want to plan to make a week-end of it. That's what we did when my husband got new liners for his telemark ski boots. Even though he was re-using the shells, it still took two days: a morning to locate the liners, an afternoon appointment to have them heat-molded, and a final appointment the next day to pick them up and have final adjustments made.

Fortunately we had friends in the big city who provided us with a place to stay and shopping advice.

It was worth it, as he has problems with protruding ankle bones and finds the new liners very comfortable. I don't know if they do this when molding skates, but they taped thin closed-cell-foam padding to his feet in various places, so the molded liners have a bit of extra space in some critical areas, like the ankle bones. There's quite a bit more involved than "take them out of the oven, put them on, wait for them to cool".

Unless you're really lucky (the boots and blades you want just happen to be in stock in your size), you won't be able to pull it off in one trip. The situation described by loopy is typical. The skate tech takes measurements and tracings of your feet and sends them to the manufacturer. Even if you take a stock boot, delivery often will be ~2 wks. Special orders will take longer (maybe a month), and customs even more time. When the boots arrive, you return to try them on. If they are a close fit (perhaps with minor fiddling), you're set as far as the boots are concerned. (If the fit is too far off, you start over.) Once you've approved the boots, the skate tech may have to order the blades you want in the proper size (again, unless you luck out). This may take another week or two to come in. The skate tech will mount them temporarily and probably sharpen them, you pick up, try them out, and return for needed adjustments. This may take several iterations. Once all is well, the skate tech will mount the blades quasi-permanently.

Alternatively, if you know how, you can mount and remount the blades yourself if making the trips to the pro shop is really inconvenient.
