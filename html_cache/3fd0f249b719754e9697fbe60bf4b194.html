Hi, can anyone recommend me a reliable skate fitter/shop in Melbourne, Australia? As far as I'm aware, there are the Skater Network stores that only sell Risport (and are based at the rinks), and Google shows me one other store called Bladeworx that sells Jackson and Edea as well, but seems to be more of a roller skating shop. I've planning to check both out but would be good to know if their fitters are reliable as I'm not 100% sure I'll know what to look for when fitting. Also if they are reliable for heat moulding/sharpening etc? Can anyone provide reviews of those or recommend any other option?

Bit of background if relevant (and for further skate model recommendations): I'm a woman in her 30s, (155cm, 60kg). I skated for about 1-1.5 years in 2010-2011 (only up to doing tiny waltz jumps) and then quit as I got too busy, but am now looking to get started again. The first skates I bought were Risports (not sure what model as I've lost them, possibly Etoile?) which I found squashed my little toes (especially the right foot) so probably too narrow toebox. When those starting wearing down I bought some WIFA skates online but those are even more uncomfortable and would become painful after a short period of skating (I think my toes are proportionally longer than the skate accounts for despite total length being correct so while initially ok, they slowly slide down while skating and get squashed. Or possible too arched for my feet? I'm not sure.) I tried for awhile to make them work, and then I ended up quitting anyway so I never fixed the issue or got new skates then. I still have the WIFA skates and am planning to restart skating in them for a few sessions, but I want to get new skates that fit well as soon as I can. My goal is to at least get back to where I was before, and I would like the skates to be able to at least accommodate single jumps in case I want to pursue that. From my online research, Jacksons may suit my toes better and perhaps the Freestyles would be a good level given my heavier adult weight. Other options are Risport, but I don't know if the toebox will be too narrow again, sounds like the RF3 Pro may have a wider toebox but that may be too much boot for me? Perhaps Electra if the toebox is ok? Mixed info on Edeas for wider toes but happy to try. For the Edeas they don't sell any boot/blade sets here so their beginner Overture is quite expensive and only 100AUD less than the Chorus, so sounds like it would make sense to go for Chorus if Edea fits best, but again may be overbooting?

Thanks in advance for your help!
ETA: Melbourne, Australia. Just remembered I should specify as there are other Melbournes!

Hi. Risport does with the toeboxes, not Edea-it's going to be too narrow for you. And b/c you ask: I know from experience for multiple decades, as a former elite, now pro who has worn or tested most boots and blades out there and some that don't even exist anymore.

Based on your foot type and what you have said already in your initial post: Graf, Risport, and possibly Jackson would work for you.
Now, being in Australia you more than likely can't get Jackson there but Graf and Risport you should be able to. With your other stats included you should be fine in Risport RF3 Pro (no it won't be too stiff). In Graf F4000 would work as well. If you do have a place to get fit in Jackson you should try Synergy Elite or Premiere 2800.

Blades: traditional intermediate blade: JW Coronation Ace or MK Pro.

The next closest would be Skaters Edge in New Zealand, which is a figure skating fitter and they specialize in that along with some dabbling in inline and roller. I know they carry the boots I have suggested (at least Jackson and Risport). I would contact them if you can and they may be able to do a virtual fitting if you cannot travel there.

Good luck!

No. Intermediate blade is where you would start. Beginner blades are for beginner sets and usually flat which will not help your skating whatsoever.

I will not steer you wrong here and there are many that also give advice here like @tstop4me and @Diana Delafield that usually chip into these threads and agree to recommendations. I have helped countless skaters here for 8 yrs, as well as offline for decades.

As far as virtual fit-I'm always going to say that getting fit by a figure skate fitter in person is always the best.
VF techniques vary and well, it's better than nothing especially with a fitter that knows what they are doing and specializes in figure skates.
That's all I'll say on that front.
