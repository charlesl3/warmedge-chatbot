Hi everyone,

First time poster here!

I'm an adult figure skater, and I've been skating for just over 1.5 years now. When I first started skating I was on rentals, and had a fall onto my hip which caused a bit of pain for a few months but nothing too bad. I fell again which caused tailbone pain but I just bought some crash pads and kept going.

Fast forward to two months ago. I slipped off the back of my blade mid-mohawk and landed flat on my back. Hip pain returned and so did the tailbone which were quite bad. A week later I returned to the rink and witnessed a fellow adult skater fall face first into the ice and crack her head. All the coaches rushed over and my coach had to cover the blood.

After that I decided to take time off until January next year to recover.

Long story short - I am absolutely terrified of getting back on the ice. The fear of injuring my hip again is so bad and so is the fear of hitting my head on the ice.

I'm 170cm tall so there's a fair way for me to go when I fall!

I already wear a crasche band, knee pads, hip and tailbone pads and wrist guards but I just can't seem to get over the fear.

Any tips to help me get back out there would be amazing!

Welcome! What were they doing when they fell face first and cracked their head? And when you say adult skater, are they taking figure skating lessons or do you just mean an adult who happens to be skating.

I had some pretty bad falls on the side of my hip (nothing broken but I was in pain for a good week or so). This made me fearful of the pain so I started wearing hip pads. That was about 1 year after I started skating. I'm now up to 2 years. I gave up the hip pads a few months ago so I actually only wore them for a few months. I have fallen but it just doesn't hurt that much as I go in with much more speed. I have never worn any other protective gear.

I'm also a little concerned that you said your fall onto your hip lasted a few months? Should really last like 2 weeks at most.

If you have a coach you should ask them to conquer your fears. Unfortunately I think some people just have bad falls and some people don't. I'm also 170cm and that's not really tall in the average world. There are many elite skaters especially men who are taller and do difficult jumps.

Check with your coach for mohawk technique. You're obviously doing something wrong that's why you fell. Identify that you made a mistake, not that you're going to fall going on ice doing things.

Asking a coach would be your best bet, I think. Also, having an orthopedist check out your hip and tailbone, if you haven't already, might be a good idea.

How was the skater who cracked her head? How soon did she go back to skating? Was it actually a severe injury, or was it a fairly minor but just particularly bloody one? The fact that she was an adult probably had very little to do with it. I think that kind of gnarly injury usually happens as a freak accident.

Are you still on rental skates? Make sure you have proper, well-fitting, level-appropriate skates. Also, you might need to go up a level if you're heavier than the usual skater at your level.

It sounds like you need to learn better falling technique. Ideally, skaters should be landing somewhere fleshy (butt cheek, thigh, etc.), not flat on the back. Of course, that's not always doable, but it's something anyone can learn to do better. Working on strength and balance off-ice could help as well.

I second the advice of learning to fall safely, as well as the advice of seeking out your coach with your fears. They're used to this. They're somebody you can trust to be there with you and make sure you're approaching your technique safely, which should minimize injuries. And you're outfitted in all the body protection you need, it sounds like - although would a helmet help? Some really experienced adults who skate with me still wear them for extra precaution. If it gives you peace of mind, go for it.

Yes, learning to fall safely really is the best advice you can get I think. Whenever I notice I'm getting scared/cramped up I fall on purpose a few times to remind my brain that falling is ok and doesn't even have to hurt and it works every time for me haha. Do you work out beside skating? Doing strength training might help you get the confidence that you're strong enough to control your falls and decreases the risk of injury even if you have a bad fall.
Lastly, about the height thing - you might have a greater distance to fall, but you've also got more mass to absorb the blow. I'm even taller than you (1.82) but don't have more bad/painful falls than my short friends - I even know someone who's nearly 2 metres tall who's working on double axels, falling a lot but always immediately getting back up again.

So just from what I read, maybe it would benefit you into looking into some off ice balance exercises. If you learn to find your center and know where place your arms and position your legs properly, you will reduce the chance of falling. Lets face it though, falling is part of skating. You're never not going to fall haha. having a strong core and legs are very important in balance in skating. I would try some things like yoga off ice and balance exercises ontop of that. Both would be very very good for you to learn how to keep your body centered. Good luck, let us know how things are going in a bit! Never be scared, the more scared you are the more you're already setting up your self to fall because your body is automatically becoming stiff and off center.
