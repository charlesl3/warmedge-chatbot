Mom and the grandparents loved quad skating. Nothing fancy, just backwards skating at most. But since they loved it, we went many times when I was a kid. I started out in quad skates since that’s what they did. But eventually switched to roller blades because I decided I liked those better. Once again, the most I ever did was skate backwards in them. I didn’t even know artistic roller skating existed until the past year!!! I’d never seen anything like that and my mom and grandparents crazy enough never knew it existed either! So our family and town was 100% roller skating is something you do for fun. There’s no such thing as artistic tricks.
I learned about figure skating’s existence from seeing Michelle Kwan on TV around 2001-2002. Love at first sight. Mom took me to our ice rink a couple times, but I hated it because the rental skates made my feet bleed every time and I couldn’t stand up without holding onto the wall. Completely different from quad skates or roller blades. I still desperately wished I could skate like Michelle Kwan, just had no freakin clue how to make it happen. Mom wasn’t aware our rink had lessons and didn’t bother to find out. Eventually, I found out myself when I made another attempt to skate another public session at age 14 and spotted some local figure skaters on the session. Begged mom for lessons and managed to get a year of lessons of out her before she forced me to quit.

I haven’t been roller skating in years, but any time I do, I generally hate it, because it feel like I’ve got rocks tied to my feet. I can’t do anything in roller skates like what I can do on ice. It’s all wrong and feels wrong.
I always felt like I was “upgrading” to the smoother experience: quad skating -> roller blades -> ice skates.
Each upgrade was smoother and less clunky than the previous. The only reason I struggled with ice skates at first is because it was too smooth and too slippery.

It seems really strange, but everyone seems to know about figure skating, while hardly anyone knows about artistic roller skating. Even though it seems like artistic roller skating is how a lot of people transferred into figure skating....



EDIT: I think roller skating is way easier for a total beginner to enjoy because they’re easy enough to stand up in without hugging the wall. But I think in the long run, especially if you want to do tricks, figure skating is way easier and more pleasant.

As a child I tried ice skating in some second hand skates that didn't fit properly and fell multiple times ending up with several cuts and bruises.

Then I tried quad roller skates at an outdoor rink and had a blast. Still fell but not as many, and the added 'stability' of the wheels allowed me to enjoy it a bit more. I grew up within 5 minutes walking distance to a roller rink and went at least once a week. Learned a few 'dance' moves on them, and was thrilled to learn crossovers, forward and backward. Remember doing 'shoot the duck'.

After twenty years, I transitioned to roller blades, and was quite an adjustment. Learned previous moves, and did a couple of waltz jumps (didn't know the correct term at the time; thought it was a 1/2 axel lol; technically it is a 1/3 axel of course).
With roller blades I was able to learn outside spread eagles (stable 'blade). I did this for about 10 years.

3 years ago I took up ice figure skating as the nearest roller rink then was 45 km away. It was again an adjustment, but not as big when going from quads to the inlines. Was able to do most moves from inlines, and took proper group then private figure skating lessons to learn 3 turns, edges, waltz jumps, salchows, toe loops, and loop jumps in combination/sequence. I also do my outside spread eagles (now both ways), and do inside spread eagles (both ways), and ina bauers.

2 years ago I purchased some PIC skates and had a blast at first (way better than traditional inlines), and most moves translate from ice, but everything is harder requiring more precision.

I am currently an ice figure skater at heart (can do a lot more in them), and believe my experience with quads as well as inline/roller blades helped me with ice.

I have to backtrack on my first reply to this thread.

With the rinks closed and Pic skates like gold dust, I bought myself a new pair of Moxi Lolly quads (and a lot of body protective gear!) to keep my skating muscles in shape. Skating on quads came back to me somewhat easily but I have to be super-conscious of any debris lying on the road. Thus I tend to skate looking down a lot, which I don't do on the ice.
The whole quad setup is much, much heavier than my ice skates so I think my legs will be in good shape once ice skating resumes.

I am also hoping to buy a pair of Pic or Off-ice inline skates once the demand dies down, although with my bad outing on regular inlines years ago I'm not sure how well I'll adjust to them.

That’s such a fascinating question, and I can totally relate! I'm 24 now, and while I’ve been figure skating for years, roller skating was a completely different story for me. I actually took it up recently and signed up for classes through Skateraati - https://skateraati.com/. It was such a humbling yet exhilarating experience to go back to being a beginner again.

At first, I struggled to adjust—no sharp edges, no toe picks—but with a bit of patience and guidance from my amazing instructors, I found my groove. Now, roller skating feels like an entirely new way to express movement and flow. It’s also helped me rediscover the joy of learning something from scratch, which can be so refreshing when you’ve been immersed in one skill for years.

For anyone on the fence about trying roller skating (or any new skill), I can’t recommend it enough! It’s a chance to challenge yourself, step out of your comfort zone, and experience that wonderful “aha!” moment when it all starts clicking. Who knows? It might even improve your figure skating in surprising ways!

So, if you’ve been eyeing something new, go for it—whether it's roller skating, dancing, or even learning an instrument. Life’s too short not to explore the possibilities.

The lack of available ice time (rink closed in spring & summer and the club where I am a member offers just one 40 minute group lesson per week for the lower level skaters; public ice time is also limited and sometimes crowded so difficult to practise things) gave me the idea to in parallel do off-ice skating with artistic inliners. So I became member of a club that has access to a sports hall with two slots per week for off-ice skating.

I did a lot of roller skating (and occasional ice skating in those good old unpadded boots ... ) as a kid, bought some recreational inliners as young adult and started figure skating maaaany years later. Latest purchase were the artistic inliners (the linea frame with the adjustable rocker which I can recommend).
I need to apply more force when off-ice skating (compared to on-ice) to gain speed and to do turns plus the skates are heavier (more workout, haha ...). And with the backward wheel being just under the heel the "blade" is shorter than a real blade i.e. one must pay more attention not to accidentially fall backwards.

Otherwise I guess every hour of off-ice skating is beneficial for the ice skating.
I do not see an issue wrt switching between ice skating and off-ice skating. One profits from the other.

While I've done all 3, I haven't done roller (which I did with Pic frames) or inline recently.

The main problem for me on roller and inline is stopping on hilly roads. Beyond a certain slope, I simply can't. Which makes it dangerous. But on inlines that's partly flexibility - I can't comfortably roll onto the brakes. Even in a flat roller rink, I stop slowly.

I felt quads and inline are faster, with less resistance to forward motion - but some people disagree. There are a lot of comparisons of that on Youtube, with varying results. Also that turning, spinning and stopping in general are easier on ice. Almost everyone who does both agrees with the latter.

I did like the fact that the communities in actual roller rinks were a lot friendlier. People tended to take it less seriously. And management has people do games, like line dancing, limbo, etc. At least during public sessions.

(OTOH, I knew people in the inline community who took it very seriously, and did a lot of hard core training - especially in the speed skating community. But most of them also raced on ice speed skates.)

My main ice rink has a lot of uncrowded public sessions during the day (I'm retired), and if you sign up for the season, you can attend you want, at no extra charge. But if that weren't true, and there were nearby safe places to do them, roller and inline would be fun alternatives, to use as exercise between ice sessions. I did a fair amount of inline during the worst of the Covid epidemic, because they closed the rinks. But I did it in a very flat park, where stopping was easier.

I've never done the type of the "freestyle skating" that is done only in hockey skates. It looks insanely fun. But I'm not that athletic. Same for "Ice Cross", as well as the inline and quad skating that is done down railings and in skate parks.

I agree that falling is much safer on ice. In quads and inline, I feel a need for knee pads, helmets and maybe wrist guards. But that might be partly a matter of less practice.

There is also a lot of overlap with the ski community, cross country, alpine and parks. Ice skates feel a lot like "skate skis", but the latter are longer, and it takes more hip roll strength to use them, partly because a lot of it is on hills. Some of the short trick ski moves are similar too. I suppose now there is a lot of overlap with the snowboard community, but am not sure. I've never tried the long "skis" that have wheels.

Some aspects of whitewater playboating are similar too. Maybe stand up paddleboards are more so, but I don't seem able to do it. I'm sure that surfboards are pretty similar, but I have the same problem there.

I suppose there are similarities, but I confess that I don't find the "elliptical machines" in gyms any fun at all. They are exercise, but no play. You can go backwards, but that's about it, unless I'm missing something. And I'm totally the wrong type of body for ballet, cheerleading and gymnastics, though I notice a lot of skaters do those too.

But basically, a lot of sports, especially balance sports, (yes I know - some dancers claim dance isn't a sport - unless they compete in "dance sports") overlap in skills and training. If you have the time, access, and money, I'm sure they can all be fun.

So do it if you want to.
