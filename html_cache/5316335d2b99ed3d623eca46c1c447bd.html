I have a question that I need some help with.
I got a pair of Jackson Freestyles with the Ultima Aspire blade premounted about 2 months ago. I loved them at the pro shop but now they are giving me so many problems.
Here is a list of my concerns:
1) The blade is not the best quality. It has been sharpened once and I can already see how short the blade life will be (maybe the sharpener did not do a good job, but regardless).

4) I may need a wider size (I have REALLY wide feet and heels. The skates are a C width but I think I should have gotten a D).
5) I am progressing really quickly even from the time I bought these so I am worried they are not going to last me long at all.

I am taking the skates to another skate tech today to figure out my options but here is my question.
Should I have bought a different skate to start out with? Should I have gotten the boot and blade separately? If it is going to cost me quite a bit more money to get these skates to work with my feet am I better off selling them to one of the other skaters at the rink and getting myself a better pair from a more qualified fitter? I am so confused!! I do not want to spend more money fixing skates that are not going to last me more than another 8 months! Please help.

Thank you!

It looks like those boots aren't going to work out for you unfortunately. Depending on what the fitter says, you are more than likely going to need a split width boot which is not uncommon, and that particular set up you have does not come that way. But you are going to have to do something because you are not supposed to have your feet going numb or feeling cramped in the boot while you are skating (your circulation etc is getting cut off). Also, the quality of the blade concerns me (with you saying it's breaking down already).

Your best bet if you really do like Jackson's is to go with a Premiere boot (takes you up through doubles and also comes in split width/wides) and a Wilson Coronation Ace Blade or an MK Vision, or Professional blade.
Or if you are desperate for the least possible you could go with a Debut boot (it comes in split width and wide just be aware it only takes you up through single axel) and the same blade (one or the other or at least a Double Star blade) because you can always move the blade to a higher boot if it's still in good shape later on and fits.

As for the boots you currently have: Depending on how used they are you might see if they would take them and give you money towards your new boot or blades since it was fitter error (I hope you aren't going to the same fitter that messed up the first time around), or see if you could sell them to another skater at your rink.

Good luck!

It sounds like it's just a bad fit, and they're overall too narrow for you. I wouldn't completely blame the fitter if you said you loved them in the store. They cannot do anything to help you if you don't present your issues as you're trying on the boot. However, their mistake was that they should have asked you how secure your heel was, since that is the number one most important thing with fit with skating boots.

The blades thing is also concerning - with one sharpening, you shouldn't be noticing any change in the feel or appearance that would lead to a feeling that the blade life is super short. Those Aspire blades are not the highest quality, but they're not as bad as that. When you do get better blades, definitely don't take them back to that sharpener - A bad sharpener can trash a pair of 500$ blades in one sharpening.

If you want to stick with those boots, probably the only thing you can do is to get them punched out aggressively and re-heat molded (did they do this for you at the beginning?) That does not cost a lot of money to get done. But it might not be enough to fix your problems.

Adult skater here, I used to skate in Jackson Freestyle with premounted Aspire blades, recommended by a former coach. I empathize with your problems because I had similar issues. In my case, the size was too short but also too wide, a problem with the fitter at the shop. Additionally, the arches of the Jackson sole hurt my feet so I had to swap them out with an old pair of soles and that helped ease some discomfort. My heel would also slip up and down during jumps (not good!) but my toes would go numb. New coach recommended I bite the bullet and just pay to get fitted for custom skates as my feet have stopped growing so they should last a long time. Turns out I needed a split width for the heels and balls of my feet. I also made the mistake of getting my skates sharpened at the rink shop and an inexperienced tech took the rocker off of one blade. I took them elsewhere to get "fixed" but they were only able to flatten the other side to match. The blades were already flat to begin with and now they would scrape the toe picks. I scratched my toe picks going backwards and turns were so frustrating. I switched to CorAce on my new skates and they made gliding and turning so effortless. I highly recommend getting out of the Aspires and upgrading your blades no matter what boot you choose. Good luck!

Umm. I'm a little surprised to hear that your skates went from feeling comfortable in the shop to being numbingly tight once you got out on the ice. I'm also confused because you haven't mentioned going back to the place where you originally bought them once you started getting numbness - because that's the very first thing I would have done, and I wouldn't have waited two months. A reputable fitter will try to make things right by adjusting the skates or exchanging them if you were really misfit. But if you didn't explain the problems, how would they know? Skates - even custom skates - don't necessarily fit perfectly the day you bring them home.

By the way, you said your heels don't sit all the way back in the skate. Do you kick them back before tying, and are you lacing them properly at the ankle to keep your foot from sliding forward? Can you wear thinner socks? If you are and you still can't get your heels all the way back, it might possibly be that the boot heels are too narrow, not too wide, and actually need to be stretched a bit so your heel can slide back into the heel cup. In terms of gaining more space in the rest of the boot, aside from wearing thinner (e.g., Mondor) socks and having the boots punched out, have you tried removing the insole and using a thinner one?

Finally, FYI, since the Freestyle has an LCL sole, if you can get the boot to fit comfortably, you can always replace the blade with a higher level one.

Make sure you are NOT leaving your skates in a hot car/trunk either. It will cause the skates to lose the molding to some degree if not all.
We try to put the skates in a cooler with some ice if it's more than an hour that we are travelling when it's warm/hot out.

How soon did you skate after the skates were heat-molded? Generally the recommendation is that you NOT skate in hot/warm skates but to let them cooldown first as it will cause the skates to breakdown much faster.

Are you wearing the same type of socks each and every time you are skating/had them heatmolded? This will make a difference too.

And did you have them heatmolded at beginning or end of the day vs when you went skating the most recent time? (your feet changes slightly through the day)

And you've said they checked alignment -is that with your feet in them and walking around to ensure it's correct alignment to your body vs standard mount alignment? But it doesn't sound like an issue since after 2nd time you could skate without issues.

For the first couple months with my current pair of skates, aside from stretching and punching, I did the following each time I skated:

4. Take warmers out of skates
5. Immediately put on skates
6. Skate and have fun

At this point, I think you need to do two things: (1) have a fitter you trust assess the shape of your feet, recommend a brand, and then measure your feet; and (2) think about why your feet go numb when you skate, even though the fit seems okay to you initially. I think understanding that is really the key, because you've now had the same problem with two different fitters.

Some ideas: Are you being fitted for your boots at the beginning or end of the day? Since our feet swell as the day goes on and swell even more with exercise, this could be the problem. Another thought: when you're being fitted, are you wearing the skates for a long enough time and moving around enough to really gauge their comfort? If your feet go numb after 15 minutes of skating, for example, you need to wear them at least that long after they've been heat molded, and you should be standing and walking around in them. You should test them again after the blades are mounted, since this will change how they feel to you. Finally, do you know what a properly fitted skate should feel like? Your fitter should guide you in this by asking you specific questions, such as whether your foot is lying completely flat in the skate, whether your toes feel crammed in, etc. But part of it depends on you. Since a lot of us habitually wear shoes that are a little uncomfortable because of the style (stiletto heels, pointy toes, etc.), we may not have a good sense of what proper fit feels like. If the boots feel a little tight in the shop, chances are theylll feel worse on the ice.
