The debate over form vs function in figure skating boots (and footwear in general) has gone on for ages. I still remember a discussion I had in the late 1980's on this very subject with a well-known fitter in my area. He told me that a French company had introduced a line of figure skating boots with a square toebox. They didn't sell because the skaters didn't like the looks. They were discontinued within a year. Jacksons have rounder toeboxes than many other boots. When I was first looking into them, several fitters commented that many skaters didn't like them because they looked "klunky". One fitter (who was also an experienced ice-dance coach) agreed with that characterization.

But maybe there's hope. Look at the Altra running shoes. They stress function over form. In particular, they have an asymmetric, squarish toebox. They seem to have a large enough following to stay in business. Not sure whether figure skaters will ever come round to such an aesthetic, though. But I would love to have a pair of figure skating boots with such a toebox.

Revs are a little bit lighter, but mostly because the blade is thinner. The cushioning effect is due to the design and how the blade is attached itself, so there's some flex to them, but being thinner they also tend to come warped. Balance-wise I would argue that a stiff blade is better, but I don't think the difference is that meaningful.

So, if you don't mind the thinner runner, kind of like a dance blade, and you don't mind changing the way you grab them in some catch moves, then you can go for the Revs, but regular ones are just fine. Just make sure to check the blade straightness well, if you do go for the Revs.
