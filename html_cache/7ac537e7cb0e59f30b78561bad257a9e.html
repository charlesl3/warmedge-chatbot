You are too old. A woman's athletic peak lasts from 16-20, generally speaking - so you are about to leave the athletic peak. It can take ten years or more of work to reach the Senior level. Some very talented skaters can make it in less time, but they are rarer. Even great skaters like Evgeni Plushenko took eight years to reach Senior level. Even if you managed that, by then you would be 28. Your body would be starting to protest rather vehemently.

Second, I think you are vastly underestimating the difficulty of what you want to achieve. There are many skaters out there who never land a clean Axel, let alone progress to doubles; and even more who, even if they get past that hurdle, never land a double Axel, even with years, and years, and years of work.

You also greatly underestimate the cost of such training. We are talking many tens of thousands of dollars every year for that sort of training at the Senior level. It's not cheap.

Your gymnastics background means very little, I'm afraid.

What do you mean by "skate competitively"?

If you're talking about adult competitions, absolutely.

It looks like you're in the US, so if you wanted to skate in standard competitions, you would not be able to compete in standard-track qualifying competitions (regionals) or nonqualifying events at the juvenile or intermediate levels. If you were able to pass the first four levels of Moves in the Field and freestyle tests (up to juvenile, which requires an axel) and get some double jumps, then there is a competition level called "Open Juvenile" that you would be able to enter and compete against teenagers.

The comparable level in adult competition is Adult Gold, which also involves four adult tests, only allows easier doubles, but many skaters at that level especially the older ones do not attempt doubles at all. If you reach that skill level and find you're better at double loops, flips, and/or lutzes, you might want to go the Open Juvenile route.

If you passed the Intermediate freestyle test, you would not be allowed to compete Open Juvenile, but you would not be allowed to compete standard Intermediate either (age limit is under 18). So then your only option would be the Adult Intermediate/Novice competition, which allows doubles up to 2Lo.

You'd probably have to take lessons and practice most days a week for several years to get that far. The above
would give you a realistic idea of what it might be possible for you to achieve if you can put in, let's say, 10 hours a week on ice, with at least an hour of lesson time, for 5 years. Your relative youth and gymnastics background would probably help, but there's no guarantee.

If finances, distance from a rink, college and/or job constraints, spending time with family/friends/romantic partner, etc., would limit you to skating no more than two or three days a week for one or two hours, then getting even that far would be unlikely. (And the same would be true for a 9- or 10-year-old, especially if they hit an early growth spurt.)

If axels and double jumps, and mid-level Moves in the Field prove to be a challenge for you, then you might max out at Adult Silver, which is normal for adults who start later.

On the other hand, if you put in even more time on the ice and work very hard and are lucky enough to have the right kind of talent, it might be possible for you pass the novice tests (the Moves in the Field tests get quite challenging at that level, and the freestyle requires good doubles and double-double combination), which would allow you to compete on the standard track including at regionals. It's highly unlikely you would be able to achieve that, but it is theoretically within the realm of possibility, so it could be something for you to aspire to with the understanding that you might not achieve it.

Getting to the point that you can pass through the senior tests with senior-quality skating skills and all the double jumps clean and consistent solo and in combination is much less likely. Double axels and triple jumps even less so. And again, even a kid who starts at age 5-7 and puts in 10-12 years of 10 or 20 hours a week on ice may never get there.

The trick is to have a realistic understanding of what you might achieve given your talents and resources and amount of time you can devote to the sport -- including realistic top aspirations to aim for but recognize you might not achieve.

If you start skating and enjoy the process at the lower and if possible middle levels, skating can be a very rewarding hobby. If anything less than senior or even novice level would feel like a failure, then you'd be in it for the wrong reasons.

And I just want to say I was one of those skaters that started late, I didn't start learning spins and the jumps until I was 18, I was VERY EMBARRASSED to skate with elite Senior skaters. It was impossibly EMBARRASSING (sometimes I still feel embarrassed lol). The funny thing with figure skating is you have to be absolutely crazy about it because nothing is guaranteed. It's thrilling and scary, and you learn a lot about your inner demons and weaknesses. I think the parent of an actual Olympian called skating "a sport of failure" or something like that. You have to love it so much that you laugh when you fall and get inspired when you don't succeed. It's an obsession, you have goals, sometimes you reach them, many times not but---if you love it, so what! DO IT and be embarrassed!!! If you have a passion to skate, don't let thoughts of immense success nor failure stop you. Go ahead and do it.

I started late and it took 10 yrs. to learn, relearn, and fix my doubles. And two years from there to get 2axel. And sometimes on a Tuesday in the middle of the year on a lucky day when I've got the camera on, I rotate a two- footed triple. My goal right now is to pass all of my tests. The last one is Senior free skate, which I've failed twice! Embarrassing! No other adults out there with me. But. I have to do it or die trying. Why? Probably I have a problem &#55357;&#56842;
