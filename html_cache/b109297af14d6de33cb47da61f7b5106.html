I'm not an expert, but for me personally I would get a cheap pair of skates that fit properly and don't cause pain.

When I first came back to skating after a long break I tried to use my old skates. The problem was that my feet had grown just ever so slightly after I got the skates, and my feet were a bit too wide. I tried getting them stretched out, but it didn't seem to fully take away the pain. Every session I was struggling with pain and it was ruining my enjoyment of skating. Also I wasn't putting as much pressure on my outside edges because the sides of my feet hurt so bad I didn't want to feel the extra pain. As a result my outside edges were not as deep and secure as they should have been simply because I had so much pain whenever I tried to really press into them. I was basically a rat getting shock treatment whenever I pressed for outside edges lol.

I now am absolutely pain free with my new skates and I will never again suffer through incorrectly fitted skates. From the moment I put them on to 4+ hours later they feel basically the same, so I would try not to settle for any skating boot that causes you long term pain past the break-in period. I think skating is hard enough without having to deal with extra unnecessary foot pain.

Where do your feet hurt? If it's pinching, particularly around the toes or heel, then yes they don't fit and you should find something else - if they are fitting badly, then as figureskaterdude said, you will end up over/undercompensating when your feet are in certain positions, and will develop bad habits as a consequence.

If it's an ache in the arches, then it might be that your feet are still getting used to the stiffness of the skates, particularly if they weren't used all that heavily before you bought them. Skates don't fit like street shoes, and your feet/ankles/legs are performing very different actions from your run-of-the-mill everyday movements and most other types of exercise and using different muscles. Even in boots with a perfect fit, it takes a while for you to get used to them - I'll get cramps after 20 minutes or so the first time on the ice after a few weeks off despite my skates fitting well & being completely broken in.

If it is the aching type of pain, I would recommend stretching out your feet before putting the boots on, and then skating for short periods - say 15 minutes, then unlace, stretch feet out again, do another 15 mins, repeat. You should find after a few sessions that you are much more comfortable.

Break-in period varies based on the brand of skate and its stiffness rating. Skates that are built for skaters doing triple jumps will be harder to break in than beginner skates.

Speaking only from personal experience, I needed typically around 10-15 hours of skate time for my feet to feel comfortable and adjusted to the skates.

If you don't have any sharp pain in any specific area then it may be your feet just getting adjusted to wearing skates in general like others have suggested. I know when I switched from the SP teri brand to Edeas, my feet had a general cramping to them for about 6-10 hours of skate time as my feet just got adjusted to being in a new type of boot all together. After that they were completely fine though.

Have you tried putting some little arch supports in them? Lack of arch support may cause your feet to cramp. Another common cause of foot muscle cramping is the boots being a little narrow in the widest part of the foot.

Also, you didn't say what level skater you are and what brand and model of boots these are. If they are too stiff for your level of skating (i.e., they are a more advanced boot and you are a beginner), it might be best to buy a pair of less stiff boots.

My old Reidells were too narrow at the forefoot, and my feet cramped on and off for the entire life of those skates. My Harlicks, I was able to buy in a combo width, so the forefoot is wider, and what a difference. No achy foot crampies. I'm sure the heat molding (which the Reidells, at the time, didn't have) also helped me.

Also, watch out how you're lacing your skates. If you lace them too tight across the foot part, you can cause aching and cramping.

Second opinion on blame the skates. If you are a beginner buying used skates, make sure you aren't buying recreational skates that aren't really suitable for figure skating. These charts are a good place to start, for what brands and models real figure skates come in, and what levels they correspond with.

https://www.kinziescloset.com/skate-comparison-guide.html

If you are an adult and relatively serious about skating, I would personally recommend to get a boot a couple levels (but not too far) beyond what you're working on initially, because first of all, we are heavier than kids and require a stronger boot, and there's very little risk that our feet grow, and ideally you want to get at LEAST a year or 2 out of your boots. I see a lot of adult beginners starting in Jackson Freestyles, Elle, or Reidell Diamonds
