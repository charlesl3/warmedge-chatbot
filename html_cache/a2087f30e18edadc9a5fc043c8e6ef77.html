I do think marketing plays a huge role in them being super-popular. But you have to remember that Jackson re-designed all of their skates to be more like Edea. Edea has a better design for a lot of skaters, and Jackson noticed and tried to copy it.

I don't think they're as good for ice dance or more of the ice dancers would be in the flamenco, but Edea is supposed to be superior for jumps in freestyle. And the lighter weight and shorter break-in time makes them very appealing as well.

I switched to Edea because it's what the top skaters use. But if it really hadn't worked out for me, I would have gone back to Jackson. I started out in Ice Fly and am in Pianos now. I haven't been injured at all in either of them. I did have a transition period of a couple months between old school Jackson and Edea where my ankles had to get stronger to be able to succeed in Edea. Once I was past that, they've been great!

kolyadafan2002 said:
Which Jackson did you have? Since Jackson Elites (the older and newer version) from what I remember actually had higher ankle than Ice flies (they are equivalent skates). Debuts might have smaller heel but maybe I'm just misremembering.
Click to expand...

I had the Jackson Freestyles that were made 17 years ago when I skated for one year at age 14. They were still usable when I started skating again at age 26. I used them for about a year or so before they were definitely dead when I started working on axel. Switched to Ice Fly at that point. Jackson only saw about 3 years of skating max, but they did sit around in a closet for 14 years in between year 1 and 2.

My body weight and height at age 14 and when I started again at 26 were exactly the same. I was 5'9 tall by the time I was age 14. So that wouldn't have made any difference to how fast the skate was breaking down.

Jackson certainly secured the ankle well enough. So when I switched to Ice Fly, it felt like I had NO ankle support, so it took a couple months to build up the strength and feel normal again. In the beginning, just doing 3turns felt kinda dangerous because of the severe difference in ankle support.
And the weight of the boot was insanely different. Jackson Freestyle was real leather and extremely heavy. Ice Fly felt like I was barely wearing anything. I could suddenly do sit spins with ease because I could hold my leg up and it took awhile to adjust my camel because I kept overshooting the leg because I was so used to hauling up real leather into the air.
I did actually weigh the boots and I think it was only 0.5 difference on each foot, but it felt like I had dropped 4lbs from each foot.

When I switched to Piano about a year ago, after 4 years in Ice Fly (could have probably squeezed 2 more years out of the boots before really needing to switch), I weighed them again. The boot weight was the same, but the blades I switched to gained me 0.1 weight. Well, I could feel even that weight difference. It felt like I had gained about 1lb on each foot, so I had to work a little harder in my spins (sit and camel) at first until I got used to it.
I would feel a difference BEFORE weighing. The weighing was just to see what the actual difference was, instead of just going by feeling.

I fully believe the boots break down quickly under those who are doing triples. But for skaters doing doubles and singles (my rink is full of these types), the boots seem to last 4-5 years at least, sometimes longer. Anyone older who's foot has stopped growing and is at least working on axel and doubles is usually in Ice Fly or Piano. Anyone who's feet are still growing is in Chorus (if they're working on doubles) or Overtures to save money. They tend to outgrow their boots long before they wear out. But for the few adults (doing jumps lower than axel) who are in Overture or Chorus, they seem to wear those boots out in 2 years or less. Which is why they all jump to Ice Fly so quickly, especially at axel level. So it makes sense that those doing triples would kill Ice Fly quickly. But obviously it works for elite skaters since they have sponsors to replace the boots as often as needed.

Don't remember where I read it, but some post I saw long ago was about Gracie Gold or someone else admitting they replace their Edea skates every 3-6 months or something crazy like that. They don't last even a full year. So sponsors are critical to pay for that. Either no other brand is willing to give them free skates or the elites really believe Edea is superior to make it worth so many replacements.

It's more Edea are willing to sponsor loads of skaters towards the start of their career (before becoming famous), and when they do become famous they are used to edea already and don't switch.
They might sponsor a top 3 skater at novice level or lower, and by the time they are in seniors they are still using them whereas Jackson only tend to sponsor successful junior and senior skaters (and some well known adult skaters).

Gracie is lucky if edeas last three months for her. I heard (don't quote me for this) some people need 8-10pairs of ice fly/piano to get through a season without boot issues. For me, if I stuck to edea I'd need 4 pairs of boots a year to be able to not have boot issues, and even then I'd need extra tongues .

Of course, skater weight and technique vary and that will effect lifespan, but for me edea make boots too cheaply, and are missing QC. And ice fly material doesn't last as long as the rest of their line.

In addition to other features mentioned in this thread, I've often wondered if Edea's "Instant Custom fit" is part of the reason so many elite skaters wear them. I'd imagine that if you don't have any really specific or out-of-the-ordinary fit needs, it's probably much easier and faster to just get another pair of stock Edeas and have them heat-molded by a tech than to get a true custom. Especially with supply chain issues being what they've been the last couple years.

Like others have mentioned, the loose ankle fit was an immediate deal-breaker for me the one time I tried on the Ice Fly - but it's fair to assume that elite skaters have much stronger ankles than I do!
