Little figure skating girl in tears

Joined

I was in a public session at a rink and saw a little girl (probably about 5) initially practising by herself and then her mum came on (in hockey boots) and she started bullying her and telling her she wasn't good enough and not trying hard enough (from what I heard, I was mostly concentrating on my practise) but the girl started crying and the mum wouldn't stop. She just had this really mean streak about her. I didn't say anything but not sure if I can or supposed to? I wanted to get some advice.

I've seen plenty of kids with mums on the rinkside but this was a bit confronting because 1. she was crying 2. the mum was literally there on the rink. 3. it's so out in the open.

The fact is, the mum has absolutelly no idea how hard it is to do figure skating (at any level). I wanted to say b!tch, why don't you take off those hockey boots, put a figure boot on and show me a jump or spin instead of bullying your poor daughter.

Let me know what you guys think.

Your heart is in the right place but criticizing others for their parenting style rarely turns out well especially if you they're complete strangers to you. As Loopy pointed out, you could have gone to a rink manager or maybe a coach to ask them to intervene. They have the authority to tell mom she's being too much and making a scene. You don't.

The only other option -- which I do not recommend and which requires that you appear to be completely socially inept -- would be to try and defuse the situation by skating up to the pair and as brightly as possible giving the 5 year old a compliment about how focused she seemed to be or her work ethic or or her skating skills, and then complimenting the mom on how her daughter is impressive, and then skating away. It would have to seem as if you've completely missed the fact that she's yelling at her daughter. There is also a very high possibility she'll start yelling at you, in which case get the manager.

Confronting the parent rarely works out well and may in fact make the parent's behavior towards their child worse. The best thing you can do is contact the rink manager who has more authority in diffusing the situation. If you ever see behavior that is truly abusive contact the proper authorities (in the US that CPS). However be very careful when making this decision and be sure you understand the situation prior.

I worked for a girl scout camp for 6 years and have no patience for abusive (verbally or otherwise) parents. However that doesn't mean storming up to the parent is a helpful course of action.

I work in an ice rink. Most of the time when a kid skating at the freestyle level is crying, it is because the parent set limits. Parents sometimes handle it very well. But sometimes they let the child be more like the parent, and other times they get frustrated and express themselves to the kids in ways that reflect their frustration. In most environments, I take the side of the child, but in the rink in this area, with all the wealthy spoiled children and over-babying parents, it is almost always the parents who are the ones being treated unfairly (hockey parents are a whole different ball of wax).

We can't possibly know everything that happened up to the moment of observation, and not even during it. People communicate in ways other than words, and often people who know each other well (like families) know each other's thoughts or intentions just through non-verbal expression. Anything could have been happening in the communication that a casual observer would not have known was happening, even right in front of the observer.

When it comes to public observations of people we don't really know, unless the child is in physical danger, it is best to let it go, hard as that may be. This is coming from someone (me) who studied the social work specialty of children and families.
