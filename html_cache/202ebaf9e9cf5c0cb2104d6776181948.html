We've discussed ROH in several previous threads (enter "ROH" in the Search function, and limit the search to "The Lutz Corner"). Here's one previous general overview I gave:

https://www.goldenskate.com/forum/t...-of-hollow-affect-skating.81787/#post-2429477 Reply #5

Since you mentioned you went to a different sharpener, here are additional comments:

* With most (not all) commercial sharpening machines, the ROH is varied by dressing the grinding wheel. In practice, the actual ROH you get will depend on the calibration of the dresser, and how carefully the tech dresses the wheel (and how often he redresses the wheel if the ROH stays the same for multiple pairs of skates). There are radius gauges for verifying the ROH you actually get. In other words, if you specify an ROH of 7/16", what you get from Tech A is not necessarily the same as what you get from Tech B. And even for the same tech, what you get is not necessarily consistent from one sharpening to another.

* Ideally, the sharpness of the edges is set by the ROH, which can be accurately controlled if done right. In practice, after a machine sharpening, the tech hones the sides of the blade to remove burrs resulting from the grinding operation. This honing operation is done by hand. Even if the ROH is accurately controlled, the actual sharpness of the edges is strongly dependent on the manual honing operation (e.g., type of hone, angle at which the tech holds the hone, how steady the tech holds the hone, ....). So, if you want to compare the effects of different ROHs, you want to go to a single competent tech who produces consistent results.

If you are skating on a regular basis and if you have your own skates, you should know which ROH are under your blades.
First of all, when your skates needs sharpening, you can ask it should be done with that particular ROH.
If the tech is using another ROH your skating will feel awkward.
Secondly, when your skill improves, the ROH can be fine tuned according to your style and body weight.
This will bring you to a higher level.
Just keep in mind that the common ROH's only counts if you perform jumps or if you are a hockey player.
For recreational skating (even at a high skill level) with figure or hockey skates, you're much better of with a radius of hollow above 1".

60 years of skating experience, started at the age of 5.
Using my own sharpening machine and trying out all kinds of ROH's.
These are my findings :

A deeper hollow will cause the skate blade edges to penetrate deeper into the ice resulting in greater gliding resistance. This means that a deeper cut feels slower to the skater when skating in a straight line.

Shallower hollows produce faster speeds but don't grip the ice as well as their edge bite angle is not as direct into the ice.

These are 2 facts that experienced skaters know already.

But, the more deeper the hollow, the more the skates are taking control of the skater !


The recommended ROH are all meant for hockey games and figure skaters who are also performing jumps.

They need a certain deep hollow in order not to skid too much.

This does not apply for recreational skaters ! They want to enjoy the gliding and are much better off with a shallow hollow.

After some practice they are able to stop and turn when and where they want and also are able to perform a large variety of moves.

But there are more advantages regarding a shallow hollow :

  • more maneuverable
  • less drag
  • more effortless performance
  • less stress on the knees and other joints
  • less fatigue
  • greater forgiveness on rough ice
These facts are very important when a person becomes older and the joints starts to become more painful.

During my early years I was skating with the common ROH of 13 and 19 mm.

In 2009 my ROH became 25 mm.

In 2022 I started to experiment with the higher ROH, each time increasing : 32, 34, 38, and finally 45mm. The latter is, in a practical way, the highest ROH possible on my SSM skate sharpener.

Now I’m using ROH 45 mm all the time, the effortless freedom of movement is significant.
