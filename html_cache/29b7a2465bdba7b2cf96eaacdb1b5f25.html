I've competed at National Showcase before, and there are lots of adults who do. If it's an affordable trip for you, do it! The spotlights can be disconcerting, though, so be prepared to have a bright light in your eyes and have no idea where you are on the ice, LOL! You'll also need to adjust your program layout to fit a shortened rink (since they have a curtain or boards at one end of the rink that cuts through the two hockey circles at that end).

I'm an adult and have competed at National Showcase as well. It is a major, legit competition.

Like vlaurend said, the spotlights can be disconcerting. I found them much brighter and the rink much darker than any spotlight I'd skated in for ice shows. I couldn't tell where the judges were at all, and I was skating in my home rink!

Also, my artistic program, which was received well at regular competitions (several medals), was a flop at National Showcase. My perception of National Showcase is that they are looking for a certain type of program. I came in dead last skating to Les Mis and was beaten out by a gal in a full bird costume and a guy with a ribbon, among others. If I were ever to compete at NS again, I would have to completely rethink the program I was putting out there.

Yes, there was a spotlight practice at NS available for a fee. At the NS I skated at, I think it was $25. As freaked out as I was after the spotlight practice, I actually pulled myself together, big time, during the competition, and skated my program the best I'd ever skated it. I think that's why I was extra disappointed I came in last in my group, but to this day I'm proud of having pulled it together and performed well. I would highly recommend doing the spotlight practice, especially if you haven't skated under spotlights before.

Google National Showcase Adult Skating to get an idea of some of the programs presented there. That being said, just go out and skate and have fun, and don't worry about the placement. It's a challenge to compete in a competition like this one, and it's rewarding. In the non-adult category the year I competed, a Les Mis program won the whole thing, including best of show, so my notion that they are looking for extreme programs might be completely off-base. Maybe others can chime in. At any rate, I say go for it, and have a great time!

In terms of judging, any kind of non-techanical/showcase program is always a crapshoot. You'll never have the same judges someone else had, so you can't always apply anyone else's experience of the judging. But the bottom line is that what is rewarded is the skater's ability to express a theme, story or character through her skating, with technical content being less important than skating a clean, well-presented program. Do it for yourself and for what is hopefully a video you'll be proud to show your grandkids someday. Don't do it for some anticipated placement.
