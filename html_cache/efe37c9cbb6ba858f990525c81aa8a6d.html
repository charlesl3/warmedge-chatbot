I started taking figure skating lesson May 3rd of this year. I have already learned the waltz jump and I'm going to be started single jumps and getting coaching soon so I was wondering what skates and blades I should get next. I currently have Riedell 2015 Model 110 Opal skates and the Riedell Spiral blades that came with them. They're for recreational skating and don't have a lot of ankle support. I was wondering if I should get Edea Overture skates, which are for single and axel jumps, and Coronation Ace blades which are the lowest John Wilson blades. My friend suggested that I shouldn't get them because she doesn't want me to waste my money, even though she has Riedell 910 Flair skates with Eclipse Mist blades. People have suggested that I get a blade with a 7ft rocker which is why I'm considering the Coronation Ace blade of the Coronation Ace Revs. My other skating friends suggested the Eclipse Aurora instead but the Coronation Ace Revs are for single and doubles just the the Aurora blades and they'd be $10 cheaper if I were to get the Revs. What do you think?

The Coronation Ace blade should be good for you, but if it was me, I'd go with a Coronation Comet, yes it has an 8.5 ft rocker but they will last you up through your skills better as long as you take good care of them, and if you plan on advancing to a higher JW blade in the future it will be easier to adapt to the 8ft rocker on the Gold Seal or Pattern 99's. If you really do want a 7 ft rocker like on the Coronation Ace, then you would have a harder time adapting and your best choices for higher blades would force you to convert over to MK's which all have 7ft rockers. So just something to think about.

As far as boots you are making a bigger change in the feel of boots going from a Riedell to a Edea. My first question for you is: Why an Edea? If you definitely like the Edea after getting a fitting and want one, I'd be looking at the Chorus over the Overture since it will take you up through doubles.
Consider also looking into other brands of boots. I've worn Riedell, Risport, and Jackson over my career on the ice. It's hard to tell you what might work for you since we don't know what your feet are like: Narrow, Wide, etc....

Lastly, don't worry about your friend and what she's saying or her abilities. This is about you and you need to get what works for you.

Good luck!

Ic3Rabbit and Karne are exactly right. Getting boots rated for triples would be overbooting. If you are 16 it's unlikely boots rates for doubles would be too much. There is a lot to learn in the levels you are starting and it would be easier for you to not have to adjust to new boots in another year.

Different boots fit differently. For example- I have always skated in Reidell skates but my new skates are Jacksons. My feet have changed as I've gotten older and Reidell skates are too narrow in the toe box (thanks bunions!). Long story short- skates are expensive and it would likely be worth it to go a little stiffer so they will last you longer. Not having to adjust to new skates again in a year would help you in the long one.

I understand your concern with not wanting to overboot but there is a big difference between getting boots that are a little stiffer and getting Ice Flys.

Remember: There is no industry standard for boot stiffness ratings. An Edea boot rated 50 is not necessarily the same stiffness as a Riedell boot rated 50. Even within a single manufacturer's line, I've not been able to get any info on the stiffness scale; that is, whether a 50 is twice as stiff as a 25. So you can't blindly go by these numbers.

I think Coronation Ace would be a good blade for you, until you figure out where you could use the most extra help (general consensus is that 7ft rockers make spins easier, and that 8 ft rockers make landing jumps easier). If you take care of them and have a good sharpener, you could probably use them on a second pair of skates when the ones you are buying need replacement. Regarding the boots, if you/mom can afford to pay for new boots more frequently, it is fine to go with what you already have decided.
