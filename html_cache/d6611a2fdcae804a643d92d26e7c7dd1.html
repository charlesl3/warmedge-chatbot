Hi !

We are planning to do a figure skating show with my club, and we were thinking to ask novice girls to wear make up (red/dark lipstick and eye shadow to be compatible with the theme). In my opinion, because the audience will be far, the skaters will move fast, plus the ice is reflecting the light, it has to be a little excessive. But some of the girls are 7/8 years old, and we are afraid to shock parents cause normally little girls are not supposed to wear makeup...

How do you feel about this ? Would you be shocked if a girl of 8 yo wear red lipstick and eye liner for a show or a competition ?

Thanks a lot !

One of the most horrifying things I've ever seen in person at an ice rink was an eight year old whose makeup looked like she should have been walking the streets in the red light district.

If there will be no spotlights, you actually don't need a lot of makeup. Before I was a skate mom....I would have said "no make-up, no way." Our coach had to twist my arm (and act quickly) to add a bit of blush to my daughter's cheeks for her first competition. The claim was that their faces look washed out with all the white ice in the pictures. Frankly I think it's completely irrelevant when they are so young...but it was true that she looked what you might call "healthier" in skating pics with the blush than without. My daughter LOVES make-up. I don't allow it for anything but fun dress-up or skating competitions but I have relented more than I thought I would to let her wear eye shadow, lipstick AND blush for shows and competitions. (She is 8 now). But I do shudder when I see the amount plastered on some girls' faces, especially the mascara. And I really don't want my daughter to get the message that she is any less beautiful or any less of an athlete without makeup. But I do get that for shows it's also a "costume" and not necessarily real life. Re-reading my post I can see I'm very conflicted/contradictory on this subject - which pretty much does describe it! It'll depend on the lighting and needs at your individual rink. I was a dance kid and I was wearing makeup at five and six that's far heavier than I wear on a night out as an adult, because our stage lights required it. It was very obviously stage makeup (I looked totally ridiculous in person/close up!) and totally appropriate... for the stage. Performance etiquette is a different beast. But if you don't have any special lights, I doubt you need much. Lights and distance are the deciding factors. Something to make the eyes pop will probably be helpful if the audience is far away. You don't have to make up a kid like a streetwalker to make them visible from a distance. Again, it'll really just be up to the individual needs of the space. I think nysk8temom above has a good point. If it's a concern, make sure your child(ren) know it's not about making them prettier, it's about functionality--an average person just can't see your face very well from 100 feet away with no makeup. It's that simple.
