Was her test marked "retry" by the judge(s)? If so then she can't retake the test prior to 27 days following the date of the original test. It's in the USFS rulebook. Also, don't try to test without your coach present, please. BTW, you never specified what kind of test it was other than novice and that makes a difference.

A. You have to wait a month before you're allowed to try to pass again, no matter where you test at.
B. Testing without your coach present is terrifying and increases the chance of failure. Your coach "knows" you. They can remind you on test day (as they watch you on warmup and see what mistakes you're making on the day) about the little details you're probably still trying to correct or not forget to do. Another coach doesn't know you, doesn't know how you've been taught, and may have completely different ideas about what's wrong and what's not.
C. Be ready for judges that think completely different things at a different rink (this can help you pass or make it harder to pass, just depends). There's a ton of strength in testing in your home rink or just a rink you use consistently for most of your tests because they tend to use the same judges over time and you learn what those judges want. Just like coaches, every judge has their own opinion on what they want to see.

Failing by such a narrow margin sounds like she will be passing by the skin of her teeth and it would be much better to have her coach present if at all possible.
Testing without a coach really means you're so confident and above the skill level required for the test that you don't need a coach's help. AKA Michelle Kwan isn't going to struggle to pass a Senior FS test because she's already got a ton of triple jumps and Senior FS test only requires double jumps. So it's easy for her to go pass a Senior FS test without a coach. She's far above the required level of skill.

My mother is the test chair at my rink and she said you don't have to have a coach to test. Additionally, I've tested and competed about one quarter of the time without my coach (I'm switching coaches soon so this will not be the case in the future). I agree with Arwen17, she's already going to barely pass. Best to have a coach present.

However, if this basically is a necessity, have her write down all the corrections that she gets consistently in a notebook. Refer to it religiously. Bring a bag to keep a water bottle and jacket in. She won't have anyone to hold it.

Every skater is different. My daughter has gone to a bunch of moves, free skate, and solo dance test sessions without a coach. We were initially hesitant to go without a coach, but the coach was ok with it and my daughter gained self-confidence each time. This eventually gave us the freedom to go to test sessions at convenient schedules for us (some were 2-hr drive away). Of the test sessions she attended without a coach there, she passed all but one (over 90% passing rate).

llouise88, I wish the best to your daughter.
