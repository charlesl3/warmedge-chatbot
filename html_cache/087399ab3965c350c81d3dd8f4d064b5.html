gkelly said:
...Skating technique is all about using centrifugal (and centripetal) forces….
Click to expand...

:yes: In terms of physics, there are only two forces in play: gravity and the ice pushing against the skater's blade. If the shape and angle of the blade constrains the skater to move in a circle, then the force exerted by the ice has a component in the direction of the center of the circle (centripetal force) and a vertical component that counters gravity and keeps the skater from falling down. The smaller the radius of the circle (assuming constant speed in the tangential direction), the stronger the centripetal force. (Outside of a physics class, the situation is complicated by the fact that a skater's body is not perfectly rigid.)

The other question is, how is figure skating possible in the first place. Heat generated by friction which melts the ice a bit as you glide along has long been the starting point for the answer, but it does not seem complete. Witcher's question about why and how deeply the blade actually penetrates into the ice is a lot more complicated, I believe, than just having a sharp blade. Sometimes the ice is too "hard," perhaps from being the wrong temperature but more likely from not having the proper mix of chemical impurities in the water from which the ice was made. I think ice-making is quite a subtle art, actually.
