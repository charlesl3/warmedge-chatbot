I wouldn't recommend jeans, if they get wet they can take a while to dry. I'd go with leggings, sweats, or joggers for pants. For your tops, it's better to layer if you don't know how cold it'll be. If you are usually hot, you can wear a tank top, a t-shirt, and a sweater on top, so you can take off a layer if you want to.

I suggest some type of stretchy pants. If you don't wear leggings, then some type of yoga pant. If you don't have these type of bottoms, then wear jeans this time, but buy athletic pants for the future sessions.
Wear layers on top as jf12 suggested. While moving around you may actually get quite warm!

Don't encumber yourself in a bulky jacket and don't tangle yourself up in a long skirt.

Most of all - have fun, and congrats on taking that first skating step!!

Edited to add: Don't compare yourself to the others in your class either. It's class #1 and everyone will progress at their own pace.

Yes, stretchy stuff you can move around in easily, no baggy bottoms that can get caught on anything, and layers. For Detroit, IIRC, you went to a competition there this winter, right? Yeah, if you were walking around Detroit in the winter in leggings, I would not recommend sweats, and you should make the bottom layer a tank top or short-sleeved shirt.

Also, keep your leggings/pants over your skates instead of laced up inside them.

Good luck and have fun!

I'm curious about the part where you have to get up without using your hands. That's not how we teach it. We have people get on their hands and knees, set one foot then the other with their hands still on the ice (kind of a squatting position), then rise to standing position. Some people like to put their hands on one knee after they set the first foot and press up from there. Like karne said, it doesn't matter how you get up, just that you can.
