Hi joonieskates, I'll share my experience choosing the right skates for me when I decided to resume skating after more than 20 years off the ice.
I had a big dilemma then, as I was torn between Risport and Edea which were the 2 main brands distributed in the area I lived.
Like you, I like my skates stiff and tight around and above the ankle and after trying on both brands, I eventually decided to go for Risport exactly for that reason.
Risport somehow felt more similar to the boots I used to skate in as a child, while Edea was a completely different sensation for me.
From my experience, Edeas were lighter, more comfortable and I really liked the thin sole, which I felt would give me better sensitivity and control of my movements than what I was used to. However, when I tried to stand up after putting them on for the first time, I lost my balance and almost fell over, as they were built quite differently to what I expected. The heel felt higher than in Risports (and I absolutely hate high heels in general!), the boot seemed to be cut shorter i.e. not reaching as high above my ankle as in my previous skates and I felt I couldn't tighten them as much as I'd like to.
So eventually I chose Risports and I'm very happy with them, altough I think Edeas are really nice and I would have probably gotten used to them too after some practice, just didn't want to take any risks at that time.

Your experience might be different from mine, but I just wanted to point out that if your are looking for boots that feel tight, Edea might actually not be the best choice for you, even though it has some other nice qualities.

When I started I was given a similar choice. My pro shop guy (Risport) wanted me to go for the lower stiffness model but said that the higher stiffness model would also break in eventually, it just takes longer and you don't have the heavy impact elements and technique yet. At the beginning you might indeed feel a little boxed in and tend to skate stiffer in the knee, but I think a beginner does that anyway. You have to built up your muscle support and strength over time. I have heard good things about the jackson and wide feet. It's very difficult to say though without trying them on. The Risport RF3 for example is great for me ( I have a wider ball area and narrow heel), the RF4 though, was too small for me in width. They probably used different lasts for each model.

TLDR I can see both choices working fine, if you really don't skate that much and only do half or single jumps, even the Elles should last you quite a bit if they fit well and are treated well. But all IMHO, of course

Hmm you should be ok with skates rated up to axel or beginning doubles. It may take longer to break but you can wear them home to get your feet used to them (with your hard guards on, and not walking in them, but just wearing them while you're reading or sitting).
I'd say you'll break down Elle's very fast. I, personally, would go with the Freestyles or Competitor.
...I don't want to say anything bad about your seller but not bending your knee in Freestyles is definitely bull... Just lace them lightly at first.
Try to get your feet sized at a pro shop, if that's not possible in your country try to get someone to help you in this. Every brand has a different size chart, so use their website to check. Try them on before buying of course.
