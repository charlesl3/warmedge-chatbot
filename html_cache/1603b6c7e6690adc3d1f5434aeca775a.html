So I've be skating for a little more than month now, and am currently doing group lessons and a weekly private lesson. I feel like I've learned a lot (mainly in privates, actually, progress in groups have been... slower), but everything is so hard!!! We're following ISI levels, and have looked at most of alpha-delta and some of FS1. I have questions on a lot of things I've learned, and was wondering if anyone had tips/suggestions for any of these.

Forward/backwards crossovers: They are definitely coming along, but I'm struggling a lot with the underpush after the cross on both forwards and backwards, particularly going CW (CCW is usually OK).

Spirals: Just doing them on flats, going forward. The ones of the right foot are above parallel, but on the left foot, I can just barely get them there. I think it's more of a strength issue than a flexibility one, so are there are any exercises off ice to help with this?

Consecutive edges: Forwards are fine-ish. Going backwards, outside is hard but it's getting there... Insides make no sense at all to me!! I can't hold the edge for even part of the semi-circle. Any tips?

Forward 3-turns: Outsides are fun, insides are a lot harder. I did several in a row on the right side, but the left side I either have to put my foot down after the turn, or I don't even make it through the turn.

Two-foot spin: THIS IS SO HARD. I did one OK one with 6ish revolutions and haven't been able to do one since (all my other ones are like 2-3 revolutions). I can hear my toepicks, but when I try to put weight farther back I get really unstable in the spin. I also feel like my upper body is off-center??

One-foot spin: A little easier than two-foot but any spinning tips would be helpful haha.

Jumps: So far, just the waltz jump and the 1/2 flip. I get so little air haha. We did waltz jumps like an open mohawk -> back outside edge -> step forward -> waltz jump, but it's so hard to connect the elements together. For the 1/2 flip we're doing them out of a left outside 3 turn. I feel like I'm not really jumping on this one as much as I'm just stepping from my right toepick to my left toepick as I'm turning. Any tips on making it more jump-like?

Yeah, so that's a lot of things that I'm have issues with at the moment haha, but I'm really hoping to get better!

I also had some questions regarding equipment and testing... So right now I have the Riedell 33 boots (I think the adult equivalent is 133 but I have tiny feet and can't fit the adult sizes) with the blade they came with. Obviously I'm not thinking about upgrading quite yet, but when would be the best time (maybe when I have some of the single jumps?)/what boot should I be looking at? I actually think I would fit better in Jacksons -- I have wide feet, but having never tried them on, I can't be sure -- so maybe Jackson Freestyles? For reference, I'm in my 20s, 5'0", and in the healthy range for BMI (although I'd like to be lower within that range LOL), so I'm probably not putting as much stress on my boots as someone who's 6 feet tall.

I'm also thinking a bit about protective gear... My elbows and knees have taken a bit of a beating and I'm not as young as all of the little girls and boys at the rink! Is Skating Safe the way to go for that? (Sadly, I think my hips and butt have enough natural padding that I might not need anything for them.)

As for testing... I'm kind of interesting in passing some of the adult tests, but not super committed to the idea yet. I've technically learned everything on the pre-bronze free skate test (although much work is needed for them to be anywhere near good haha), and I've learned most of pre-bronze moves as well I think. Would it be a reasonable goal to be able to test pre-bronze moves/free skate (if I decide to test in the first place) by the around August/September this year? Or is that overly ambitious?

Anyways, lots of questions haha sorry! But if anyone could even help me with one of these things I would be so grateful.

At only a month of skating, it feels like your coach has thrown so much at you that you cannot focus on a select set of elements at a time. It takes time to master these things, and expecting them to work after only a month when you're working on a hundred other elements during that is unrealistic.

Heck, jumping this soon is way too soon IMHO. Your coach should instead be focusing you on the basic skills leading up to that, not trying to get you jumping before you can do a proper back crossover.

Sounds like you're doing great for skating a little over a month.

For most of those moves, you'll just naturally get better with practice. Even senior-level skaters work on improving crossovers and three turns. Practice the moves every time you skate, listen to your coach's suggestions and write them down if you're likely to forget by next time you skate on your own. The progress might be too slow to notice from day to day or week to week, but if you video yourself now and again in a couple of months, you'll probably see clear improvement.

For spirals, you can work on strength by getting into a spiral position with your foot on the boards (on the ice or outside the ice if your rink is built that way) in skates, or on a counter or railing or ballet barre or back of a couch at home, anywhere it's appropriate. Then lift your foot an inch higher off the surface, trying to do the work with your back and butt and thigh muscles, not by pressing with your arms on the wall etc. Since you're on the short side, you'll want to start with a lower surface. If you do it in skates, then you could start higher. The other advantage to doing it with skates on is more weight to add resistance and strengthen the muscles more. Or you could use something like ankle weights at home.

How long before the test date does your club require you to sign up for a test? A few weeks before the deadline, you'd need to check with your coach whether s/he thinks you're ready to sign up. Before then, you'd need to keep working on all the moves until you feel comfortable with them.

Pre-bronze is an encouragement test, so if you can get the moves accomplished correctly, without putting your foot down, they don't have to be good for you to pass the test. But the more comfortable you feel with all the edges, the FO threes, and with getting power from crossovers, the further along you'll be toward getting ready to work on the next test. There's no need to rush into testing pre-bronze if that will mean spending more time working on bronze.

As you get a little closer, you might want search youtube for videos other adults have posted of their pre-bronze tests, to get an idea of the general skill level that's typical at that level.

Work really hard on your fundamental edges. This might be stating the obvious, but developing a strong command of your edges from the outset will give you the best foundation for literally EVERYTHING that you will ever learn on skates! I've skated since I was a kid, yet to this day, I still start with a ton of edge-work before I even touch my spins, jumps or moves. Hold onto any edge exercises that your coach gives you, and keep them in your "technical toolkit" to work on your fundamentals.

For jumps, off-ice practice in a pair of sneakers is essential to developing confidence in your movements, which you can then work on translating onto the ice.

Spins - you are absolutely right about your upper body being important to spins (and, well, everything else really)! Work on keeping your upper body + arms taut and in check, to help you achieve speed and revolutions. (Lower body is also important, of course!). For one-foot spins, gradually developing a strong forward outside edge leading into the spin will help with the speed/revolutions (...which comes back to my point about fundamental edge-work being crucial!).

Stretch regularly at home so that your body is ready to tackle all the new moves you're picking up. Arms, legs, back, everything - because skating really uses your whole body. I also like to do a lot of squats to strengthen my legs and core.

Good luck on your skating journey!

For your spins and spirals, (and jumps eventually...) you need to do a lot of core work. Planks & reverse crunches have to become your friends A barre class, or a ballet based workout will help or anything where you are focusing on slow, controlled movement while working with body weight & balance points.

With the spin in particular, the unstable sensation you describe sounds like you aren't engaging the core muscles enough. When you are spinning, image that there is a string going from the top of your head pulling you up as straight as possible, and at the same time the muscles around your middle are contracting to hold your upper body as still as possible. It's a difficult sensation to describe, but when you get it all locked in, you will definitely be able to feel it - it's like you can hold the spin forever!
