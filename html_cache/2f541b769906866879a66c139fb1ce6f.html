Hello, everyone! This is a long post, so sorry in advance, but I'm super excited about all of this.

I had my first figure skating lesson two weeks ago and frankly, I'm shocked at the level of enthusiasm I'm showing about it. I have NEVER been so excited about something like this before, which is eye-opening for me because I played the flute in band from fifth grade all the way to college without feeling like this. Growing up I heard nothing about figure skating and basically only understood that it existed, but it was thing Other People did. I know I triple axels are not in my future, but I wonder how my life would have turned out if I had know about the sport when I was small.

But I'm now a 25 year old woman, so I will try and do the best I am capable of. This seems like a friendly and active forum and I wanted to see if I could get some general guidelines. I'm 5'4" and my weight fluctuates between 130 and 140 without any physical activity. I know weight will drop once I get to moving more on the ice, but I'd also like some tips for off-ice training exercises I could do that would help get me in a good spot for when I start working on jumps and spins. Flexibility is an issue for me, so I know I need to work on that.

There's only one rink available for me, and we have one skating club that hosts freestyle practice sessions at time slots that fit better for me as a working adult. I have to be at FS4 to skate in those, so that is my main goal for now. My coach is a former Olympian and even though I know so little about the sport, I can tell he knows what he's talking about by the way he explains things to me. He said I have very good balance and good knees. I think being in marching band for so long helped, because I had to keep a straight posture and keep my upper body fixed at one point while my legs moved in different directions. He also said I was athletic, which I found hilarious because I haven't exercised in god knows how long. I was told I'm open-hipped but I don't know what that means?

His rates are higher than the other coaches, but I'll be getting a new job in July that should help tremendously with me being able to afford his 30 minute lessons and more practice hours for me. Right now I've only been on the ice for maybe 2 hours total. I tried practicing by myself last Sunday but had a terrible experience with the rental skates. My ankles were hurting after 20 minutes (which hadn't happened before) and the blades were rusty. One felt lop-sided. I learned to do all the Adult 1 level skills in my lessons, but in those skates I could barely forward stroke. It drove home the point that I need my own skates to improve, and I went to the website my coach recommended and ordered a pair of Edea Overtures with the boot and blade for about $250. I hope they will help!

Going forward, what advice do you have for me while I'm learning basic skills? I think I would like to compete in the future as well. I'm a little confused about the levels. I know there's the basic 1-8, the adult, and the freestyle. Do I test for those, or does my coach check off what I can do?

Thank you all so much, and I'm looking forward to spending more time here!

Welcome! You asked a lot of questions so I'll try to answer a few. I would argue that there isn't really much point in regretting you didn't start earlier - maybe you wouldn't even have enjoyed it as a kid. I also feel that for some talented kids whose parents spend so much money on skating, the pressure and expectation get very high, and it can become less fun, especially since it's so competitive that most really good skaters don't even make it out of regionals. Starting as a 25 year old, you're still young enough to do a lot in skating, but you're doing it just for yourself.

You don't need to lose any weight to jump/spin, and you might not end up losing any, even if you might lose inches. Muscle weighs more than fat and you'll probably need to build some if you haven't been active lately. Core exercises are all important for skating - ballet, pilates, yoga. For stretching, try to get into the habit of even 5-10 minutes a day, but EVERY day.

With your skates - ordering online is always kind of risky, especially if you have never skated before. I would recommend to go to a good pro shop and get fitted for skates. If you've already ordered, I hope that when you get them, that you can send it back if the fit isn't exactly right. You want it to be as snug as possible without causing physical pain, with no room for the heel to move around once the skates are tied. Meanwhile, before you get your skates, if you must use rentals, ask them to let you poke around and find a 'good' pair and always use those - at the rinks individual rentals can vary a lot.

The levels that you mention are the USFSA learn to skate levels - they're generally used for categorizing skaters in group classes, and coaches decide what levels you've passed or should be working on. The official Moves in the Field and Freestyle levels are actually tested in front of a panel of judges - that starts with pre-preliminary on the kids track, and pre-bronze on the adult track. Those tests are the ones that serve to decide what level you compete in at competitions.

I'm so glad you've discovered skating! I am an adult (33) more or less beginner too (I skated for a couple years as a teen but not very seriously). I have been amazed at how much I immediately loved being back on the ice, and it's now something I look forward to and go every chance I get. I think you will love it even more once you get your own skates. The difference is HUGE.

There really is no point in wishing to change the past, but I kind of know how you feel anyway. Since I have been back on the ice, I've been making pretty good progress even though I'm very out of shape. I can't help but wonder how it would be if I had never quit as a teenager, or had picked it back up again earlier in life. But at the same time, I'm just glad that I can enjoy it now. Unlike some sports, skating is something you can do well into old age, so I look forward to many years of enjoyment and fitness.

It seems like others have answered most of your technical questions, but I will say one thing. Start working on your leg strength now. Specifically start working towards one-legged squats. When you start learning lunges, sit spins, and some other skills, you will need to bend down low to the ice and lift yourself up again with only one leg. This is not a skill that comes up in a lot of other contexts, and I have found it requires MUCH more strength that you would expect.

Good luck with your skating!

Skating becomes quite the obsession. I started private lessons at 41 with the goal of leaning a few things (had done basic lessons as a kid). Basically started from scratch and was addicted almost immediately. I practice twice a week during public sessions (as well as freestyle sessions this year when I can) and work with a coach every three weeks. I notice my body has changed, even though I was in good shape when I started and work out a lot off ice, in a really good way. After almost three years, I have most basic spins and can land loops and flips. Definitely addicted and want to compete in the adult league eventually. The investment in good skates was so worth it. What I notice at our rink is that the adult skaters are way more focused and work really hard since our time for skating is valuable. Luckily I made a lot of friends at the rink and we have some great adult skaters who are really inspiring. My advice, focus A LOT on basics--edges, moves in the field stuff when you get to that, and don't give up. I never thought I would love a sport more than gymnastics, but I really do.
