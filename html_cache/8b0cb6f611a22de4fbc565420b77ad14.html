Hi and welcome. There are many factors: Advanced blades have a higher grade of steel and differ in carbon content. Many beginner blades contain/contained nickel. And then there are alternative options for advanced blade materials now such as titanium, aluminum, carbon fibre etc. Chrome processes and soldering and if there is hand brazed with bronze on the stanchions also varies on level of blade, it's stronger on more advanced blades to put up with the higher and stronger jump level and repetitions, among other things. Blade cut (parallel, side honing, tapered, parabolic, etc varies and some of these are only on the highest level blades), same with toepick size, cut (straight or cross cut). Hollow on blade with how it is ground, and so on. Radius (rocker). Traditional style blades vs Revolution style blades.
There are many factors. But the most important thing to know is that you should always be in a blade that is appropriate level for your current skating. Beginners do not need an advanced blade.

  • Beginner blades are typically made from the cheapest carbon steel available, and are pretty flat in profile as to help people learn the very basics.

  • Then you have intermediate blades that typically have a much rounder profile, which helps with learning some actual figure skating moves like turns and spins. Some of them are still made with some pretty cheap steels, while others, e.g., Eclipse Aurora is made with 440C stainless steel, which is the hardest and most expensive steel available in figure skates current manufacturers.

  • When you go to advanced blades, the blade goes back to a more flat profile to give you better balance with advanced moves and jumps etc. They are less forgiving. Here you have blades still made from the better carbon steels, or harder (and more expensive) stainless steels. Some blades in this category also have some gimmicky features that drive costs up, that either don't matter that much (tapered and parabolic blades), or actually work exactly opposite to what's advertised, such as side honing, which is supposed to increase bite, but the chrome removal process actually makes these blades bite less, not more *endrant*.

Anyway, there's certainly nothing too special about the more advanced blades. You're probably better off staying away from the cheapest blades anyway, so a good "intermediate" blade will get you a long way. They're also easier to get sharpened well, which is another thing, if we're talking about smoothness and feel on the ice: It's not really the blade that matters as much as the person who sharpens it. And also the ice you're on. I'll take a well maintained beginner blade over a botched Gold Seal any day. As to the "feel", it's pretty subjective. When people have an expensive blade that is supposed to be good, they think it has to be good, so it's good. It's also expensive, so they take good care of it, so it stays in good condition. And as far as always being in a blade for "your level", I disagree. There are differing opinions on this one, but here's mine: An advanced blade won't make you a better skater, it might even make you bit worse at first, or learning more difficult, but I'd say: If you're going to end up with a Gold Seal, better start with a Gold Seal. So what matters more, is what your goals are.

If you learn all your turns and spins well on the Mirage, you might want to look for either an intermediate or advanced blade with a similar (flatter) 8' rocker (That would be Legacy 8 and up in Jackson Ultima, or from other manufacturers the likes of Pattern 99 or Gold Seal, and their "copies").

If you have troubles learning good technique, or just would like to try a rounder blade profile to work on your turns and spins, then maybe a good intermediate blade with a rounder 7' rocker might be better (Legacy 7, Coronation Ace, MK Pro, and their "copies").

It is a personal preference as well, so some people might prefer the flatter rocker of the "advanced" blades anyway, especially if they've progressed far with beginner blades. If you have the option to try out different blades, then of course you can do that. However, any intermediate blade and up is surely good enough for anything you could imagine doing anyways, that's kind of my point also, and why people typically recommend them. Ultimately, it's up to you and what you prefer, however. Just remember that a good skate technician is also vital. A good blade in the wrong position or poorly sharpened isn't going to work.


As for myself, I was put on some advanced blades at a very young age quite some time ago, so it wasn't as much about my goals, if you know what I mean . But, the blade I ended up with after trying a few, was the Gold Seal, and I still rock it to this day for the exact same reason as Diana with her MK Pros. Although, now I have a stainless steel version which isn't exactly the same thing, but I've had some work done on it, and it's close enough to some of my old Gold Seals, hehe.
