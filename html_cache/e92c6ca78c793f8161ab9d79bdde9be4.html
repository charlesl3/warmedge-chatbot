Ah, but that's a different story. I assume you are paying for your artistic roller skating lessons, correct? In which case, a "free" practice session is merely bundled in with the price of the lessons. At ice rinks I'm familiar with, there are similar policies if you pay for Learn to Skate (LTS) group lessons. In one rink, the group lesson is held immediately before a public session. Students in the group lesson are given a wrist band to continue practice on the public session. In another rink, students get a set of public session passes (one pass per lesson) included with the group lesson fee. They can use the passes at any public session through the end of their group lesson block.

Additionally, the costs of running a roller skating rink would be significantly less than an ice skating rink, so a roller skating rink would probably be in a better financial position to offer a few free sessions than an ice rink would.

Not sure why you're obsessing over the Cains and Euless, but as far as I know the skaters pay. Maybe they arrange a lower fee or some personal sponsorship to help offset the costs - and many elite skaters also coach on the side to help offset costs which may help them get free or reduced cost ice time - but I doubt there's some sponsor just willing to pay the thousands of dollars a week that getting free ice time would entail. There aren't so many elite skaters down there that it becomes a return on investment. Even at the World Arena, my understanding is that even skaters like Max and Josh and Vincent have to pay.

As far as I know the Cains are not Olympic coaches - yet.

I’m surprised this thread keeps going. As far as I know, in the US good ice and free ice are mutually exclusive.

But I’ve been to a lot of rinks where someone could easily skate free if they’re sneaky and bold. That is, lots seem to work on an honor system for freestyle sessions. Many rinks I’ve encountered simply don’t have enough staff to make sure everyone pays for freestyle. So it would be fairly easy for someone to pop in quickly for a session here and there without paying, but that’s STEALING and is not ‘free ice time.’

Scholarships and sponsors are usually just for individuals, not someone pays the fees for anyone to skate.

Keep in mind also that in LA and Dallas the weather requires significant costs to cool indoor rinks.
