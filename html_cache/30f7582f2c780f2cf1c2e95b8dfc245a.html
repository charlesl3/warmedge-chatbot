What are you guy’s opinions on learning via youtube videos.

I personally try to shy away from them simply because i get so paranoid of learning bad technique that i will have to fix later on but i can’t deny that it’s not a convenient way to learn, especially if you can only do lessons with a group or coach once a week.

Have any of you learned from watching videos? How did it go? Pros? Cons?

I think there are things you can take and build with from YouTube videos most certainly. Some tips resonate with some minds better than others. Everyone learns and absorbs information differently. The best way to utilize them though IMO is to see them as tips as opposed to actual coaching. Coaching involves someone relating similar instructions but MOST importantly reacting and adjusting to how you execute those instructions.

There is nothing wrong with getting information from online videos if it helps you feel more confident and have a positive mentality while learning but I think it is very important that you communicate with your coach what tips you are using so that they understand and can best advise you how to proceed. If you trust your coach you should have no problems including any tips you’ve picked up elsewhere. Even if they tell you to ignore such tips. Your goal should be to get the most out of your lessons whether they are group or private so it’s up to you to communicate this information.

I don’t like the idea of learning anything new from a YouTube video honestly. IMO after you’ve learned something is the best time to then scour the Internet for tips that may help you perfect the execution of those moves. Little mindset tips or use of balance secrets that one person shares could be ineffective with you and completely beneficial to someone else. This is true with both online and in person advice so to me it makes sense to stay open minded and seek tips on a myriad of things. Heck...even things you’ve done for years.

That said...everyone learns differently. Do what’s best for you and be safe!

I try my best to look for quality youtube examples of things like MIF tests for example. I actually learn the patterns from the vids first, then watch my coach who shows me it too. This way I already know in a general way from the videos what she's going to do and I can pay more attention to the nuances.
You do have to be really careful with youtube, because even if someone looks like they're doing it correctly, different regions have different techniques and standards..... or the person is just doing it wrong.

I also watch various youtube videos on jumps and spins etc just to get a different perspective and to see what it looks like when different people do it. My coach is always the finale word on anything, but I like to research stuff from every source available to better understand it.

Everyone is very different. It all just depends on how you learn, what your goals are, and what else you're doing.

Figure skating as a culture is basically private lessons with trained coaches - that's all. But there are other, similar sports where learning on your own is more common, like skateboarding, surfing, or snowboarding. And then there are similar sports where most learn in group situations with rare private lessons like dance and gymnastics. YouTube is a very popular learning tool for dance.

My skater has learned a ton of skateboarding and trampoline tricks just from watching YouTube. He watches a lot of skating and dance stuff too and learns things that way too, but he also has a coach in figure skating and so everything defaults to coach's technique. So it will be like he watches something on YouTube and then tries it during practice but then if it comes up in a private lesson and coach says to do it another way then he does what coach says.

I think if your goal is recreation and fun and exercise then by all means use YouTube. If your goal is competition and striving for perfection then primarily do private lessons. Either option need a strong Learn to Skate foundation though IMO as well as a healthy dose of practical care. Don't attempt elements that are out of your reach, be reasonable with what you try to do.

My first reaction to your post was a "NO!", until I realized that would be somewhat hypocritical of me, because I do it. I'd say 'no' for a beginner skater, trying to self teach a new skill. You need a coach, for reasons other posters mentioned. But in some cases, I think it's ok, if it's more of a complement to lessons and not truly self-teaching.

I've used them for ice dance, but I am not a beginner and I have 45 minutes of private ice dance lessons a week.

I've mainly used you tube videos, typically compulsory dances from Worlds/Olympics, for three things:
1) to teach myself the steps so that I spend minimal money and lesson time on it
2) to work on timing. I'll go through the steps on the floor, or count out beats, while watching Tessa & Scott skate it.
3) to look at specific things that my coach talked about like distance between your hips and partner's hips, leg/arm position, style/expression,...

I don't think watching them will do you any wrong but you need to be critical on how to use them. As many users have pointed out before, they need to be a complement to lessons, and not a substitute —because the fundamental difference between a real lesson and a video is that a coach will correct the differences between what it was taught and what you did, and a video won't do that. So if you're a beginner it will be very very difficult for you to know what you're doing wrong and thus will become incorrect technique...
However if you use it to know how other skaters are doing it —to learn tips that then you can safely try in front of your coach, then that's ok. It's kind of like asking advice to a rink partner outside the rink and then trying it inside —it won't hurt, but you wouldn't pretend that to be a lesson!
Just don't do it entirely on your own —and it's not a good idea if you're a beginner or to learn a completely new movement.
