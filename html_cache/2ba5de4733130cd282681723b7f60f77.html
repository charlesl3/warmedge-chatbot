It is normal to be more flexible on one side.

Pushing a stretch to the point where it hurts or you feel a pulling sensation or a sharp pain is not advised as you have discovered - It sounds like you are repeatedly tearing something which is why you end up having to take a break to recover. But even a few days isn't enough to heal from an injury, which is why you keep aggravating it. Rest & Ice and maybe see a orthopedic doctor or sports medicine doctor if you can afford it to diagnose exactly what you have done. Regardless, I would advise not stretching those muscles for at least three weeks or longer and then easing back into a stretching & strengthening routine. It would be good to know what you are currently doing. How are you stretching? Are you going right to splits? Splits require flexibility in a variety of muscle groups and I find it works best to ease into a variety of stretches of the hamstring, quads, hip flexors, lower back, glutes, piriformis and ilipsoas before I even go near a full split. Are you stretching passively? If so, this is is pretty ineffective. Google active stretching. And be sure never to stretch before a good warmup. Good luck! Be sure to baby the area that is injured because it's not going to help if you keep tearing a ligament.

Where is the pain? Is it in the muscles or the hips? What kind of stretches are you doing when you feel it? Are you bouncing when you stretch?

If it's a sharp pain in your leg that inhibits your movement for the next few days, you probably tore something. You need to ice it and rest, and then go see your doctor. Overstretching and tearing muscles will only inhibit you in the long run. Take it from me: I tore my adductor trying to do a box split about a year ago. I was sitting on the floor with my legs apart, pressing my chest to the ground, when I felt a sharp stabbing pain in my inner thigh. Every time I activated that muscle over the next few weeks, I would feel the same pain, and I was afraid to skate. After about a month it finally healed. I could finally do exercise again BUT every time I stretched that leg, even gently, I would feel an ache in that spot and had lost all the flexibility in that area (probably because of scar tissue). I am only starting to get back to the flexibility I had on that side before I tore it. Even now, when I stretch that side the muscles clench up voluntarily to protect the injury site, and I have to consciously relax them.

Long story short, you should NOT be stretching to the point of pain (more than discomfort), and especially not if you're repeatedly feeling sharp pain. Sharp pain is a signal that something is injured. You should protect the injury first and then go to the doctor to ask about how to heal it. The doc can also recommend how to rehabilitate so that you don't end up permanently injuring your leg and/or holding back your skating/strength/flexibility in the long run. If you want to skate well you need to be healthy, so get healthy!
