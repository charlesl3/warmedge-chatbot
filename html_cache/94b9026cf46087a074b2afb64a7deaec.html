Well, yes, the rules for Spiral Sequences also need to be better. The old rules were stupid, since there were very few ways to gain levels and those ways not only required the skaters to endlessly hold out positions for no reason but also rewarded lackluster Miki Ando-quality catch-foots. In the third underlined section here I detail how the Spiral Sequence element should be re-instituted.

I even have an additional addendum to feature #1 from that posting, to to provide further clarity/variety for getting the spiral sequence level - "Alternatively, any two "quick" positions during the spiral sequence (they do not need to directly follow each other) where the leg is "kicked" or briefly held forward, sideways, or backward will also count for this feature, as long as the positions show good extension and at least one of them is done without assistance of the hand. These positions do not need to be held for the full second that is normally needed to count as a spiral position."

With those rules, Kwan's SP spiral sequences from 1996/1998/2002 would be Level 4 (and any of her others would be there with very minor modification). All of Cohen's would be Level 4. Arakawa's from the 2006 Olympics would be Level 4 and she wouldn't need to do do a catch-foot (or backward inside) on the final position either. Most of Asada's would be Level 4. Those are the kinds of spiral sequences we want to see.

Recently, more skaters started to incorporate spiral into their choreo/transition/step sequence. although spiral sequence is not a required element anymore. I think the figure skating community certainly value fan's love towards this element.
such as Yuna's spiral in SITC step sequence (Suzuki and Carolina also had similar idea), Zijun's change-of-edge Biellmann spiral 2A entrance. Pollina's change of direction spiral choreo. and Many other little spiral sequence used effectively to highlight the music.
also, skaters can still choose to do long spiral sequence in their programs as well, if the music happens to requires it. such as in Yuna's homage to Korea.
When spiral sequence is a required element, it usually a hard part for choreographers to truly incorporate it well, that is if the choreographer/skater truly respect music interpretation. David Wilson mentioned that he listened tons of 007 music, until he hit that a little off-key slow tempo melody(great suit a spiral sequence). then he knew he got it. And the truth is, Yuna's spiral sequence is indeed really a "wow" factor for the arena audience every time (extended leg or not).

In my opinion, a spiral is an element of beauty. Depth of edge/control is important because you need fairly secure edges to do a good spiral. However, extension/position should be worth at least 50% of a spiral. It's one of those elements that is more fully appreciated at a closer distance or on the screen. Michelle Kwan's spirals are so iconic because they were beautiful, secure, had good flow/speed, and were well-timed to the music. She fulfilled all the criteria. I also appreciate skaters, such as Sasha and Mao, who have attempted to do a variety of spiral positions during their career, while maintaining the beauty of the element.
