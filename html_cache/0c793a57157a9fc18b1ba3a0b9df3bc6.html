lulapaloola said:
Even if I enjoy something, I find it hard to get into it mentally if I know it will take me nowhere. I don't know a lot about adult figure skating and starting again as an adult, or if it's even physically possible to get very very good after puberty.
Click to expand...

It seems like you have a very specific idea of what it means for skating to "take you somewhere". If it is something you're thinking of pursuing, try to redefine what "taking you somewhere" means. You appear to be someone who needs concrete goals to work towards. I'm the same way. But there are all kinds of ways to excel at skating that have nothing to do with high level international competition.

I have a friend who had never laced up a pair of skates until he was in his 20s. He has managed to achieve his gold level tests in dance and freeskate, including landing all his doubles except the axel. He is also a fully certified level 2 coach. Even though he never had a chance of competing at high level competition, he has still excelled at a high level and he's gotten a career out of it.

Achieving high level tests is a massive accomplishment. So is getting into the adult competitive scene. It's not as fancy as the major senior international competitions, but it is a lot of fun and especially given your background, just as impressive.

I suggest that the OP get on Youtube and watch programs from adult skating competitions. It will give the OP a good idea of what is possible - there is quite a range of possibility when it comes to what an adult skater can accomplish, and at 19 with some basic skating skills already the OP will already have a leg up on many adult skaters when they start. With a certain level of training and commitment (without quitting school or your day job!), you can skate beautiful programs, pass difficult tests, and do well in competitions! As to landing double and triple jumps - well, time and hard work will tell.

I really enjoy watching other adults skate especially in competition/showcase. What is so great about it, in my eyes, is that adult skaters, even if they are at lower levels, often have a mature and lovely quality to their skating that young kids at the equivalent levels (usually) fail to have. They are graceful, artistic, and emotionally connected to their music. And from what I can tell, most adult skaters, like myself, are skating for the LOVE of it.

In just a month of being back on the ice, I have realized that not everything I do has to be about reaching some arbitrary level of success, and that same attitude has helped me in other aspects of my life. For example, I have for several years (starting in college) been pursuing a professional career in theater/dance/music, and that has its highs and lows. When your passion becomes your career it can sometimes be hard to remember that you do it because you love it. And I remember, through the act of skating, that I also love to sing, and act, and perform, and that I can love those things whether or not I am currently in a paid gig and have multiple gigs lined up down the road. And that I am not WAITING to skate, I AM skating. I am not WAITING to sing, I AM singing. I am not WAITING to act, I AM acting, or pursuing the opportunities that will allow me to act. (I am currently waiting to dance, because at the moment, I can only afford skating or dancing, and I'm choosing to skate. But the two are fairly related.)

It's very Zen. By skating you may avoid having to pay for therapy, right?

I wanted to address this remark because I personally found it extremely troubling and I also suspect it may be part of your problems with school, though I'm no therapist and don't claim to be. Skating requires self-discipline, hard work, the ability to pick yourself up again when things are going badly and try, try again. I have learned more about myself on the ice than I ever did at school or at university or even at work. The ice gave me confidence, the ability to back myself in tricky situations. The ice taught me to fight like I'd never fought before - not just so that I could do an element, but for myself. It made me realise that even though I was flabby and overweight and unfit, my body was still an amazing thing capable of doing amazing things - and that's done more for my self-esteem than anything else.

Click to expand...

USFS doesn't hold state-level competitions.

There are nonqualifying competitions hosted by local clubs, open to anyone who is eligible for the events offered (some include adult events, others don't; a few clubs host adult-only competitions) but mostly attracting skaters in driving distance.

Some states or other geographical areas larger than a single city and smaller than a USFS region or section may have consortia of skating clubs that together host a nonqualifying competition for members of those clubs only. If it's statewide, they may refer to the event as a state championship, but most states do not have something like this.

Many states include figure skating in their State Games. These may include both USFS and ISI events.

Does ISI have state-level championships in all states? I'm not familiar with their structure.

In any case, all of the above could be opportunities to compete relatively close to home at beginner/no-test/adult pre-bronze levels all the way up to senior level.

USFS itself hosts only regional, sectional, and national events on standard track and sectional and national events on the adult track. Adult Nationals does include open events that anyone who has passed the requisite adult bronze-level or higher tests can enter without qualifying through the sectional competitions, as well as championship events from adult silver level and up.

There are similar events throughout the country.

The state grand prix competitions aren't really anything you see or notice. You just accrue points while going to normal competitions. It's not a separate thing or even mentioned at the actual competitions.
