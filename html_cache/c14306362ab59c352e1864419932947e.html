Regarding the pronation, if you haven't already adjusted your blades to compensate for pronation, I suggest talking to your skate tech (I have a weird thing because of a past injury, so I would often flip edges in the middle of a spin unpredictably, until I did something different with the blade position). Something I do that helps a lot (I only do it with the bad foot), is to tie your skate while you are forcing your foot opposite the pronation, bending your foot inward (instep toward the sky, outside of foot toward the floor). It will eventually re-shape the boot a tiny bit over time, in a way that will help you counteract the pronation (I often tie skates for customers, and at least half the time the tendency for beginners is to hold their feet in a pronated position while putting on skates, so they are automatically setting themselves up for putting excess weight on their insteps... I physically re-position their feet as I'm tying the skates).

I don't believe there should be any lean at all, beyond what being on an edge naturally forces the body to do. I try to step in with my entire body lined up, shoulders over hips over knees in a fully straight line, back completely upright, shoulders and arms parallel to the ice, and leg as close to parallel as I can make it go (at least the thigh if the free leg is bent at the knee a bit). The gradual bringing in of the arms and legs in exact timing with each other will pull you through the spin with increasing speed. The higher above the knee you can bring in your foot before starting to press it toward the ice, the longer the spin will last. (just a note, I used to teach, before I had to stop skating for half my life). When I was learning to spin, one time I leaned and ended up hitting my cheekbone so hard that everyone came rushing over telling me they thought I broke it.... so I try really hard to not lean at all.

I think there is an issue of semantics. I'm trying to say that no part of the body should be out of line. Even stepping onto and following an edge, the entire body is to be in a straight line, though tilted to the same degree as the edge of the skate. Leaning of the upper part of the body into a spin makes the body bend, and the bend throws the skater off center from the very first step.

Maybe think of it more as "leading". If you are a counter clockwise skater, you will be leading with your left foot and left hand. The hand and foot should move in exact unison. The angle of the turn-out of the foot should be the guide for where to place the hand. When the foot makes that definitive step onto the ice, that is where the hand stops. If you lean into the spin with the upper body, it's impossible to follow through with everything on point, in line, and in unison. If the hand/arm/shoulder move any bit past the spot where the foot steps down, it twists the body off point and the foot is left behind and has to play catch-up, thereby no ability to simply snap into the spin.


Some coaches teach spins by gradually winding in from an outside edge, but I think that technique causes people to twist and forget about the snap. I think when I learned to spin it was more like a step in and a push that is combined with a snap up from a bent knee to a straight knee, after which point every body part should be working in line and moving in unison. I wish I could diagram it out and post it.

I've made a lot of progress with my spins in the last couple weeks! Turns out one of the root causes of my spin troubles is my head position. My coach suggested trying my entrances for my forward sit and scratch with my head square with my shoulders (it's normally far to the left), and this helps a lot! She thinks it has to do with me thinking my body is in line with my head, i.e. that my edge has gone around as far as my head has/my head thinks it has, so I try to bring my leg around early. Keeping my head in line with my shoulders is really tough, but I've seen a lot of improvement in a very short time after becoming conscious of this (I hesitate to say it's fixed, but at least we've identified the problem ).

I tried a few in my lesson and it felt like I was going into my spin from a flat edge/straight line, because my head wasn't making me feel all twisted like it had been! My coach reassured me, though, that those entrances had been my best ever, and I had better spins to show for it!

Thanks for your help!
