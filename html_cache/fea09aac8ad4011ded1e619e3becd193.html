The link you provided includes both black and white covers. Maybe white ones would stain your white boots less noticeably? (I have no idea, but it sort of makes sense.)

Not exactly an answer to your question:

A closed cell foam insole (I make mine from camping sleeping pad foams - they are designed to insulate) can insulate the bottom of the foot from cold better than most stock insoles.

TBO, I have stopped skating at rinks with unusually cold air. But that might not be an option for you.

BTW, if your boots are very tight, your circulation won't be as good, and your feet may get cold. So you can play with different socks.

If your feet are too loose in your boots, thick socks might be possible - but be aware that they may slip, and reduce control. Experiment. I have tried fleece socks. Wool may work too.

If you want thin socks, polypropylene (warning: may retain a smell) and silk liner socks are better insulation than most. Cotton is extremely poor insulation if your feet sweat.

Or go to an outdoor sports store (e.g., place that sells sports & backpacking equipment), and ask what high end cold weather "liner socks" they sell. (some retain less smell than pure polypropylene.) However, the higher performance socks are generally not be machine washable or dryable hot. May shrink, or wear out fast. Some people actually hand wash them, and hang dry.

It might help a fair bit to keep the rest of your body warm. E.g., if you aren't using them, you might be surprised how much wearing gloves and a ski cap helps. But a ski mask might make you look like an outlaw. Though at outdoor rinks in very cold weather, especially if there was wind, I sometimes used to wear a neoprene ski mask (or maybe I bought it at a paddling [watersports] store). (Possibly available at dive stores too.

Just curious:

Is there a tendency for leather boots to be more or less warm than other materials - both in the outsoles and the uppers? Or is that too generic a question?

I occasionally tried thin neoprene socks - though more for skiing and paddling than skating. I think they worked pretty well - perhaps they cut the wind better than woven socks?

If you have poor blood circulation that contributes to making you cold, perhaps compression socks or stockings would help keep you warm? And in fact, poor blood circulation might also make your feet temporally larger, which would make your boot fit tighter - cutting off circulation more, and so making you even colder. But I understand using compression socks or stockings, if for some reason you shouldn't, has potential medical consequences - maybe talk to your doctor first?

At one point I tried chemical heat packs (designed to prevent frostbite in outdoor sports) and battery powered heaters inside my skate & ski boots. I wasn't happy with them, and they weren't very effective. And I had to redesign my insoles to make room for them. But batteries are lighter now - I wonder if it is a plausible option.

o.p.: I'm sorry if I didn't read your post carefully enough to see what you said about the white covers. Were the bad zippers on just one pair, and in just one batch? I might even have been tempted to call the manufacturer and discuss the zipper issue.

When I briefly used neoprene covers, I tended to accidentally catch my blades on them, and they interfered with "neat feet". But maybe that is because I'm not a great skater, or wasn't used to them.

If you don't need it to look pretty, basic repair sewing is actually pretty easy, though you may need a sturdy needle for neoprene, and you my want a needle threader.

Many "seamstresses" (no gender intended) routinely replace zippers. They often work at dry cleaning stores, and other places. Some shoe repair places may have people who can replace a zipper too.

I used Zandstra boot covers, which are deep blue. They didn't stain my boots, but my boots are black. Instead of a zipper, they use velcro. But it was a bit of a stretch across my relatively wide boots. I can't say whether they would stain white boots.

If the cover is too small, as Nanpat suggests, then a seamstress (or the o.p.) could add Velcro as she also suggests, sewn on in such a way as to add a little extra size.

Velcro (and other generic hook and loop strap systems) eventually wears out too. But could be easily replaced.

If you can't find a seamstress, maybe a tailor or dressmaker could do the same things, though probably at a higher price. Are there people at your rink who get custom skating dresses or production costumes made, or have them repaired? (Some skating parents do that to help support the price of lessons.) Whoever does that could probably sew on a new zipper or Velcro too - maybe also at a higher price.

If you ask around in the lobby of a skating rink, while the kids skate, you might find someone who would do it for a fair price. It's only a few minutes work. And unless you have very high standards of appearance, it doesn't require extremely skilled labor.

OTOH, playing with different socks might give you adequate results too.

Good luck!
