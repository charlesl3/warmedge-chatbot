I don't think they do, but I am not an Olympic skater-ha ha. Other than having separate skates for school figures I have not heard of having two rotating pairs of skates. I don't know how much you skate, but, I sometimes skate multIple days in a row and understand it is a challenge to get them to dry, so I can describe my drying routine for you, it works for me, perhaps you will see something that helps in your skate care routine as well.

So, for me both drying the inside of the skates and the soles and blade mount area is important, so I will include all of it. 1) After skating and then drying my blades, soles and boots the best I can, I slide a dessicant pouch (like the Stink eEze critters) into my boot and I pull very thick puffy soakers onto my blade and up around the sole and bottom of the boot. I stick my boots in my Zuca, which also has a large dessicant pack inside to absorb moisture, zip it up, and they stay there until I get home.
2) When I get home I immediately pull my skates out and pull off the puffy soakers and remove the dessicant inserts. I put a blade only soaker on and pull the tongues way out to open them up. I then set them back in the Zuca, door open (I never close the door to my Zuca unless I am transporting my skates) and I prop them up blades out the door to dry the soles, until I go to bed, and then I flip them around to have the top of the skates open at the door to dry out the inside foot part. You may want to do the reverse or just leave them always with the foot opening facing out since that seems to be the issue. Make sure they are always stored in a warm dry place while they are airing out like this. I have heard of people keeping them near all kinds of heat sources, but obviously use common sense. Anyway, by the next morning everything is dry again

Now, one alternative may be to just leave the Stink eeze dessicants in the skates all the time when not skating, but I have found it works better to stick them in when the skates are really sweaty for a couple of hours and then pull them out and let them air. On weekends I also pull out my insoles and clean them and the insides of my skates with antibacterial wipes and then let everything air before putting it back together again before monday.

So that is how I keep my skates dry and smelling good. I suppose the type of socks you wear could be an issue, I wear very thin trouser socks, but if you have sweaty feet, wearing a thick sock may just make your feet sweat more. Skates are going to get sweaty and wet, we just have to try to get them as dry as possible when they are not in use. I think that is probably easier than a second pair of skates.

I don't know any elite skaters who have two active pairs of skates being used at the same time. I suppose there may be some, based on what people here are saying - but here, where I am, there are not. Those I know have one active pair, and maybe what you might call a "backup pair", which is an old, worn out pair of skates that are past their useful life. But the senior and elite level skaters I know are so hard on their skates that an old pair would normally be too beat up to really use for anything useful. If they had to wear them, they'd probably need to tape themselves into them, to give the boot some support. Or have a skate pro at the event rebuild them a bit, to get them through the night. And the boot may not even have blades on it - the blades, if they still had life left in them, might be on their new boots.

Skates are expensive, especially for the higher end models/custom skates, and most skaters don't have a sponsor picking up the cost of their skates. And because most skates are made, at least partially, by hand, there's variability from one boot to the next, even if you buy the same model - and that can impact how you skate. So it's usually one "active" pair, and if possible (if they aren't too broken down), an old beater pair for just in case - but the old beater pair would be in a closet, not something they actually wear for training or competitions.

And that's if their old boots are in any shape at all. The elite level skaters here go through boots once every 6, 9 or 12 months, depending, more often with Edeas. And their old boots are *wrecked* - I mean, we're talking heels breaking away from the boot, ankles so broken down that you'd need to build in a structural support to wear them plus tape them onto you, etc. But blades can last longer, which is why they might put old blades on new boots, if the blade has life left in it and fits that boot.

Myself, I have one active pair of boots, and one of my old pairs in a closet. I use the old pair occasionally if I want to skate on a pond or do something else that would wreck my blades. I couldn't afford to buy two of the same pair of skates or similar. I think my current skate and blade combo would be $1,000 new, so I'm not buying a new pair until I really, really, really, really, really have to, and then, I'm buying ONE. :lol:

I posed this question to the mom of a 2x World Medalist.

She said they learned the hard way that 2 pairs were needed. There was a concert at some arena where an exhibition ice show would be and when the wood that protected the ice was removed, a bolt got accidentently left behind on the ice. The skater did not see the bolt and skated over it destroying the edge of the blade. The skater's program had to be reworked the night before the exhibition to remove all the edge jumps. As result, the skater then got a second pair. Each week the skaker rotated pairs. Mom said that after that, they traveled with 2 pairs - mom carried one pair and the skater carried a second pair.

Mom also said that not all skaters do this. One team flew in for the pro show and one pair of skates got lost on the way. The pair could not perform so they did not get paid.

Because then if you lose one bag both skaters are screwed and you have twice the problem to fix. At least if Radford's skates have arrived, then he can continue practicing until Duhamel's do. If neither's skates have arrived, you not only have both of them off the ice til they arrive, but also the potential for having to borrow/buy two new pairs.

Last season, the Dodds brothers (and co) lost their bags between the SLC Senior B and Nebelhorn. They were then in an extremely stressful situation because ALL of their bags had been lost (!) and none of them - not Andrew, not Matthew, not Paris - had skates. They couldn't afford to buy three new pairs, and it would have been dangerous for Paris and Matthew to skate in new or borrowed skates (as Andrew explained to one twitter follower). They missed every practice bar the last one, and their bags arrived with less than 24 hours til competition start. Packing all their skates in one bag would not have helped because the airline lost ALL their bags.
