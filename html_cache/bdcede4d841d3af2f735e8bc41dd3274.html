I started skating at the age of 7. From then, I've been on and off with the sport of figure skating. My basics such as turns, spins, etc. are pretty good. I've recently rekindled my love for the sport and I'm currently 15 years old now. Ice dancing is just something I think about a lot and I wished I had realized sooner how much I wanted to do it. I would like to become an ice dancer and am willing to put in the work. My only problem is, would there be a partner willing to skate with me at this age if I have never done ice dancing before despite my strong basics? There are enough rinks and coaches in my area that aren't too expensive and I don't think that would be the main problem. I've heard that many singles skaters switch to ice dance or pairs and that some of them do so because of injuries that occur later in life (late teens) and go on to train with good coaches and have success.

Your dance coach should be able advise you, but if you are a girl and have never done ice dancing before, it is not yet time to find a partner. You should be working on passing all of your pattern dances, and start competing solo dance. If you have good skating skills, I have seen higher level freestylers who've passed senior moves get to this stage in under 2 years. Then you might be ready to try out with partners and potentially compete as a team. At that point there is an extreme shortage of boys, so if you are a girl you might not ever find a partner even if you get there, and if you are a boy, you probably will..

If you would like to eventually compete with a partner, take all your tests partnered. Solo tests do not count towards partnered competitive levels, but partnered tests do count for solo competitive levels. Your coach can act as your partner, even if they are the same sex as you. You can also hire a partner to take you through tests. I started dance as an adult, and have tested all possible ways - with my partnering coach, with a paid partner, and with a competitive partner.

I agree with the others that solo dance is a great way to get competitive experience and also gets you some visibility as a potential partner. Good luck - especially if you're a girl, it isn't easy, as there is a shortage of male dancers.
