Hello there!

I'm a bit new here, so forgive me if this is a tad awkward..

There's quite a few stretches and yoga poses you can do to loosen up your hips to help with skating actually. One very simple stretch for this is the butterfly; sitting on the floor with your knees bent and bring the bottoms of your feet together. (I'm not terribly good at explaining these things, sorry about that). And then, poses in yoga like the pigeon pose can also help or even doing some lunges.

Just be careful stretching and never push your muscles to the point it hurts! It's always a good thing to have someone spot you while working on these too, just in case.

As for knowing whether or not you have 'closed' or 'open' hips, to tell for sure I recommend getting a physical done with a doctor.

Hope this helps~

P.S. To Axelpiggy - Even for an inside Ina Bauer, you need to have a certain amount of turnout to hold it on a larger circle. Try to make it go in a straight line and that will help. Also, make sure you have your leading shoulder pulled back and are looking over that leading shoulder. You also need your pelvis pushed forward and your back leaned back so that you get as much weight as possible onto your back foot.

There are two types of flexibility required for Ina Bauers and spread eagles: (1) groin muscle flexibility, and (2) hip joints that are positioned further to the outside ("open" hips). The first one you can do something about, with stretches like the butterfly, mentioned above. The second you can do nothing about; it's just the way your joints are structured.
In order to determine whether you have open hips, do plies against the boards. Plies look like this: http://dancewithheidi.com/wp-content...13/10/plie.jpg
Stand on the ice holding onto the boards with your feet at shoulder width, turned out and pressed against the boards. Bend your knees and press the insides of your knees against the boards so that your knees are directly over your turned-out feet. Now, pressing your pelvis forward toward the boards and keeping your butt tucked under under you (not sticking out), straighten your legs. If you can do it, you have open hips. If you can't, you either have closed hips or tight groin muscles. The good thing about doing these plies against the boards is that they serve as a good stretch before attempting the spread eagle or Ina Bauer and the boards are forcing the knees to stay over the feet so you don't damage the knee by trying to force turnout (which must come from the hip joint).
