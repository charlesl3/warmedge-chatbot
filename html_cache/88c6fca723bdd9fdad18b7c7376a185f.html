I would suggest not working on an off ice axel without first going through it with your coach. Even when you know what you are doing it can be dangerous if you do something wrong. I sprained my ankle 2 months ago doing an off ice axel (I l landed wrong, it was a fluke accident) and I have been doing them for almost a year with supervision from my coach. I am just now getting back to on ice jumping and recovering from it has been very frustrating.

Off ice is a great way to work on jumps but for the axel work with your coach first.

If you are only doing bunny hops and waltz jumps on the ice you are far, far, away from attempting Axels, even on a jump harness. There are six jumps to learn and the Axel is the hardest.

Rushing your training and attempting things you are not ready for is a recipe for disaster.
