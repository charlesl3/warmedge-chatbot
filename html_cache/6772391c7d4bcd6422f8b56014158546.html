Because obviously for the purposes of what you’re talking about they are the same thing, and it’s amazingly disingenuous to pretend otherwise. The issue at hand is about the traditional gender color. If your boots are black and you cover them with hot pink boot covers, the judges see your feet in hot pink either way. They don’t mark you down for showing hot pink boots but then say “oh, well, hot pink boot covers? Totally different.” (They’re not gonna mark you down at all, actually, either way.)

And I gave more than one example of male skaters who wore actual, nontraditional, white boots, which you’ve just chosen to completely ignore for whatever reason. They got good results in them and both went on to win the Olympics, despite skating in a more conservative decade.

That you think the supposedly arch-conservative judges are going to mark people down for wearing gender nonconforming colors of boots, but are going to be fine with guys in sequinned periwinkle and sparkly feather costumes skating to ballet and girls hitting full splits in pants and catsuits, well, it boggles my mind. Nor are American standards of conservatism the only ones in the world, and the judges are international. (And at least for competitions, I think their costs are covered?) It may well be risky to skate to a brand new piece of music or introduce brand new choreographic elements to your skating, especially if the judges are expecting to see and reward certain styles of skating. That’s unrelated to the colour your skates: we have plenty of examples from skating past and present to show that bold costuming choices mostly aren’t going to hurt you.

(Except cello-butt. That was unfortunate on a few levels.)

————

OP: You can wear black or white skates. Or purple or red plaid or bright pink, if you can find them. There is generally no difference and there’s clearly no risk to compete in them. You might find that, say, black boots from a given brand run bigger and maybe are a bit stiffer around the ankles or whatever, because they’re typically expecting those to be sold to guys and design them with that in mind, but it’s the kind of thing you’d find out anyway trying them out.

There are several women at my rink who wear black skates. None of them are competitive skaters, but in every case, they just preferred the black color.

I would be more concerned about the skate color clashing with costume colors. There are competitive skaters here who wear different colored skates (blue, red, purple), and at least one person with custom boots that have big pink hearts on them. I wouldn't wear pale or spring colors with black boots, but black boots might be better with costumes that are dark colors than white skates. Some of the lightweight blades can be had in black, and those would look really nice with black boots for either male or female.

Um, no they are not. As explained early in the thread rules about boot covers are codified and ARE considered part of the costume. And thus judges COULD judge based on them.
Also, they are temporary and clearly a part of a specific program.

For instance, a woman wearing black boot covers with a black dress may simply want to match for that program. She wears white boots for other programs with or without covers.
Whereas a woman wearing black boots is making a statement about gender or at the very least about tradition.

Edit to add the specific rule:

“At ISU Championships, the Olympic Winter Games and International Competitions, the clothing of the Competitors must be modest, dignified and appropriate for athletic competition – not garish or theatrical in design. Clothing may, however, reflect the character of the music chosen. The clothing must not give the effect of excessive nudity inappropriate for the discipline. Men must wear full length trousers and must not wear tights. In addition, in Ice Dance, Ladies must wear a skirt. Accessories and props are not permitted.
Clothing that does not adhere to these guidelines will be penalized by a deduction “

Obviously it’s up to the judges to determine what boots and boot covers would be deemed “modest, dignified, and appropriate”

AFAIK, no elite high level skater has tested this yet by wearing nontraditional boots IN COMPETITION.
