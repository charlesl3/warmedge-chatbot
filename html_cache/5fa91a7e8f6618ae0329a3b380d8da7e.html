Each skater has a different situation so there is no "norm."

I believe that USFS says that the "average" skater who passes their Senior MIF test took between 5-6 years (fastest that I know of was 3 years). Novice is the hardest to pass - review the longest time it took to pass any level up to then, double that, and that is the minimum of how long you should expect to work on Novice MIF. Junior and Senior are a "breeze" compared to Novice.

Freeskate tests are an entirely different beast since the minimum elements are not in sync with the competitive elements. As a result, lots of competitive skater hold off passing them until they want to move up to the next level. For instance, the elements required to pass the pre-pre include only passing single sal and toe loop but most competitive skaters hold off taking that test until they can do an axel. I know of one girl that competed as a Juv at Regionals (finished 5th in her QR) and decided to test out after that. Within 6 months she had passed her Senior freeskate test. As of 2 years ago, you "only" needed all your doubles (not including double axel) to pass the Senior test - no triples were required.

That is still true. Last fall the freestyle test requirements changed so that it's possible to use harder or different specific elements to meet the requirements, so skaters could skate their competition programs for the test. But the minimum required to pass remained the same -- a test that would have passed last October would still pass after the rules changed in November. (Except for pre-preliminary, which now requires a second spin and a half-ice step sequence even in the No Music option.)
E.g., the jump requirements for the senior test are now
One single, double or triple Axel* • Four different double, triple or quad jumps, one of which must be a double Lutz, triple jump or quad jump* • Two two-jump or three-jump combinations, each including two jumps with at least two rotations

You're talking about the senior freeskate test?

According to the current USFS test rules, judges can ask the skater to reskate up to two moves on a freeskating test after the program. If the 2Lz were missed on a senior test and the rest of the test was reasonably good, judges would probably ask for a reskate on that jump because 2Lz is the minimum top jump for that test, the first time and only time it's required. (Or, as of last fall, a double axel or triple could take its place.)

It's also possible for judges to pass a test with only one serious error by marking up the second mark, assuming the skating is good enough to deserve higher scores there. So for a really strongly skated program with a missed 2Lz, it would be within the rules for judges to pass the test on the second mark, but not all judges would be willing to do so.

Also as of this year, skaters can use signed protocols from a competition to pass freeskating tests at juvenile level and above. They need to earn minimum scores for both TES and PCS and also for the total score, and they can't have any elements that earned 0 points. So success on the other elements and solid skating skill and performance could make up for the missed lutz.

Beginning next fall the freestyle test scoring is going to change even more, but we don't know the exact details yet.

So the short answer is that yes, it is theoretically possible to pass the senior test without a 2Lz or any harder jump, but the other jumps, other elements, skating, and performance would have to add up to better than just adequate senior quality on their own to make up for the missing lutz.

Keep in mind that many, many things will affect how long it takes you to pass a test. So what's average for the typical skater may not reflect your own situation -- if you're really dedicated and have the discipline and good coaching to really study the tests, you'll probably be faster than average, but if you take a more laid-back attitude toward skating and the tests, you'll probably be slower than average. So don't fret if you're a lot behind average.

Looking at people who have passed the Senior MIF test, and notice this pretty much means looking at the most serious 1/6 of skaters, as a rule of thumb it'll take roughly 2 years from passing the Pre-Preliminary MIF test to passing the Juvenile test. Within those 4 tests, Pre-Juvenile is the most difficult one, taking the longest and having the lowest pass rate.

After that, it takes roughly a year each, with Intermediate and Senior being several months less, and Novice and Junior being several months more. So overall, roughly 6 years from Pre-Preliminary MIF to Senior MIF. Novice takes the longest, with Junior taking the second longest. They also have the lowest pass rate.

On average, the "average" skater will fail one of the tests between Pre-Preliminary and Juvenile, then fail 3 more tests before passing Senior. This is average though, and individual experience varies widely -- some skaters will go through to Senior only failing once or twice (or even not at all), while others will fail 4 times before passing Pre-Juvenile. So it really depends a lot on the skater, the coaching, ice time, nerves, all sorts of things.
