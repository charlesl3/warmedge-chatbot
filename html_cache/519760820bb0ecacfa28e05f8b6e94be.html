hi, I'm getting new skates soon and will either get Edeas or Riedells. I've always worn Riedell, but I broke down my last pair (Silver Star) in 8 months and they have no support at all anymore. So, I was looking at the Ice Fly, but am concerned about the high heel. I've heard alot of skaters get foot pain with Edea and am scared to try them. Is Edea similar to Riedell? Will Edea fit like Riedell? I used to have Yellow Superfeet in my skates but I was able to take them out recently and my skates fit great and no pain. Will I need Superfeet with Edea? I am in 6.5 B/A Riedell, will Edea fit ok? I am mostly concerned about the pain, but if Edea is similar to Riedell and will fit my foot, I think it will be fine.
I am landing my doubles up to a double loop and am on Novice moves. Will Ice Fly be ok or should I look into Riedell? Either Aria or Gold Star, but my main concern with those is whether they will be strong enough and last me 1+ years. (I have a friend in Ice Fly who has had her skates for almost 2 years working on all doubles so they are probably stronger than Riedell? I don't know anyone who skates in Aria). I am 5' 4" and 105lbs. but apparently pretty hard on my skates. Any information will be much appreciated.

Wearing an Edea is completely different than wearing Riedell or any other traditionally made boot.

What you should do is go to a good figure skating expert fitter, get your foot measure and traced and then let them make suggestions of what brand and boot is best for you and your foot and skill needs.

Figure skating pro fitter, NOT a run of the mill proshop in the ice rink that more than likely knows more about hockey than figure skating at your level.

Good luck.

In my completely biased opinion, stick with the Riedells, just look into a stiffer boot like the Aria or Fushion. Full disclosure, I have been skating in Reidells for about 6 years and I love them. I am also hard on my skates and I'm quite a bit bigger than you are (5'9'', 200lbs) and I usually went through the Aria skates in about a year when I was training doubles. After my first pair of Riedells, I tried Edea's and I hated them. I could barely skate and I have been on the ice for over 20 years and have my Gold level tests. They were Ice Flys and I was definitely over booted. I also hated the higher heel. I took them back and exchanged them for Riedells.

All that said, Ic3Rabbit is right. Find a figure skating shop and have them fit you. They can give you a better idea of what you might need and give you guidance to finding the right boot. Unfortunately, the only way to really know which one is right for you is to skate in them.

Definitely go to a fitter that knows Edea skates, just like IceeRabbit said. Also, I would listen to a fitter because, in ny experience, they are trying to lookout for you. My fitter down near my college listened to my coach's suggestion of Edea because I had wide and it was either Edea or custom Riedell (yeah, my feet are that wide where Jacksons were not a top option).

I switched from Jacksons to Edea, and so far I do like them. They are different and a little odd for the first few skates, but you'll either adjust or not. I've adjusted to them to the point that I cannot stand my Jacksons anymore, and I've only had the Edea for a month or so now. I had to put superfeet into my skates because I have low arches and the footbed of the Edea boots are really flat.

The biggest thing I've noticed with the Edea is getting use to the shorter blades, which I may have rocked off the back a few times now . Also, the shorter blade, for me (or it could be the recovering injury), has led to off timing for jumps and trying to find the sweet spot for spins.

I agree with silver.blade! The only way to really know is to try the boots and see if they work or not for you. I've tried Riedells and Jacksons, as well as a variety of blades.

Good luck!

Going from my experience and thoughts:

I do not think they are similar in shape, but then again I have Edea Concertos E width (yeah... my feet are that wide). The footbed layout is different - Edea is flat and more steep-like than Jacksons, which have a but of support in the footbed and are not as steep-like (I think this is the illusion of the Edea boots being high heeled) I just measure dmy Jacksons' heels at the back and my Edeas' and the Edeas' heel was only bigger by ~0.07-0.08 cm. I found the Edeas very breathable compared to the Jacksons, which means my feet didnt hurt as bad (after I put in superfeet to amend the flat footbed) or as quick. The padding is nice. What gets you is the the different way that Edea supports your ankle, as well as the looser ankle and shorter blade.

However, I cant guarantee you anything because I'm not a fitter. A fitter will be able to find what is right for you. I hope I answered the your question in some form. If not, you can PM me , or listeb/read other peoples insight.

Good luck!

Are you comparing the new jackson fusions or the old style jacksons?

The new fusions have a higher heel than Edeas. They are also made out of synthetic material like an edea. However the foot shape is different than an Edea. The toe is rounder and the boot is a split width, meaning one width narrower in the heel than the ball. If you need a narrower heel, the Edea might leave you with your heel slipping a lot.

FWIW I tried the new fusion Jackson with the higher heel, and I never adjusted and I hated them. I'm back in a standard heel and the difference is amazing. But a skater at my club switched to Edeas last year and loves them and has no issue.

But mostly I'm on the "if it ain't broke, don't fix it" train. If Riedells fit you nicely but are just breaking down quickly, I'd first try a stiffer Riedell. You're going to be adding a lot of new variables with the Edeas. You're also not going to get your B/A split width in Edea.

Everyone's foot is different, but I hated Jacksons and I hated Edeas. It's always a risk that the skates will feel great in the shop, but not work on the ice. That's what happened with me with both Jacksons and Edeas. A good skate shop/fitter will usually be understanding about the skates not working on the ice for you and at the very least buy back the skates you don't like at a discount as long as you haven't beaten them up too badly. I got luck with my Edeas and my shop took them back after a month and gave me a full refund, but that is an exception as opposed to the rule.

Unfortunately, finding the right skates can be long and expensive and you just have to keep trying brands until you find one that works for you.
