Here's a rough translation, since it doesn't look like there's one available on the video:

The situation is as follows: I have a friend, who is a coach from out of town, whose student bought some skates online and was looking for help mounting the blades. I agreed, and asked them to send me the skates. The blades were sent first, some blades costing about 1000 yuan (about $150 USD)—I looked at them, they were good. Next, the box of boots arrived yesterday. When I opened it, I was a little surprised to see that it was different from the usual EDEA boxes. Let me show you.

(He takes out the 1st box) This is what was sent to me. The color is a bit orange, and the logo is off-white. Next, when you buy Edea skates, this is the box that you receive (He pulls out the 2nd box): It’s is a very deep red, and the logo is a bright white. When you look at the boxes together, you can see the difference. Well, I wasn’t too concerned, I thought that maybe the box was stored longer and became discolored, just as long as the skates inside were fine, it isn’t a problem.

Well, so because I needed to mount the blades, I loosened the laces and took out the tongue, then took out the sole to drill the holes. Well, when I took out the sole, and because I also skate in EDEA Ice Flies (and take out the soles to dry), I wondered why the sole of this Ice Fly didn’t feel like mine. So then I compared it with the sole of my own Ice Flies, since I had never encountered this before. Once I compared them, they were really different! So then I carefully inspected the entire boot and ascertained that this was a pair of fake Ice Flies. I also relied on 10 years of experience and sent pictures to other skate technicians and they agreed that this was a pair of counterfeit boots. So, I decided to make this video in order to warn others about these fake boots when they go make this expensive purchase.

Okay, let’s look at the boots. (He takes out the boot) From the outside, can you tell if this boot is fake? Here’s the left one, and (He takes out a second boot) the right one. (He takes out a third skate (blades are mounted)) Here is my own skate, let’s compare. (Comparison of skates begins at 3:00) You can’t really tell, right? Let’s go over where the fake boot is different.

Let’s start with the tongue. You can see the logo on the fake boot. But if you look at the real EDEA skate, where is the logo? It’s not all the way up at the edge of the tongue. With my own skates, the friction from tying laces has already rubbed the logo off, but the logo is about 1cm under the top of the tongue. Let’s look at some other EDEA skates. Here is a black EDEA Piano, see, there is a gap between the top of the tongue and the logo, same as the white Piano, same as the Concertos. Also, this is the same for the Edea Chorus and Overtures. But, this fake Ice Fly, the logo is placed all the way at the top. This is the first difference. Okay, I’m going to clean this up a bit.

Next, I’m going to take out this tongue. My first thought was, how could the tongue of a high-level skate be so soft? Especially here? (He’s squishing the tongue) It’s just like a sneaker. There’s no stiffness at all. Meanwhile, with my own skates, I’ve already worn them for over a year, for 3-4 hour sessions at a time, I’m going to take out the tongue and compare the tongue stiffness. (He squishes the tongue of the real skate, and then the fake one) And this is the tongue of a new boot! It’s never been worn! It’s soft and floppy. And the real Ice Fly, which I’ve already worn for a year, the tongue is still relatively stiff. This is the second difference.

The 3rd difference is at the bottom of the sole. There is the EDEA logo and the boot size. The fake one is size 280, and mine, the real one is size 270. If you don’t look carefully, it’s hard to see the difference. The difference is that the real logo on the bottom of the boot is a bit more deep, and the numbers on the fake boot are a little skinny. You have to look carefully to tell.

And then look at the heel. This is the fake boot’s heel. The edge is not flush with the rest of the heel. But with the real Ice Flies, the bottom of the heel comes up a bit. The part with the logo dips down a bit, and the bottom edge is a little raised. It’s not just Ice Flies—Pianos, Concertos, Chorus, Overtures—the bottom edge of the heel is always a bit raised. And the fake one, the bottom edge is lowered.

So next, I’m going to take out the sole. Once you compare the two, you can tell. Here is the sole from the fake boot. Next here is the sole from the real skate. See? The first thing you notice is that the boot size is written on top of the sole from the real skate. The numbers are missing from the sole of the fake boot. And the texture of the sole from the real skate is rough. The sole of the fake boot is smooth. Pianos, Ice Flies, Concertos, and Choruses have soles that have a rough texture. Only Overtures have soles that are smooth. And boots from Choruses up have the boot size written on the sole. Okay, this is what to pay attention to for the soles.

And if you can’t tell from that, there’s something else. It’s the loops for the laces (okay, I don’t know what the actual word for this, it’s what’s keeping the laces in place on the tongue). On the fake boot, the front part of the tongue is completely smooth. For Ice Flies and Pianos, there are loops for the laces located here. If you look at my skates, there are two loops to hold the laces in place. (Comparison, the fake boot is on the left) Looking at them together, the real skates have the lace loops and the fake boots are missing them. You can tell them apart pretty easily now. The Ice Flies without the lace loops are fake.

Finally, everyone knows that EDEA has special screws for their boots. They are specially made for their boots, and are golden. They are packaged in a dark green bag. (He opens the bag) And the screws are golden in color. But lets look at what came with the fake boots. Since I was going to mount the blades, I’d already opened the bag of screws, but, the bag color is a light green, and the real bag is a dark green. And look, the screws that came with the fake boots are silver. I’ve never encountered silver screws coming packaged with EDEA boots. Of course there are variances in how gold the screws appear, but never silver screws. Perhaps they couldn’t get a hold of gold screws, so they just used readily available silver ones, and just packaged them similarly to EDEA. Once you compare, you can tell the difference.

With all these differences, hopefully you can tell the differences between real and fake EDEA boots. If you have EDEA skates of your own, you can take a look yourself, and see if what I’ve described is true. I’ve also contacted the coach of the student who sent me these boots. He purchased them online and spent 2800 yuan (roughly $430 USD) on new EDEA Ice Flies. I also do work in selling skates—our wholesale price for Ice Flies is above this amount. To be able to pay that amount for new Ice Flies is a bit inconceivable. So there’s a reason cheap skates are cheap. Ice Flies retail for about 3800-4000 yuan ($580-615 USD), and differences of 200 yuan ($30 USD) is fairly common, depending on the store. But for skates to be 1000 yuan ($150 USD) less, then that’s a bit hard to believe. And this boot that he bought, ended up being fake. So everyone, please be careful. There are some immoral people out there that do this kind of thing.

I know that once I post this video, I might get some hate from people that sell these fake boots, since they might also watch my channel. To them I say, if you’re going to go and do this kind of thing, then you have to be ready for people to report you. I don’t have anything to be afraid of, but I don’t know the whole picture other than these fake boots being sold, so I’m not going to publicize the name of the online shop. Who knows what will happen in the future when I put this video out there. I’m just going to put it out there for people to see. It would be best if counterfeiters just stopped, there’s no need to corrupt the marketplace.

I also found what counterfeiters are selling tend to be relatively inexpensive, and to those that don’t know too much about skates who just want a cheap pair of nice skates. They tend to be the easiest people to fall for this trick. For a new skater, they don’t know what a real pair of EDEA skates are supposed to be like. The fake boot looks pretty nice, and the EDEA logo looks real. If you don’t have the background knowledge, or aren’t comparing with other skaters, you might not notice. So I’d like to ask everyone to take a look at their skates and make sure that there aren’t any problems. Finally, I’d like to recommend for everyone to go to a real store or reputable online stores to buy skates, if you somehow end up getting a fake boot, you have some recourse to get refunds or replacements. If you just buy them online and they end up being fake, what can you really do? The counterfeiter’s closed the shop and fled. And lastly, if you could help spread the message and let everyone see this, and help prevent new skaters from falling into such a big pitfall when they just begin. Okay, that’s it for today’s video, I’ll see you next time, bye bye.

I have been an Edea dealer in the US for many years. I believe the boot shown is one of the early versions of the Ice Fly rather than a counterfeit boot. They make improvements nearly every year. I have seen silver Edea screws come from Edea directly. Long time ago, yes, but they had them. The insoles at first were designed to be used in 2 sizes of boots...so at first the size was not printed on them. The tongue has had a few improvements as well over the years. I fully believe the boot shown is merely a really old version. It would have been sold at a deep discount just to get rid of it I would think.
