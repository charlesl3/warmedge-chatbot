As long as the child skates well in competitions and as a result, draws in more students to this coach, then the family is probably fine. Here is the question that the mom really what to ask to the coach - how much longer are you going to be giving my child free lessons and what are the associated expectations on my family's end. But since I assume the free lessons are rather hush hush so how does a parent even broach the subject let alone what are the associated expectations?

Another concern that I would have - what happens if my child and this coach are no longer a good "fit" for whatever reason. Since she has given your daughter so many free lessons, will you stay with this coach longer that what is beneficial out of obligation? From that I have seen, very few students stay with one coach thought out their entire skating career so I think that should be considered.

Sorry to sound so negative but I have spent a lot of time thinking about this subject over the years. If a family can get free lessons, I would say go for it but I would want to know up front what was expected in return.

I think the most important thing is just to keep it clear as to which lessons are paid for and which lessons are free. So saving text messages is probably the thing to do. The worst thing is if there's miscommunication on this between you and the coach resulting in a dispute about how much to pay. So as long as that's clear, for example on a weekly basis "this week I'm paying for 4 private lessons but not more", then it should be fine.

You should also keep in mind your own expectations, that these lessons are free and thus to not start relying on them or depending on them or expecting them. As other posters have said, the coach may at any time decide to give their "free" time to other skaters at no obligation to you.

There's a large number of reasons why the coach may offer free lessons to their students. Some posters have mentioned the marketing aspect of it. I think there's a big difference between offering free lessons to a coach's existing students, versus offering free lessons to skaters who do not take (paid) lessons from them, or (worse) skaters who take lessons from other coaches. But I assume your case is the former. Off the top of my head:

1. It's a way to keep active, if they have downtime between lessons with paid students. Otherwise they have nothing to do during that time.
2. The skater is available when the coach is available -- the coach wants to see the skater improve, so since they're already at the rink anyway, they might as well as help out.
3. The coach wants more experience coaching students; just like the above, if the coach is already at the rink anyway and has some free time, they might as well as be productive in sharpening their coaching skills.
4. The coach wants to "reward" so to speak the skater for being at the rink and working hard. If the coach feels the skater is one of the more hard-working students, they're probably more inclined to help out that student if they have some free time.

Basically there's the human aspect to this -- yes some coaches are driven to coach for the money, but others do it because they simply want to see skaters improve, and the money is just there so they can afford to do so. It's not necessarily just pecuniary. When you talk to your coach about this, I think it's important to make it not adversarial, but to keep it friendly while you're being upfront about it. I don't even think it's necessary to ask the coach "why" (although you certainly can if you want), just as long as you make sure it's clear which lessons are paid for and which ones are free. I think it's a good thing that your coach actively texts you to make sure it's okay with you -- it means your coach is probably also aware of the potential for miscommunications on this, so your coach should be receptive to you making sure that it's clear which ones are paid for and which aren't.

In terms of if it's common, I know that at least one of the high-level skaters at my rink simply has a flat weekly rate regardless of how many lessons she has with her coach -- and her coach is very active with her. My coach does give me quite a bit of advice on the side without the expectation of being paid for it, though those aren't formal "let's get on the ice and I'll talk you through some skills" lessons, it's just chatting and fielding some questions. So it certainly does happen.

Coaching is a business. Businesses have a concept called "goodwill". That is, businesses have the freedom to unofficially grant goodwill (this includes freebies) on an informal basis. But if you insist on having the goodwill formalized, you run the risk of having the goodwill withdrawn.

The only risk I see you running (based on your input so far) is that your daughter's rate of progress is likely accelerated by the freebies, and you can come to expect that rate of progress as the norm. If the freebies are withdrawn, her rate of progress will likely decline. And then will you and your daughter be OK with that? Or will you feel the need to feed her habit, so to speak, because you're hooked, even though you really can't afford it.
