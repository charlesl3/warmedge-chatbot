Hi everyone,

I've been searching everywhere for an answer to my daughters question and I just couldn't find it anywhere. My daughter started skating a while back and has made good progress. We bought the Risport Antares with the MK 21 blade back then. Now she is moving up with spins and jumps and more complex footwork and we decided to get her new boots and blades. For some reason, she is persistent to want the John Wilson Pattern 99 revolution and on top of that, parabolic. She is concerned though that the blade might affect her skating negatively because of the 8' rocker. I thought the coronation ace might be good but she doesn't like the cross cut toe-pick. Can anyone help or has advice for us? What is the difference between a 7' and 8' rocker? Is the Pattern99 suitable, if she doesn't jump triples?

Thanks in advance!

What jumps is your dd working on? That really drives the blade selection as well as age, height and weight. Although I do know of small younger skaters using Gold Seal equivalents but they are working on double axels, starting triples . It really depends on if they understand how to use the blade.

Generally speaking if working on singles a Coronation Ace or similar would be good. It is a personal preference on crosspicks but it does provide a better pick on toe assist jumps.

Double Jumps and higher - Pattern 99 is suitable. More of the blade is on the ice for an 8' rocker. Some elite skaters have stayed with Pattern 99's through their whole career as they did not like Gold Seal (or equivalent) which has a higher stanchion (higher off the ice).

Hope this helps!

Where is your daughter getting her information? There must be someone influencing her thoughts about the Pattern 99s and cross-cut toepicks?

Honestly, the Coronation Ace is just as good a blade as the Pattern 99 in most respects, and it's a good intermediate blade on the way to eventually getting Pattern 99s. I have used regular Wilson blades as well as the Parabolic version and Revolution version of those blades and I can honestly say that there is no significant difference. I felt a slight advantage on edges with the Parabolics, but no difference at all with the Revolutions (except that they are harder to wipe down because they aren't one smooth, solid piece of steel). And some people who have tried the Parabolics hated them and ended up just re-buying the standard blades. Until her feet stop growing and/or she's working and paying for her own blades, I would get the Coronation Ace blades and use the money toward her ice time and/or an extra few lessons instead.

As for cross-cut toepicks, I go out of my way to buy blades with a cross-cut pick because they can grab the ice regardless of my picking angle on a jump. Why wouldn't someone want that?

For reference, I've passed USFS tests through Intermediate MIF and the equivalent of Juvenile FS (Axel required).

There is such a vast range in that it's hard to tell where someone is just based on what jumps they are working on. What level is she competing? If it's high beginner, pre-pre test track, or freeskate 1-3 then you're likely fine with a Coronation Ace or MK Professional. If it's no-test and ready to complete pre-pre WB next season and your daughter doesn't tend to grow out of things quickly, it might not be a bad idea to go up a notch.

If she's weeks from landing an axel and ready to start doubles, the blade choice (if you have to get one anyway and knowing it could last a year or more) might be different than what you'd chose for someone who is just landing singles and just starting an axel with 6+ months before starting a double sal.
