I'm returning to the ice after a 20 year hiatus because there is a rink adjacent to my home with free public skating and I want to counteract the effects of working a sedentary desk job. Back in my earlier years, I competed at the Intermediate FS and Silver Dance levels. I consider myself extremely fortunate to have retained a nice chunk of my muscle memory and technique, but my body has changed (as expected). I'm rusty, but every day on the ice, I move a little faster and with more confidence.

I'm currently skating on a pair of Klingbeils from 1990 (seriously!) with MK Gold Star blades of a similar vintage. I recently had them sharpened and that was basically the last sharpening they'll ever see. This means I'm in the market for a new blade.

I've been trying to research on my own but as you might have guessed, I was a kid/teen back when I competed, and didn't really need to think much about it. I have a vague sense of how ROH works, am still totally mystified by the discussion of rockers, steel types and so on. Complicating this is the lack of information available for plus size skaters (I am 5'5", 185lbs), and the possibility that my body may change more as I continue to skate (or not, that's okay too).

Here's what I'm looking for: Something that's going to play nice with my height/weight/skill combo, and will be responsive to physical changes. I've got all my single jumps and spins, but I'd like to work towards being able to land an axel again, and finishing up my dance tests.

P.S. If anyone can recommend a reputable sharpener in the Greater Philadelphia area, that would be fantastic!

I think karne is on the right track. You skated as a child so you don't have toepick fear, and you want a blade that can carry you further. I wouldn't worry about the weight issue, I had a male skater friend who was doing singles and he weighed 230 lb. His problem was that at his weight he went through two pairs of boots a year. (and I just want to say, that watching a 6'3" 230lb man doing toe loops with a soft landing was just one of the most amazing things I ever saw because it was unexpected. And when he was on the ice he *owned* the ice. You could not keep your eyes off of him, he was that good.)

Anyway, the Gold star has a 7 foot rocker, and if you feel wedded to that, the coronation ace is 7' also the MK pro. Or you could just get new Gold Stars.

ROH? Why not start at (EDIT) 7/16"? That's pretty forgiving. If you don't like it, well, it's only 40 hours until you need to go to your next sharpening and can get it changed.

I would stick with the gold stars, if finances aren't an issue. The CorAce and prof are really good blades, but they don't have the side honing you're used too, and particularly on the Ace, the spin rocker is going to be different. Why give yourself something else to adjust too while you're refinding your feet?

As you're probably discovering, lots has changed since you bought equipment last. I went through that whole discovery & education 2 years ago when I came back. Have a look at ultima blades, the supreme is likely cheaper than the gold star (but! It's an 8ft rocker), and it's a harder steel so you can go longer between sharpening, especially on the matrix models. Quality control is reportedly better than MK/Wilson. Riedell is making blades now, too, but I know very little about them. Good luck though, and welcome back!!

I believe the only parameter that could vary with the weight of the skater is the optimum ROH. Fortunately, the ROH can be changed during sharpening. I assume you plan to lose weight once you start an intensive exercise program, and that is your concern? If so, you could start with a less aggressive 1/2" ROH, and then switch to a more aggressive 7/16" ROH if you lose significant weight and want to maintain deeper edges, or keep the 1/2" ROH if you are getting deep enough edges.

There is nothing wrong with MK Gold Star blades; Misha Ge uses them and lands quadruple toeloops in them. I'd recommend either getting another pair of those or getting Wilson Gold Seal blades. Gold Seals have the same rocker shape as Gold Stars (so spins and jump takeoffs will feel the same), but they are flatter from the middle of the blade to the heel of the blade so there's more stability and glide to them. When I went from Gold Stars to Gold Seals, the only adjustment was learning to get my weight farther back on the blade when skating backwards. I love the glide and would never go back. They also have side-honing and cross-cut toepicks like the Gold Stars (both features I love). Just remember, any new blades will probably feel very round and "rockery" in the front, since they won't have been flattened by years of sharpenings.

As for ROH, I agree with the idea of starting with 1/2" before trying a deeper hollow (i.e., 7/16 or 3/8). You've gained weight, so your edges are already being pressed more deeply into the ice than they used to be. Why make them feel so deep and sharp that you catch your edges? I am 5'5" and 113 lbs and I use 1/2" ROH. I tried 7/16 and my blades felt so sharp I was afraid to do turns at speed.
