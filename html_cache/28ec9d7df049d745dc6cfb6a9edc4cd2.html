I found this on Kinzies: Used Blades
Buying used blades can be a little risky. Ask the previous owner the reason why they are selling the blade. Ask how long they skated in the blade and what skills they attempted while using the blade. Ask if they purchased this blade new or used and how many times it's been sharpened.

Look at the thickness of the dull strip on the sides of the blades along the edges. They were three or four millimeters when new. If they're now thin, then the blade has been sharpened many times. The concern will be that the rocker may be distorted after many sharpenings which is almost impossible to restore without specialized equipment.

Ask the skate sharpener at your rink to examine the blade. They can tell you if the blade is bent, incorrectly mounted or obviously damaged by abuse or bad sharpening.

http://www.iceskatesnblades.com/used-skate-guide.html

I would buy from certain skaters - they are the ones who always use guards when leaving the ice, dry their blades well after every use, they have a sharpener who I use. They would have to be willing to let me take the blades to that sharpener so I can ask about longevity. If I had a choice of used gold seal revolution for the price I could get a jackson freestyle new, I'd always go for the new blade.

I'd worry more about the shape of the rocker than the wear on the blade. The wear on the blade may be fine but the rocker can be completely hosed.

1. Compare the blades side by side, do they match? Not sure? Outline the rockers on a piece of paper on top of each other to make sure.
2. Do the rockers look like the ones you plan to buy? It's harder to do, but you can try and take a look at a rocker drawing and see.
http://www.askmredge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/Blade-Radius-Explained2.jpg

On the other hand if you're buying off Ebay and you get a fantastic bargain, you might get lucky.

If there were Pattern 99s that looked in good shape for $50 with only a handful of sharpenings left, I'd buy them just to try them in my back up boots.

If you can't inspect the blades yourself, a picture is worth a thousand words. Have the seller send you 4 photos: one of each side of each blade. Make sure the rocker shape appears intact in all 4 photos and that the same amount of sharpenable blade appears to be remaining in each photo. Also, ask how many times the blades have been sharpened, and at what radius of hollow they are sharpened. A high quality MK or Wilson blade can get about 18 sharpenings in its lifetime.
