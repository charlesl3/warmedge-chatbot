Hi!

I've been having an issue with crossovers and I think it's really holding me back. I have an unsettling tendency to fall backward on forward crossovers, which mystifies my skating friends. Back in August I took a very scary fall while doing them and hit the back of my head. It wasn't very hard and I was okay, but now I think I have a mental block. I've been forcing myself to practice them and I narrowed my fear down to the motion of crossing my feet. It feels like I have to put my foot down immediately instead of having that nice, fluid motion I want to see. I can hold a one foot glide so I'm not sure what my issue is. Sometimes my weight shifts whenever I put my foot down and I think I end up falling when my weight is too far back. A friend also told me that I need to flex my foot so my toe pick doesn't hit the ice.

My coach has also been telling me to lean into them more. I can do them perfectly when I have his arm to balance on, but I think I'm leaning more with him because I know he's there to catch me if I fall. Does anyone have any tips? It feels like this is the only thing in the way of me starting my first program.

Some exercises you can do (daily):

- Start with two-foot pumps around the circle. Learn the correct up-down motion of your knees and ankles.
- Progress to alternating a pump with a stroke + chasse (without the crossover, yet). Again, feel the up-down motion. Also learn how to control the outside edge on the stroke and inside edge on the chasse.
- If you're comfortable, just do stroke + chasse, skipping the pumps.
- Now progress to stroke + crossover. Feel how you are balanced on the inside edge, similar to when you did the chasse.

I know it's tantalizing to want to achieve the fluidity of perfect crossovers, but at an early stage of learning, you should not be too caught up with small details like that. Focus on the correct basic technique first, and fluidity will come with time.

From what I understand, there are different philosophies when it comes to technique for crossovers (or any skating element). Do as your coach teaches you. My point is, there are exercises that break down each individual component of a move into small components. Rather than making repeated poor attempts at the full element, work on exercises and build up from there.

Re amount of bending knees on crossovers, regardless of how much or little you are lilting up and down, that precise movement still has to be learnt, and the best way to learn them is to do exercises.

Personally, I've had many different coaches in my lifetime and each tells me slightly different things. IMO, as long as I have figured out a way to gain good power and speed from crossovers, I don't spend too much time analyzing how much up/down I actually do.

One commonality in most people having trouble crossing over is that they are not down in their knees enough at all, at any point. You can just try this out on land - with knees too straight, it will be almost impossible to get any cross at all. The reason that you feel that you have to put your foot down immediately, and have an abrupt weight transfer, may be because you have to jerk your other foot out of the way, if the knee is too straight. If you bend down, you have a lot more 'room' for the free leg to cross over, and you can maintain your balance while you do, with nose over knees and toes. It is going to feel like you're doing a squat, but if you have your coach tape you, it'll actually look normal.

Hi Renla, you need to approach your fear to overcome it. You need to lean into the circle when doing crossovers, shifting your weight from one foot to the other while traveling in the same direction. And remember to bend your knees.

Let's say you're crossing right over left.

-Begin by pushing with your right leg (free leg) and extend your free leg back. Until you get comfortable lift your free leg higher then you normally would, then cross and drop it on to the ice transferring your weight onto that leg.. Then after you cross stretch back with your left leg extended which will now be your free leg, then lift your left leg (free leg) and take a big step into the circle and repeat.

It's important that you always stretch your free leg and shift your weight from leg to leg. If you do not shift weight your legs will get tangled and you can easily fall.

When going in this direction you are either on a left outside edge or a right inside edge.

Hope this helps.

A rule of thumb that will always keep you from falling backward: Make sure you always have your chest over your knee over your toes, no matter what edge you are on. That means deep ankle bend in addition to knee bend, and it also means keeping your back arched and leading with your chest. Pay attention to where your shoulders are, too.

Talking about crossovers takes me back a bit but everytime you come back from illness or injury it's good to brush up your crosscuts. What are crosscuts? Almost the same as crossovers except we take our non skating foot and pull from an inside edged crossing under the skating foot pushing behind until you cutting the circle and you freefoot edge is on an outside edge. In essence you end up pushing with firstly the inside edge and then outside. Doing this backwards is even easier to understand. If you can push with both edges, you can maximize your speed. Leaning into your circle helps you to gain speed too. Ever notice that when you're riding a bike or driving when you take a shap turn, you move faster? Leaning into your circle is the same. It causes you to turn faster which increases your overall skating speed for less work.

While we know that crosscuts and crossovers are the same thing, the way a beginner does them and someone who has been skating for quite a while would be different. A non-beginner would add the extra under pushes and leaning into the circle to get more speed. Beginners tend to stay virtical when doing their crossovers and may only push with the outside of the circle crossing foot. I help to teach CanSkate (learn to skate in the rest of the world) several times a week.

Practice for crossovers;

Walking crossovers; face the arena barrier glass with your body and toes. Bend both ankles and knees while keeping your hands out from your hips. Practice walking sideways to your right one foot at a time while keeping your head up looking into the glass. Then repeat to your left. Now that you can walk sideways on ice, it is time to add the crossing over your foot while still bending both ankles and knees and continuing to look into the glass. Do it 10xs to the right and to the left.

Next Forward Outside Gliding:
Go to a hockey circle, skate a few steps, then glide on both feet leaning looking into the center of your hockey circle with your outside arm in front and your inside arm clostest to the center of your circle behind you. This will make your skate blade clostest to the center of your circle go on an outside edge. Keep looking at glass barriers even when looking into your circle so that your head and shoulders stay up and you do not look at the ice.
Now lift your foot furthest from your inside circle just a bit while having it touch your skate still on the ice. This makes your balance strong.

Now that you can glide around a circle on an outside edge, you're going to add push glides.

Push Glides: While gliding on a circling on an outside edge, place your free foot down on an inside edge next to your skating foot and push outward away from your skating foot and your circle. Repeat all the way around your circle. Change from CW to CCW and repeat.

Next Gliding On Outside Edge with free foot in front. Do this all around your circle in both directions; CCW & then CW with skating knee and ankle bent much more than your free foot.

Next add your Walking Crossovers to motion and around the hockey circle going CCW using all the steps above with your toes following the circle, crossing and placing your free right foot towards the inside of your CCW circle of your once skating foot on an inside edge, then push your now skating foot outside of your skating circle, all while stepping onto your Left foot bent on an outside edge and do it all again and again. Then switch to CW direction and the right foot becomes your foot on the outside edge and your Left foot crosses the right.

You are now doing cross overs.

If anyone has anything to add, please let me know.
