I'm currently wearing the Men's Elite Suede; the previous generation leather boot which I bought at the end of 2014. The Jackson line is confusing because some of the women's models and men's models have had different names [though it appears they are consolidating the women's and men's lines under the same names recently]. Adding to the confusion, they are also in the process of revamping their line, with the higher-end leather models being replaced with lightweight synthetic models.

The Freestyle and Competitor (a different boot from the Freestyle; one model up from the Freestyle) used to be available as a boot only in their competitive line. First the Freestyle, and then the Competitor, were then downgraded to their recreational line, as a kit with blade attached. The kits are still listed as current on the Jackson website. I personally think this is a bad mistake on their part, but I guess their marketing guys are trying to push their Ultima blades onto customers [but some potential customers are turning away because they don't want to pay for a blade they don't like].

The entry model in the boot only competitive line is now the Debut, a synthetic boot introduced just several months ago. Above that is the Premiere; the website does not state what the material is. But since it does not state synthetic microfiber or new lightweight, my guess is that it's still leather [you need to verify with Jackson]. Above that is the Elite and the Supreme [for women's, the Supreme was previously a different model number under the Elite label]; these are synthetic boots that were rolled out ~mid 2016 (I believe). One issue is that since the synthetic boots are relatively new, there's no service history on how well they hold up.

So, the Debut is definitely a model to consider, if you are willing to take the risk of a brand new product. If the Premiere is in fact still traditional leather [needs verification], that might be tempting if you're conservative. And I wouldn't dismiss the Elite 5200 [for women]. It has a stiffness rating of 75. My boots have a rating of 80. I'm an adult male, 135 lbs. I concentrate on edge work and spins; don't jump any more (most I ever did were half jumps). I had no problems with break in, no problems with deep ankle bends. One advantage of the Elite 5200 is that it comes in 5 stock widths, whereas the Premiere and the Debut come in only 2 stock widths, so you have a better chance of getting a better fit with the Elite 5200. Also note that the Elite 5200 and the Premiere are formed on the Elite last, which is stock split width: the width of the heel is one width less than the width of the ball; a good combo if you need a snug heel with a roomier toe box. As long as the boot is not overly stiff, as you advance up the model line, you typically get more complex contours, leading to a more comfortable fit. So definitely at least inquire about the Elite 5200. I asked my skate tech a month ago about the Jackson 5000 series boots. Not much feedback, but so far he hasn't heard anything bad about them.

Our goal is to get 1 year out of each pair of my daughter's boots. Here is what we have experienced with both the Premieres and Elites. Hope it helps at least a bit.

My daughter got the Premires and loved them. One year later, she got a new pair (one full size up) and they never worked quite right. Both were leather boots.fyi -we used the same fitter both times.

In May 2016, she got her first pair of Elites. These were the brand new lightweight version wirh the black base on the soles and loved them. This past June, she got a new pair that are 1/2 size bigger. No compaints with either pair of Elites. For these, we used a different fitter than before but the same one in both 16 and 17.

My daughter has mostly been in Jacksons. We got used (she did not weigh enough to break them in on her own) Freestyles from when she was 4 until about 6 (she got her axel at 6). Then we switched to Reidells (can't remember why). Those give a HUGE footprint so the blade size is actually about 2 sizes bigger than a comparable Jackson blade size. After about a year, we went back to Jacksons but I think we then upgraded to Premieres (i think she was 8 and doing duubles).
