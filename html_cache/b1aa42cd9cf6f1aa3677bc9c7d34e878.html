Hi and welcome! Unfortunately, if you're adult-sized, neither of those are going to have enough support for you and it will be dangerous for you to skate in them.

There are people on this forum who can advise you about the boot brands and models that are right for your foot shape and level if you provide this information:
  • your height
  • your weight
  • your foot shape (see here)
  • whether you have high, flat, or moderate arches
But it's very important to get properly fitted for boots. If you let us know what area of the world you're in, people will be able to help you figure out your options.

Also please start lessons soon if possible! Self-teaching can lead to bad habits that take a long time to fix, as well as injury.

Good luck with your skating! Starting out for the first time is very exciting!

Hi! The Artiste and the Elles are great skates, the Elles being a skate that's typically more advanced. For my first pair of skates, I got the Elle fusion, because my size was too big for the Mystiques. The Elles are heat moldable which makes it mold to your foot better once it's baked. Stores generally sell the Elles in a W width size, and I would recommend to try them on before (if that's an option). The Jackson Elles also have a more comfy tounge, a bit more padding and a larger toe Pick. The Artistes are similar to the Mystiques, but they are designed for skaters who are only learning jumps. The Artistes are very reputable, and the Elles aren't really talked about much, but they are great skates. Pro tip: if your skates do end up being a little big (mine did because at the time I didn't know how a skate was supposed to feel like) get them heat molded (Elle fusion) or wear a thicker sock/ buy extra padding designed for ice skates. Ad a beginner, it's ok if your skates don't fit perfectly, it's only when you start learning jumps.

Hi welcome, but so much of this information is incorrect and can get a skater injured including yourself. Skates should fit like "a glove" a comfortable glove (not way too big and not too tight). They should fit perfectly no matter your stage of skating and also should be the right stiffness for you based on many things. The person we suggested boots here in this thread would not do well in any of the ones you suggested b/c they weren't stiff enough for her particular needs. Skates are a very personal thing. Also, you don't wear "very thick socks" in figure skates and add tons of "padding" you are supposed to wear the thinnest sock possible to be able to "feel" the ice.

We are glad you are here and want to be helpful, if you can give keen accurate advice.
