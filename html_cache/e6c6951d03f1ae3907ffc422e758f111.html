Has anyone ever tried this specific boot in either a stock or custom form?

Background:
I am currently using Edea Ice Flys and I am looking to see what else is on the market because I am predicting needed to replace my boots in a few months. I like how the Ice Flys are light and heat adjustable. With these boots it's the first time I have gotten a better fit (I used to have a lot of trouble with heel lift). Unfortunately the insole and foot bed of the Ice Fly sucks (in my opinion). I use a different insole in my boot which has helped make them tolerable with my high arches. These insoles also help with shock absorbance. But even so, I still experience pain. Despite all my fitting troubles with Jackson and Riedell stock boots I never had problems with the insoles and foot pain caused by them. Ordering customs from SP Teri and Harlick seems risky and orders take a long time to receive. So i'd be more interested in Riedell who I've heard can make them faster and more consistent. I am interested in the Aria, because it appears to be lighter and adjustable like the Ice Fly, but I want to hear from others who have used it.

Thanks!

Yeah, basically the stock Jackson's didn't seem like a fit for me. I have narrow feet and high arches. My feet also are split width, with my heel area being super narrow and the ball of my foot being noticeably wider than the heel area. I did wear Jackson boots while I was a beginner (using the Mystique and then the Freestyle model), but I was improperly sized by the fitter both times and at that point I had read about how Jackson's are more designed for average/wide feet and flatter arches. So I didn't trust a stock size to work and moved to Riedell. With Riedell I was put in what is probably the closest stock size that I could wear (6 AA/AAA). But really they didn't properly fit either for multiple reasons (The size 6 was a little too long, but size 5.5 seemed too short for one foot, AAA heel still wasn't holding my heel down in place, AA ball appeared to not be narrow enough because over time you could see the boot bowing in the area where you tie the laces. I tried on a 5.5 AAA/AAAA years later, before buying ice flies and it hurt so bad that I just gave up on Riedell stock boots. But on the other hand I never had an issue with the insoles, they did not cause any repeated pain.).

I haven't come across many custom Jackson skates, but that is an interesting idea since I have heard that Jackson now makes something comparable to the ice fly, which is what I think the poster above was mentioning. I was curious to see if the Riedell Aria was basically the equivalent of that or if it's more of just a typical traditional boot.

My experience with traditional boots is always having heel lift problems. It's scary to go for the custom, because I don't want to return to the same issues I had prior to ice flies, but at the same time I feel that there must be something out there that could work for me better.

Because there have been a lot of conversations about Jackson custom boots, I wanted to share a photo I had taken of the Jackson custom form the last time I was at my skate tech (Beacon Hill Skate Shop in Summit, NJ). My plan is to go with custom Jacksons the next time around (I currently wear stock Elite 4400 LCFs), so I had been asking about the process and he showed me this:

https://www.dropbox.com/s/su020tu3rcwzpqh/jackson-custom-form.jpg?dl=0

My understanding is that the company makes them, but doesn't necessarily perceive themselves as a custom bootmaker a la Harlick, SP-Teri, or Klingbeil. But you can get them. Jackson will build whatever you want, however you want it. The form shown above is merely a starting point and only available at authorized dealers. According to the tech, he considers the Jackson custom to better than Harlick in some aspects. (And maybe slightly less expensive than Harlicks? That part wasn't clear to me.)

You can also order semi-custom boots, which have been discussed all over this board before, and seem to be the best way to serve the needs of skaters who have boot width issues. I hope the OG poster has learned that split-width boots can be ordered for an additional charge of $50-75 with a turnaround of about 4 weeks (check with your dealer).

It's a shame that the Jackson website is not very good in providing information about split-width boots to prospective customers, and properly directing skaters towards a dealer for customs. (Related board suggestion: A pinned post with comprehensive boot/brand info to answer redundant questions?)

Of course, as with any boot, and this especially rings truer for customs, having a skilled fitter that knows all the ins and outs of the brands, plus a strong understanding of foot anatomy, is absolutely essential to getting the best fit possible.

I just had a fitting for custom Jackson Elites on Saturday! My left and right feet are apparently two very different sizes, and I have a huge bunion on the left foot (they can somehow build out the boot where the bunion is, so the boot could be a bit more normal width while still having space for the bunion; the fitter measured the width in front of, across, and after the bunion so that Jackson will know exactly what to do).

I had no idea until I was at the shop, that Jackson stock sizes are already split width. I found out that the "B" width stock boot is actually "B" toe with "A" heel, and that my split with C/A is actually a "B" stock boot which has had the toe box manipulated to make it a "C" (I have had my toe boxes punched and stretched so much that they are more like a "D").

Custom boots can be hundreds of dollars more than stock boots of the same model, depending on what has to be done. The stock Jackson Elite boots with no mods are about $500, but the fitter thinks my custom ones will end up costing about $900 (they better last an eternity!)
