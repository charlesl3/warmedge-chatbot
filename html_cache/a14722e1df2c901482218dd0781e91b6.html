Hello,

I've been working on my forward scratch spin for about 2 months now, and I haven't been seeing any improvement on the entry edge. I'm doing 2 right backward crossovers then stepping onto a left outside edge to enter the spin. I'm finding a couple issues with the entry edge:
First, I often hear a skidding sound when try to go deeper on the edge. I see evidence of this in the tracing as well. I think it might have to do with lateral ankle motion, because I tend to 'wing' my foot like in ballet, since I've done more ballet than skating, but I'm not sure what's causing it.
Second, the circle that I'm creating with the edge doesn't get progressively smaller like I expect it to. Before I ever see a 'hook' (the final sharp wrap of the edge as the circle gets smaller), I just stop. I don't hit my toe pick or anything, I just lose all my speed. Not sure if that has to do with knee bend or something, but I find all my rotation ends up coming from my free leg and arm coming around since I've stopped moving when that happens. I've read that I need to lean into the circle more to make the circle get smaller, but when I try to lean more, I fall. That's happened many many times and almost every time I try to make the edge deeper by leaning into the circle.

I have some videos but I don't know how to upload them. I can email them if someone would like to look at them.

Since something I see a lot on posts here is that you should ask a coach, I am working on these spins with my coach, but I only have 1 lesson a week and don't always skate at the rink where she is, so I can't ask her as easily as I can here.

Thanks!

Hi NumberOneRobot,

It is easier to watch and correct someone's errors vs reading the post. And you should listen to your coach's instruction first.
That said - I'll give this a simple whirl (no pun intended).

My honest opinion is that you're worrying way to much about edges and how you circles "look" on a spin you are just learning.
You may also be trying to hard and leaning too far inward.

Take your two back crossovers, step onto that outside edge with a slightly bent knee - but as you straighten up concentrate on finding your body balance, also called centering your spin.
You will need to find that point on your blade just behind your toepicks where your foot is comfortable and your body balanced and centered.

At that point your free leg will be out towards the side or front, and both arms will be outstretched. You may be like this for one, two, three revolutions to start.
Once you are centered this way, start bring your extremities in toward your body - slowly but with determination.
That should help you get a few revolutions at least and get you into that scratch spin final position before you exit the spin.

Once you are comfortable with the above, then start looking at the little loops made by your spin to see how well you are centering your spin; you should travel as little as possible. (That will happen with practice).

The above comments are how I learned all my spins. Don't worry so much at this point about what you hear from you blade (although you may want to have it checked to rule out an problem) or see on the ice. Work on being centered and balanced.

Good luck!

I think it's misleading when the word "lean" is used in skating. You really can't lean, or you fall into your circle. And when you do lean (not on spin entries, but on stroking), you only have the lean from the hips down. The upper body needs to shift away from the lean to prevent the skater from falling into the circle. So don't think about "leaning".

What you want to do is place your spinning foot down without turning it out like a ballerina (good observation on your part!), bend your knees and ankles (sort of a plie), then very gently push off onto that LFO edge, staying on a deeply bent knee and TWISTING to the left with your upper body and head to lead you into a circle that goes around your own left shoulder. As you do this, NO LEANING. You need to make sure your shoulders stay level as you gently sweep the left arm around to the left and draw that circle with your blade. To make sure you are getting the correct entrance edge, forget the windup part for now and just stand in an inverted T position on a line on the ice, left foot in front, right foot turned out behind it. Put your left arm in front and your right arm out to the side (L position). Now bend your knees and ankles, look to your left, push off gently onto the LFO edge and let your left arm lead you around to the left, drawing a small tight circle with your blade while keeping your right leg way behind you.

When you look at your tracing, it should be a half circle that starts at the line and comes back to the line before you hook the spin with your bottom pick and bring the free leg to the front. Once you get the entrance edge right and you've hooked the spin, remember to keep your knee slightly bent and keep pressing the ball of your blade into the ice. Never lock out your knee or you will end up on your toepick. I hope that helps!

I think that's quite normal for a scratch spin actually. You need very deep knee bend to keep your outside edge going for as long as possible (that's how you get the smaller circles). However, you should be swinging your leg around and standing up at the same time, so that by the time you are spinning, your free leg is extended and to your front so you can bring it in for the scratch spin.

Do you have trouble entering a simple one-foot spin from a windup, or is it entering the scratch spin that throws your timing and balance off? I find that it helps to practice just that outside, entrance edge (without the back crossovers) so you get used to the knee bend, the circle getting smaller, and the standing up.

First of all, sorry for assuming you were a CCW spinner in my original reply. If you spin CW on your right foot, you'll need to switch "left" and "right" for everything I said. If the camel is easier for you, you could be right about the premature free leg swing being part of the problem (since the free leg stays back on the camel). It could also be that your shoulders are more level when you go into your camel spin. I would't bring your free leg around too high because that creates a whole different set of problems. If it seems too low when you go in on a bent knee, try extending it farther back behind the skating leg (even crossed behind so you're like a dog chasing its tail) and that will make the arc wider as you bring it around and keep that foot from grazing the ice. My best scratch spins are when I pretend I'm going into a sit spin, then stand up a little as I bring the free leg around.
