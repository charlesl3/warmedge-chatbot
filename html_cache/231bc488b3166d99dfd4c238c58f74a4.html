Okay, as far as Jackson boots: the Freestyle is a 45 stiffness rating, you could try that. you could also look into just the boot of the Debut Fusion Firm which is a 50 stiffness rating. These would take you up to your single axel. I don't normally recommend this but if you don't have the means to be buying boots often you might want to also look at the Premiere Fusion which is a 65 stiffness and would take you through some doubles.

Now Risport: The Electra Light is the same stiffness rating equivalent to the Jackson Freestyle. The RF3 Pro is a 60 stiffness rating and will take you up through some doubles, IDK much about it other than that because when I started wearing Risports for freestyle for a short period, I was already doing triples. And my other Risports were dance boots.
Here's what I do know: All or most Risport are able to be fit for many different size widths. And they are mostly heat moldable, so that could be helpful for you.

Jackson will split width many of their boots BTW. I know Jackson freestyle class boots alot better since that's what I wear. (I wear the custom equivalent to their Supreme boot with a 90 stiffness.)

It's really hard to help when I don't know what they are exactly fitting and your foot etc. So this is just a loose suggestion based on what you have told me mixed with what I have known from past experience.


Good luck!

Click to expand...
Check on the Jackson website. Models listed as being built on the "Elite" last come stock in split width, with the heel one width narrower than the toe. If you need more than one width differential, you'll need a special order. Jackson has recently been revamping its line. The latest models (recently introduced) of Freestyle Fusion and Elle Fusion are both listed as being built on the Elite last (previous generations of Freestyle and Elle, which still may be being sold, weren't, so be careful to check).

FWIW, my skate tech prefers Jackson boots, and for adult beginners, he automatically recommends starting with the Freestyle. I don't know how it works in your country, but in the US, an authorized Jackson dealer [if he has nothing in stock that fits you properly] will take measurements and tracings of your feet and send them to Jackson, who will decide the proper size and width (and whether you need a special order). There should be no guessing, either on your part or on your skate tech's part.
