Hi everyone, I am new to the forum and looking to take up adult beginner skating.

My question is about location. I currently work remotely, and I have been globe-trotting and living abroad for a while. For my next destination, I'd love to go somewhere where I could really dive in to a local adult learn-to-skate program.

Are there any cities where this would be better than others? Cost is a factor, so expensive places like NYC or London might be out of the question. But I'm open to Asia, Eastern Europe, South America (??), the U.S., really anywhere. I also don't own a car, though, which might make rural/suburban destinations more difficult.

Do you have any recommendations of where I might find a great adult beginners program? Thank you

I'm not going to recommend anywhere in particular since I'm not that knowledgeable. But things to consider:

—Having a great pro shop somewhere close is a huge adventage as well.

I only know the US firsthand, but from what I gather the US probably has the best program nationally for adult skaters. Of course whether any given club is adult friendly will depend on the culture at that particular club.

You might want to look through the results of last year's Adult Nationals and get a sense of which clubs are sending the most skaters.

http://web.icenetwork.com/schedule/2017/04/2017_us_adult_champs

(Note that only the "Championship" events require skaters to qualify -- the other events are open to any eligible skaters who meet the age and test requirements. In any case, the point of this research wouldn't be to see which club has the "best" adult skaters, but rather which clubs seem to be supportive of adult skating.)

Ice time in Thailand is pretty cheap, around 5 USD +/- for 3or4 hours session. Most rink are empty during the week days. There are many English speaker coaches (many former skater-turn-coach went to 300,000 USD a year international school-definitely good English speaking). Maybe you have to make advance arrangements.

I recommend Vinpearl Ice Rink in Vietnam. There are two now, I think???? One in Hanoi and the other somewhere in the south. Relative low price for nice rink and mostly empty, not during the weekends and public holidays obviously. There are few eastern European coaches who speaks pretty good English from what I saw from TV. Only downside is too many spectators...lol. Most of mall rinks in SE Asia are full of people watching. It can be uncomfortable in a way.
