It's odd that you'd describe rentals as being stiff, because they're generally broken down and have little to no ankle support. I haven't had experience with the Macquarie fitter, but I know that the ones at other rinks in the Sydney area are similar to a shoe store employee where they just recommend you whatever your foot will go into. I got my first pair of skates like that and they worked fine, though my heel would never stay in place.

I bought my current skates online from WA by measuring my foot and sending it to the distributer. I know some people would say that you should never buy skates without trying them on first, but we really have limited options in Australia. If you're fine with the stock boots offered by the pro-shops, it isn't a bad place to start looking, but you always have options.

I cannot say enough good things about my skate sharpener- he is so skilled and knowledgeable, and doesn't charge a ridiculous amount to sharpen skates. I can PM you details if you want to go that route; my friends have had their skates sharpened at Macquarie/Canterbury and have said their service is mediocre at best.

Either way, good luck in your skating, and I hope you find a pair of skates soon!

I know the struggle of finding skates in Australia - it took me about 3 months to finally get skates hwell: The first step would be to get a few opinions on sizing. Measure your foot and send the measurements to the boot manufacturers (i.e. Jackson, Risport, Edea, etc.) and see which size they recommend. You can then do the same to get the opinion of the Australian retailer. When you actually get to the shop, make sure they put your foot on the measuring device that corresponds to the brand that you're trying on, rather than telling them which size you think you need. There is lots of information on how to measure your foot that can be found on the skate manufacturers' websites. You want to measure your foot in the sock you will be wearing to skate and get length and width measurements. I was told to stand up (i.e. put weight on the foot) as someone else traced an outline of my foot in my sock. Then measure the length of the outline in millimetres. To double-check that you got the measurement correct, stand with your heel against a wall and put a ruler next to your foot, marking where your toe stops. Then measure that distance. To measure width, measure the circumference of the ball of your foot. You can then compare this to the size charts on the manufacturer websites. There is lots of help you can find online with measuring.

Once you've got an idea of the correct size, my advice would be to go somewhere with the most choice in terms of brands to figure out which brand suits your foot best. The different boot brands fit very differently and you need the boot to fit like a glove. Since I'm in Queensland, I can't really give advice on the Sydney shops but hopefully there's one with some choice

Another thing you want to think about is the level of boot you need. This website provides a nice comparison of the boot models and the elements that they are recommended for: https://www.kinziescloset.com/skate-comparison-guide.html

Good luck!!
