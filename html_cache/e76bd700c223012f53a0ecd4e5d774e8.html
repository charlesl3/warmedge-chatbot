I don't understand why coaches suggest particular brands. They do this here in France all the time. The thing is, every brand has a different boot shape, which consequently fits a different type of foot. Skates are not like shoes and do need to fit a certain way. Risport and Edea are great brands, and both make boots that would be appropriate for your granddaughter (incidentally, don't get caught up in the "RF"....It's just part of the model name). But there are also Riedell, which are more widely available in the US and Jackson which also make very good boots that might fit your g-daughter's foot better. Risport and Riedell are both known for fitting narrow feet.

My suggestion would be to do some research online for the nearest skate shop....either to you or her, and tell her the next time you are together that you'll go visit this shop together to have a fitting. Or ask her to go and tell her you'll handle the bill. You may need to make an appointment for a fitting, so call ahead. It may seem like a lot of trouble for a beginning skater, but if the skates don't fit right, or are too stiff, they will hurt, impede her progress, and potentially disenchant her from skating. If they're too soft, the biggest risk is you buy new boots sooner, so if there's doubt between two models, I encourage erring on the side of being too soft. But a good skate technician can help with all that, and help you determine which brand, based on her foot shape, and stiffness, depending on her weight, strength and skating level. You're most likely looking at an entry level boot; these often come with a blade already attached.

You can probably get advice on shops in your/her area on here, or one of the other skating forums. If you want to do some research on the different brands and models, "Kinzies closet" has some great information. I would strongly discourage buying skates online though, especially first ones (it pains me to say that, because the Kinzies' folk are really nice), unless you know exactly what you need.....there's just too much risk involved with the fit. Based on what I see at my rink, "comfortable" is too big. Boots that are too big are difficult to control and make learning the elements difficult.

Having said all that, this isn't going to be her last pair of skates and entry level models are more forgiving on fit than the stiffer ones. So, there is some leeway here. The folks at Kinzies and Rainbo (outside of Chicago) and probably some other repubtable online dealers will be able to help you. I don't want to discourage you, rather give you the information you need to make the best decisions!

You're a fantastic Grandmother for doing this! My own grandmother subsidized a significant portion of my own skating when I was young. Your Granddaughter is a lucky girl!!!!!

Hi Barbara,

Since you use the verbiage "hires skates" I am wondering what country you are in - England perhaps? Then maybe that is why the recommendation for Risport or Edea.

At my rink there is a shelf of used skates in different sizes that are for sale. They are usually skates that a child or teen has grown out of and many times they are in great condition - especially in those child sizes. This may be a possibility at your granddaughter's rink. The sale is a win-win for both the buyer and seller.

A beginner skater does not need anything fancy and the boot should not be heavy. The advantage of your GD having her own skates is that she'll know what to expect (they will feel the same) each time she steps on the ice, as opposed to playing rental roulette.

Kudos to grandmama for doing all the research for your granddaughter!

RF3 and RF4 are different products that Risport has come out with. It's just the model name.

What you haven't mentioned is, what your granddaughter is currently learning. Is she doing jumps and spins now? She would need a good boot to support her if she is going onto more advanced skills. I would suggest looking at entry-level boots from certain brands. Ask the fitter at the store to try on the different boots that you have researched, because in the end it is not about brand, but really how well the brand's boots with your granddaughter's foot.
You can have some idea while trying them on, but it really comes down to when she tries to skate with them on ice.

Look for nice stiff boots that can support her ankle, check the tongue of the boot and its material.
Look for good insoles that wouldn't hurt her feet.
Look to see that when she tries them on, her feet won't be moving around.

These will be her first boots, so she'll definitely outgrow them. You'll have more experience with the boots as she goes on to her second or third boots in the future
