I signed up for a moves in the field competition in January, and my coach and I have both never experienced a MIF competition before. This is my first ever competition, so I am kind of nervous and curious. Did anyone do a MIF competition before? Is it like a normal competition? And I also have a few overall competition questions. I know that you have to arrive at least 1 hour earlier, but do you come with all your hair and makeup done? Is there dressing rooms? And also how much makeup do you put on? I dance, and for our stage makeup, we put on really heavy eyeshadow and basically exaggerate our features. From the skating I saw on TV, all the skaters look like they are wearing everyday make up, so do I just put on everyday makeup? And which is better- a bun or a ponytail? My coach doesn't really prefer one or the other, so I just want to see what you guys wear to competitions. Is it ok if I bring my zuca bag? Do people usually like dig up a huge suitcase and put everything in there? If someone could list the things they bring to a competition, that would be great. Thanks!

Hm. Well, I've never done a MIF comp, or known anyone who has. That said, however...

I don't think you'd need a dress. Probably a good pair of black practice pants or leggings and a neat jacket (club jacket maybe?). Simple makeup, you're not performing, or none at all, and hair just pulled back out of the way. Why would you need a dressing room? For a MIF comp you could just turn up dressed.

You can bring your zuca, just please make sure you don't park it in peoples' way (I hate it when Zuca owners do that). I've never seen anyone bring a huge suitcase to a competition, not even a proper one.

Things that are in my skate bag for a competition:
- spare tights (I usually bring two or three pairs)
- spare laces (ditto)
- sk8tape for last minute bumps
- sewing kit just in case (but you probably won't need that)
- spare hairpins, brush, hairties and hairspray (again probably not necessary for this type of comp)
- makeup bag
- screwdriver (I like to double-check my screws before I compete)
- spare glasses

For a usual competition, there may be dressing rooms, or there may not, depending on the rink. My rink doesn't have them (or not ones I'd actually get changed in, anyway) so I always turn up with dress on and hair and makeup done, though I'll usually be wearing warmup clothes over my dress. I prefer a bun for my hair, but that's because I have quite long and unruly hair so I do better with it all tucked up and pinned down out of the way. For makeup, you will want some level of exaggeration, but not too OTT.

Is it compulsory moves? If so, nice and neat skating skirt or pants are fine, wear a dress if it makes you feel more competitive, minimal make up and a a bun or ponytail,what ever looks nice and neat. There is no music, you just skate your patterns. Don't surprised if you are a low level, to find another skate doing patterns at the same time.

W are always two hours early. This way we arrive early, check in with music, check out any vendors, watch so where knows where dressing rooms are (usually a hockey room is assigned) , we can see where the judges are so she can do her program in her head and note on enter/exit ice locations. Once she is comfortable with the facilities we leave and scout the area for a walk and a snack. Her coach meets us 30 minutes before the event/her time and they do mental and physical warm ups. At the end of warm up she puts on skates.

LIST
skates - we forgot them one time and had to hurry home
dress/back up dress (if you have another comp dress, bring it just in case...)
extra tights
Hair supplies - brush, black elastics, clear elastics, hairgami or donut, hairspray, scrunchie, bow or gems. I have done hair at and before, I prefer to have hair done before we leave. One time I did not and my skater forgot her bun cover (sheer ballet style to hold in flyaways). But if you travel to a comp it is not always possible. Do a practice run so you don't forget anything. When you are done just put it all in a bag.
Any makeup you will require - for a comp my skater wears concealer as needed, eyeliner, lip (it doubles as blush), and shadow. She has those rice paper sheets for shine.
She wears shoe for warm up there
warmup jacket
Your music CD (labeled and tested) and backup
Guards and soakers
Your "emergency bag" (spare laces, band-aids, sewing kit)
Medicine - we have allergy emergencies so I pack what she needs
USFSA number or ISI number - these are on her bag as well as in both phones as contacts
Know your event number(s) and time
Directions to the rink
water/rink bag (tissues, gloves, etc are in it)

We have brought a zuca in the past. It is nice to have a place to sit when she is in her dress. You do need a bag manager. It is me or my husband. We don't leave anything personal unattended. It is locked in the car or with one of us.

I've never been to a MIF competition but I would guess most will be in a dress though maybe a somewhat simple one. And I'd think simple make-up too. I think it's funny that you felt that that the skaters on TV don't wear much make up. I've been to Grand Prix competitions and seen the skaters up close...they are heavily made up in most cases. Ice can really wash you out. But, that would not be necessary for your competition. Hair can go either way. As long as it's neat you can get away with a bun or ponytail. Sometimes girls will do braids into buns or ponytails as well. Basically you want to feel confident about your appearance so whatever makes you feel good in terms of clothes, hair and make-up is probably fine. As long as it's fine with your coach. As long as you look neat and put together it will probably be acceptable.

Like others have said a Zuca or regular skate bag is fine. Most competitions we go to have locker rooms for the girls. My daughter ususally comes dressed and made up (with maybe yoga pants under her dress to stay warm). Sometimes we do hair there but usually not. She likes to feel prepared and not rushed when she gets there. An hour before is often fine but if you skate later in the day sometimes they can get a bit off schedule. If our coach will be there all day we might touch base with him/her to see how they are doing in regards to the schedule. And always check when you get there to see where they are. We've had test sessions and competitions where we've gotten there an hour early and when we walk in the door they tell us she needs to get her skates on.

Good luck!
