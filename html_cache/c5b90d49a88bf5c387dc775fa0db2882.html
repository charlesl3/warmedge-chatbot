I’ve been skating since I was young maybe since age 5 but a few years ago I started jumps, spins and other more advanced things I was almost a preteen at that time. I live in a small town and only can skate 6-7 months per year 3 days per week with a total of 13 hours every week. I usually have 3 private 15-30 minute lessons a week and 1-2 group lessons. This past year I started my axel and double salchow and started combo spins, flying spins, and more. I landed my axel a couple times but it wasn’t consistent and never landed my double salchow (except once in a harness). I’m wondering if next skating season (starts in September) I would be able to get my double jumps. I really enjoy skating and have always dreamed of making it to the Olympics but I probably would never make it. I would like to know when some of you had started your double jumps and when you completed all double jumps. And also if anyone thinks I could finish my doubles in the next year or 2 years.

I think getting your axel and one or more doubles next season is a totally doable goal. After you get a consistent axel, a lot of people land a few doubles very quickly after that. However, it will be tough to get them all in a year or 2 with only skating 7 months - in terms of getting all up to double axel and getting them all consistent and clean. It could be just me, but it takes me a long time between landing a jump for the first time and ‘completing’ it. Is it possible to skate even a little bit in the summer or get skating specific off ice training while you’re off?

I agree with jf12 -- it sounds like you are ready to work on the easier double jumps and may well start landing some next season, but you'll need to keep practicing to get them consistent and add more doubles.

Double axel is a whole different level. Most skaters never master it. However, if you do get all the rest of the doubles consistent and you have a strong single axel by then, maybe you will be ready to give it a try in a few more years.

Where you might have issues trying to get doubles, particularly the higher level ones, is the lack of consistency in skating time. If you can only skate for half a year at a time, that's going to make it really hard to get the consistent practice you need for them. I don't do doubles myself but there are quite a few skaters at my rink who do and I notice that if they have any time off they tend to struggle for a couple of weeks after they get back.

If you can't skate year round, try to look up (or travel to a seminar if you can) off ice exercises. Training jumps off ice if done properly can really help to bridge the gap when your off the ice for an extended period and keep you on track. I'm not sure where you are located, but Signe Ronke runs an off ice program in Southern Ontario. She also produces a skating fitness magazine that might give you some things to work on off ice if you can't get to her. I'm sure there are other similar program and options, I just don't know any off hand.
