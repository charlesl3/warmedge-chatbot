Hi everyone! i'm new so don't be too harsh and i apologize for any grammar mistakes, english is not my native language.

Anyways, i wanted to ask for advice on new skates.

I started skating a month ago, and i'm working on stuff like forward and backward crossovers, spirals, shoot the duck, hops and two foot spin. I bought myself a pair of edea brio skates before starting lessons, and last week i asked my coach to give me tips on how to keep my left foot from going on the inside edge while gliding on one foot, she took a look at my skates and told me they were too soft and that because of my flat feet and pronation i had broken them in, therefore she told me to get new skates. And about how i felt with the brios, they were a bit too big for me, because the dealer told me to get a bigger size than my shoe size, they are about 10 mm bigger than they should be, i have to lace them several times during a session, and of course the fact that my heel lifted too much. but despite that, they felt fairly comfortable, they didn't hurt my feet too badly, only a couple blisters on my smaller foot, which is not too bad considering i have very sensitive feet.
I live in south america and i only have access to Edea and Risport skates. So i would like to know if any of you could recomend a skate and blade you think might be good for my needs, so i can have a better idea about what to try on (dealers aren't very helpful here, they don't even measure you).

So some information about me ...
i'm 20 years old, i weight about 50 kgs (around 110 lbs), i'm 5'2, like i said before i've been skating for a month and i'm working on basics skills. I have flat, narrow feet, and overpronation, specially on my left foot. i skate 5 days a week for about 3 hours. And i don't know if it helps at all but, compared to some of the other girls i train with i seem to be a bit more aggresive in my skating, so that might be one of the factors that caused my skates to break in too soon? i don't know.

Well i apologize for the lenght of the post and for my lack of knowledge, i'm just a baby skater . I thank you beforehand for any advice you might give me and i hope you have a great day

I'm concerned that you're skating in improperly fitting boots. Is there a way you can get properly measured and fitted? If not at a physical store, perhaps you can contact Edea/Risport and ask if they have sizing packs to send out? "just buy a size bigger than your shoe size" is woeful advice that you should never have been given.

I had to google the Brio since I'd never heard of it but I'd agree that you are now underbooted and probably need more support. Those boots look like they're designed for the people who just go every couple of weeks to wander around, not people who are learning to skate properly.

But my first suggestion would be to contact the skate companies and let them know the situation - that you have no access to a fitter and want to know the best way to measure your feet for their boots.

I think the suggestion to contact the companies is still a good one. Or at least push the store to do so.

They should be able to take the information on your height, weight, and size using the manufacturers' sizing kits which they would have been shipped when they took on the brands, as well as your current training level and get a recommendation from the manufacturers. The information in the product catalogues is made for averages, but as an adult you likely need a stronger boot for your level.

The manufacturers have an interest in making sure their representatives are selling products that are appropriate to their clientele, so don't hesitate to push a bit.

For a boot I would recommend a risport RF-3 based on your height and weight. Since you pronate you should get a canting wedge at the very least and put it in the heel. The high edge of the wedge should be towards the inside of your foot. As for blade, you are at the very beginning stages so I would suggest a MK Galaxie. It is good for beginning jumps and spins but once you get to about a lutz you'll for sure want to change, probably to a John Wilson Coronation Ace. If you measure your foot from heel to longest toe (measure both feet!) and a width measurement around the ball of the foot (both feet!) and send me the information I can use my fitting devices to suggest a size. Measure in centimeters. Also, any time time you are getting blisters that is caused by either a skate that is too big, too broken down, or improper socks. Always skate in a thin wicking sock with absolutely NO COTTON OR WOOL. The MK Galaxie blade retails at $100 USD and the RF-3 retails at $400 USD.
