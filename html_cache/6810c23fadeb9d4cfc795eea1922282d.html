I'm still early in my figure skating journey.. So take what I say with a grain of salt. I'm also semi-oblivious to the drama that happened several years back as I wasn't active in the community back then—maybe unbiased would be a better word for it.

I recently went to them in March to get my first pair of Edeas. I've honestly had a really positive experience with them. I'm coming from a Pro Shop experience where they made me stand on a stick, went 'Looks like a [this size], just order it online', and I've been skating with Jackson Artistes since January. Despite starting in January, I would say I'm advancing quickly as I'm working on single rotation jumps, one-foot spins, and advanced dance elements.

I did some research and contacted several specialty shops, but Avanta was one that had strong back-and-forth communication. The other shop that had good communication was Ice Oasis in San Mateo. Ice Oasis also had a limited inventory—they only sell Jacksons and I'm still trying to find my brand of skates. ProSwizzles is currently taking a break, but I heard he is a sharpening wizard and he only sells Edeas. We might be located in the same area as Avanta is still the closest to me. So you could theoretically get new skates from Avanta and then go to a different sharpener if you're worried about the sharpening services.

When I went to Avanta, they were able to pinpoint my struggles with the skills just by looking at my skates. I found this to be a huge plus. I found them to be extremely knowledgeable, kind, and I trust their expertise. They created a mold of my feet to then take Edea stock boots and baked them that way. They said they could take my mold for all future boots/brands as well. Two of my coaches go to Avanta and I went prior to knowing that. One coach has custom Avanta boots—she says she loves them. Another one gets Jacksons from them. So even if you're not interested in Avanta customs, they do have options.

BTW, my impression is that some people do love Avanta custom boots.

Much of the controversy has to do with the very public remarks by Karen Chen and Avanta's management. One would hope that Avanta management has learned from the controversy that openly criticizing a very well known athlete is not actually the best way to attract business, or to persuade people that Avanta provides the excellent customer service that people who buy expensive full custom equipment expect.

Or is their another reason some people say to stay away from Avanta? I.E., is there reason to believe their equipment isn't as good as other full custom skate boot providers? (For whatever reasons, every boot company has had some customers who were displeased with the results.)

Last I checked, Avanta's typical lead time on full custom boots was about 18 months (unless you pay even more for a rush job). And Harlick's lead time on full custom boots (another company that some people who want fully customized boots love) was about 24 months, though different sources gave somewhat different delay times. Both charge a lot - if I remember, about $1875 for Avanta and substantially over $2000 (US Dollars) for Harlick for a pair of boots. I don't know what shipping charges, and tariffs, would apply to you.

Last I checked, Avanta provides a way for mail order customers to do their own fittings, by using casting socks. I'm not sure if anyone else does. (Though, at least in theory, a casting sock perhaps leads to the best fit if a qualified professional puts the sock on your foot, and guides you while it hardens.) (The Youtube videos for the casting socks I am aware of - made by STS - are designed for people who know medical lingo, though you could perhaps learn that lingo yourself.) I don't know how Avanta uses the casting socks - they aren't strong and stiff enough to use as "lasts" for molding thick leather, but maybe they could be used as a mold for composite materials like fiberglass or carbon fiber, or for a shrink fit liner, or are scanned by a laser profiler, to guide the production of custom lasts? Or maybe Avanta just takes simple measurements from the socks, much as expert fitters take measurements from feet?

Realistically, unless you have very good reasons for using them, the prices charged by Avanta and Harlick for full custom boots, and the long production delays, might not be worth it for everyone. There are other skate boot companies that charge less, and produce boots faster, though I'm not sure any of them provide the degree of customization those two companies are known for. (Can you imagine someone using them for a child whose feet were still rapidly growing?) As a low level skater, I decided they weren't worth it for me.

I too feel uncomfortable and unwilling to speak freely on my experience with avantas. What I will say though, is that people should only consider Avanta if:

1) Money and time are truly no factor and you are willing and able to wait up to a year for roughly $3K boots that may not last a year and may need to be sent back and forth to CA for adjustments and repairs
2) You intend to only use the boots for coaching and just want a comfortable, traditional boot that does not need to hold up to a moderate to heavy volume of freestyle training
3) You were formerly a serious and/or high level skater in the 90s or earlier who now only skates occasionally. In my experience these are the people who you see at the rink and online raving about their Avantas. I'm sure the boots are great if you only dust them off a handful of times a month/year, but whewwwww good luck if you intend to do more than that.

So I recently decided to purchase Avanta boots, after much deliberation. Yes it's a bit of a gamble, but it's one I'm willing to take. Worst case, I'll gain some negative firsthand experience to learn from.

For me personally,

1. Money is not really a limiting factor. Ice time, lessons, club dues, etc. all add up to more. Minor correction - the boots cost $2500, not $3K. Time is a factor, but their current lead time is not an issue for me. Harlick's 2-year lead time is, but I can deal with Avanta's. Not ideal, but it's acceptable. Incidentally, some of Avanta's increased lead time is the result of some people who would otherwise buy Harlicks but unwilling to wait 2 years coming to them. Our existing skates will work until the Avantas are ready, and can fill in gaps should the Avantas need to be sent back for any adjustments.
2. I will be using the boots for a lot of hours every week, as I have recently been using my old Klingbeils. If they aren't adequate for that, it should come to light pretty quickly.
3. I have never been a high-level skater, and did not start until 2004. My current Klingbeils were made in 2008. But due to my skating style, height, weight, etc. I am hard on boots - after a year in Graf Edmonton Specials, the soles were both separating and the stitching failed at the V-notches. Repairable yes, but a surprising amount of degredation in that short of timespan. That said, years have passed and Graf may well have improved upon this, so I don't mean to disparage them.

A downside of being unwilling to be transparent about experience is that it doesn't illustrate what the real problems are to be concerned about, or how likely they are to re-occur. I do get wanting to be respectful and not over-share, though. If I end up having a bad experience, I will be transparent about it, though I will bring any issues to the manufacturer's attention first.
