Hello everyone! I have a question about buying figure skates. Currently, I'm 24 years old, 5 feet 5 inches tall (166cm), and weigh between 121-132 lbs (55-60 kg). I'm a beginner figure skater, using Edea Chorus skates, which seem suitable for my weight and skill level. They're still relatively new, so I don't need to replace them. However, I stumbled upon what seems like a great deal on Facebook – brand new Edea Pianos with John Wilson Gold Seal Revolution blades. The skates have only been used for a week by someone who suffered an injury and hasn't skated for two years. The combo is priced at 350 euros, while a new set would typically cost around 1300 euros.
I've been fitted for my current skates, and the Pianos are the same size, although I'm aware that sizing can vary between models. I've heard that the Pianos are tighter, and I feel like my Chorus skates are a bit on the roomy side. Considering all this, would it be a good idea to buy the Pianos and keep them for when my Chorus skates wear out, giving me the opportunity to upgrade at a great price?

Don’t worry about comments that it’s too advanced for you; it’s not. You only have to be concerned if it’s too tight or hard for you.

Gold Seal Revolution blades can only be sharpened well by experts, and they may charge more to do it. But blades can be changed; maybe you can resell them, if they weren't sharpened badly. But even skates only used for a week might have been stretched to fit someone (with a hair drier or heat gun, for Edeas). So they might not fit you. You could ask. If used or fitted wrong, they could already have a breakdown crease - look at the pictures. It takes more strength to flex & point your toes in stiff boots, and they might hurt. Beyond that I can't advise you.

Used equipment is always a risk. If I were you and had your feet, and could drive nearby to try them on, and try pointing my toes in them, I might think about it. I did once use very stiff boots - they weren't ideal for me, despite putting a lot of time into modifications that most people won't learn to do. They need fixing, and I haven't bothered...

I've known skaters who advanced very fast. One had danced principle roles in a "National" ballet company. She did triple axels in under a year. One was a gymnastics coach. After roughly a year he did doubles in rental skates. They were amazing. Maybe you are just like them.

Too advanced means too stiff. And no it's not the same as hockey background and it's not "equally stiff." A beginner of their height and weight do not need a boot that is rated for someone at the elite competitive level doing triples at the least. Pianos are rated 95 and for triples and quads. Someone just starting out will end up injuring themselves in the long run by wearing a boot not rated for them or their needs.
And also as far as blades are concerned, some work for some people and some don't, also some blades are for particular needs, and won't work for other needs. Some are too flat for some skaters, and they have issues with spinning on them. Some don't have the picks they need and so on....

It's not just get whatever you want and it'll work out. It doesn't work like that in figure skating.

Last but not least, very rarely are second hand boots a good idea.

Pretty much this.

Seems like there's a bit of odd gatekeeping in the figure skating community. If you can skate, as in actually skate, there is no boot that's "too stiff" for you to skate on. And I mean just basic skating. It might be harder for beginners to get knee bends and whatever to where they need to be. And they might be unnecessarily hard on your feet. But they won't magically make you injured. Again, while doing basic skating. But too soft boots can. And that's what most adults are skating on. So, getting used Pianos isn't usually a problem. Getting new Tempos is.

And blades are a whole another can of worms. People will regurgitate about different blades and the level they are supposed to be for, without any well defined specs or arguments as to why that is, other than the price on the box.
