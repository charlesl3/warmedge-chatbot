Hello all!

I’ve skated in Jacksons for quite a while now, and I just feel that they’re too big and clunky for my feet. I’m daunted by the thought of embarking on this fitting journey all on my own, so I’ve come to ask anyone who has legitimate experience fitting boots for their opinions. I’ve included data from a 3D foot scan a Jackson rep did two years ago at my home rink. I am not a boot fitter (I have never seen one post here) so I’ll respond only briefly.

In contrast to your comments about Edea (too loose in the ankle and heel lift), which are fairly specific, your comments about Jackson are a little vague. If the boot doesn’t actually fit despite the scan - ie it’s the wrong size - that’s something you should address with the Jackson rep. If the real problem is the boot is clunky or heavy, then maybe the issue is the boot model (I wear Jacksons but didn’t like Premiere) or material (I personally prefer leather to microfiber).

Since a Jackson rep came to your rink, I have to guess that you’re in area with a fair number of competitive skaters and that there are probably good fitters near you who carry multiple lines. I’d suggest you work with one of them to get the right boot and the right fit.

I can't speak to any personal experience with Riedells - but they have a reputation for being on the narrow side compared to other brands, so if Edeas were too tight in the front, I'd guess Riedells might be too?

I have moderately wide feet and I'm in the Jackson Debut right now. The fit across the ball and toes is good - but I get what you mean about them feeling a bit big and clunky. The boots are stiffer than I expected them to be based on the rating even after skating in them for two years, and they've always felt kind of heavy (mine have the traditional sole, as opposed to the Fusion sole which is lighter).

I agree with Tavi that it might be a fit issue, so definitely go to a fitter and make sure that whatever you pick, you get the right size.

For what it's worth - I'm considering switching to Risports myself, as quite a few of the more experienced skaters here like them quite a bit, and have said that they're relatively light, comfortable, and a decent fit for wide feet. Maybe see if there's a fitter near you who carries both Riedell and Risport, and give them both a try-on?

Hi and welcome. I'm the closest you will get to a fitter here (not one but know what I'm talking about as I've been in every brand, many styles of boots and blades in my elite competitive, now pro career).

What you describe with your jackson makes me think that it's your fitter, not the boot. They may have put you in too large of a boot in general for you, or even in the toebox (you may even require a split width boot for your proper fit.

Find a better fitter, who knows what they are doing. The only two brands I would put you in are Jackson or Risport for your foot.
I live in Risport (literally) LOL and can help with those (style suggestion) if need be.

Good luck!

My fitter (Jackson authorised dealer) told me that the scans aren’t a magic wand. They’ll give an idea of what would work, but then the fitter needs to do any adjustments, like punching out trouble spots, and getting the final fit.

Jacksons are a little big and clunky looking, but they fit my clunky, square looking feet, so I’m okay with that! I am due to go to my fitter for some adjustments soon because these boots were bought online from them during a point in the pandemic where I wasn’t comfortable travelling to them. We had a conversation over the phone and I sent foot tracings and measurements but that was just under a year ago, and I’ve not been fitted properly in about 3 years, so we’ll do a full fitting just to be sure (and also to have an idea where I may need to look when it’s time to change boots again).

I totally agree about the scans!

I have troublesome feet and need custom boots despite being only a recreational skater. Since I love to skate and struggled with fitters unsuccessfully tweaking stock boots for several years, the cost of customs was worth it to me. This time when I bought new boots (my second pair of customs) the fitter used the scanner. Had we relied solely on the scan, I would have been in a 4-1/2 boot (same size as my last customs which were just done with tracings). However, when I mentioned several concerns to her, she discussed them with Jackson and they ended up adding a little extra width, length, and height to the toe box. I’m so glad I was proactive because the boots are a perfect fit and hardly required breaking in or punching out.
