Having taught ice skating at this level, for most kids, it's easier to be a beginner on figure skates versus on hockey skates. Figure skates have a front toepick and a back edge, while hockey skates do not. Kids on hockey skates thus tend to tumble over (forward or backward) more than kids on figure skates. You can always switch to hockey skates later on if he decides to go in that direction.

The Jackson Softecs are a good beginner ice skate. I think I remember they come even smaller than what you need.

Have him wear gloves. Not giant hockey gloves - just regular winter gloves, ideally the thin or fuzzy stretchy kind. Just simple gloves. They're to protect his hands when he falls. Bring more than one pair with you, as if they get wet, he'll want to change them.

Don't have him dressed so bulked up he can't move. But a pair of snow pants aren't a bad idea. They're waterproof, so when he falls or when I teach him to fall down and get back up, he won't get all wet. If at all possible, please no cotton pants/jeans. Those absorb water, and will make him miserable.

I don't like those hard plastic skates. They aren't comfortable, and I actually saw them break mid-stride on one kid.

Absolutely yes on a helmet. Most rinks require one anyway.

I teach a lot of Learn to Skate classes and agree with the great advice that's already been given.

I suggest that you double check at the skate rental counter on skates for your child.
We have kids as young as 2 going on the ice in tiny rental skates. I hesitate recommending buying skates at this point because I've seen many kids all gung-ho to skate only to discover it's not for them, they fall, they get scared, etc. and that's the end of skating.
You might also see if there are any used skates for him that you could buy inexpensively. Don't worry if they are white, nobody cares these days.

It would also be good for him to try out skating at least once before the group lessons start so he doesn't feel overwhelmed in his first class.

I hope he has fun!

I am replying as someone who works at the rental skate exchange, where we have 3 types of toddler-sized skates. We have the traditional tan Reidell rental figure skates, the Reidell Soft Skates, and the Jackson Soft Skates. Of those 3 types, the ones the kids seem to do best in, and which are most requested by regular customers, are the Reidell Soft Skates.

Standard type figure skates tend to be made in the same proportions as they are for bigger kids and adults, in spite of toddlers' feet being typically chubby compared to older feet. They also tend to be uncomfortable, cutting above the ankle at times, and not supportive enough unless tied tight to the point of cutting. I try to steer customers away from them.

The Jackson Soft Skates are very sturdy, will provide great support for tots even if the skate is a little too big. They have a LOT of padding,and the heel has to be somewhat forced into the skate because the ankle padding is in the way (fine if your child isn't fussy, but if you have a child who is sensitive to physical manipulation, this is not good). These skates are difficult to get tied tight enough, and most parents seem unable to do it (these are the skates model for which I am most often asked to assist with tying, and my trick is to get leverage by pulling laces at an angle). These skates run very small compared to street shoes, and basically every kid who wears them goes up a size (size 9 shoe gets a size 10 Jackson Soft Skate). They are pretty good skates, but they have a manufacturing quality issue, in that the metal hooks bend and break, and there isn't any way to fix them without cutting open the fabric of the skate; we have had to throw a lot of them away for this reason.

The Reidell Soft Skate is my best pick of the 3, and my coworkers, the coaches, and many customers agree. They run fairly true to toddler shoe size (assuming the shoes are actually the correct size). There is a lot of padding, but not so much around the ankle that the heel has to be forced into the skate like it does for the Jackson skate. They are easier to tie properly, though still need to be pulled a little differently than the standard type of figure skates. The hooks are plastic instead of metal, so they don't bend; I have not seen any broken hooks on these skates, compared to broken hooks on about 15% of the Jackson skates with the metal hooks.

Please have an experienced skate shop tech fit the skates for your child. Toddlers can be especially difficult to fit, due to the communication barrier and fussiness they present.

We used rental skaters for my daughter's first 8-week session (she was 3 at the time). There was only 1 pair that worked so we always got there early so she could get that 1 pair. I would use rental skates for the first session again.

After that, we purchased her skates. We got some funky skates from the sporting goods store - they had a toe pick but hockey backs. Not a good choice so after about 2 sessions, we got some real beginner skates. They were Reidell pink and white ones (similar to the Softecs).

Knowing what I know now, I would have purchased the Reidellls from the beginning.
