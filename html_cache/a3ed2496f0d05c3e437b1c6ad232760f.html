Either there is a misunderstanding or he is no longer interested. Before making a change talk to him first and find out.

You say you texted him to cancel but he did not respond - are you sure he got those texts since he did not acknowledge them? Texts sometimes do not go through.

If he did not get your text, then he may think that you no-showed and his time is very much money as someone else could have booked the time.

Talk first because it is hurtful to a child - even an adult! - when your coach passes you by with no hello.

Good luck and let us know how it goes.

Lynnabelle,

So sorry that you and your daughter are going through this. Regardless if this was a matter of miscommunication about missed lessons, most instructors should be professional enough to respond back, let alone to not ignore their own students. Unfortunately, I am not surprised to hear that your daughter's coach is behaving this way. Our friends have had similar encounters with their (ex)coaches and it was terribly sad to have to explain to the kids that they weren't at fault. We are talking about coaches rolling their eyes at the kids, ignoring them, yelling at their students to not associate with former students in front of everyone, etc.

If he really is frustrated with your daughter for not getting her axel yet and is abandoning her then you need to move on. A good coach takes accountability for their student's lack of progress and stays committed to teaching. If he is going through personal issues that are affecting the lessons, then as harsh as this is to say, you are not his psychologist. You are ultimately his employer and you also have the right to leave the skating relationship. Your daughter comes first and if he is no longer able to fulfill his coaching role then you will have to find another committed coach (no matter how much your daughter adores her current coach).

Good luck with everything, it will be ok! And just remember to not have any outstanding bills with the coach.

Edited to add: I agree with the others upthread who suggest trying to reach out to him again and others. Do this first.

^^ this is a recurrent issue with other kinds of lessons...

so here is the perspective from the other side ...

let's say that I am a coach (I am not involved in figure skating)... and i have a 48 hour cancellation policy... a few of these things can happen

1) students cancel within the proper time frame... we try to reschedule.. it doesn't work out... student doesn't pay... I lose money
2) students cancel within the proper time frame... we manage to reschedule... i don't lose money BUT i had some social plans that were up in the air... or I was going to go for a walk or a skate or watch a movie... well.. .now I have to teach... so that student took over TWICE the time for the same money that week since there is NO WAY I was able to schedule something in their regular spot... (unless she was first of last of the day... but see where I am going with this).

People forget that a coach will make a schedule of their teaching time and expect students to make their appointments simply because time is indeed money ... and rescheduling isn't always easy whether it is rescheduling lessons or personal activities...

let's say a kid misses a hockey game for whatever reason... they will not get a make-up game... nor will they get their yearly fee reduced because they miss a game... they will simply forfeit that opportunity.... so why would a coach be penalized because a student takes a couple weeks off down south?

Personally, I am very flexible when dealing with students... and of course, there are some students who NEVER miss... and sometimes, if it happens that they are sick and call me only the morning of a lesson, well I will not charge them... as they have a proper standing... however, some students are always switching times and missing... and at one point, the time and money lost just makes the patience fade and whenever they do not cancel within the 48 hours, no matter the reason, they forfeit their lesson.

People need to understand that when coaching/teaching kids, there is only a small window of after school session before it gets too late for them.... and there is often more students than spots available in my schedule... so students who miss often are not perceived as good students when it comes to earning a living... and often, I have had to talk to the parents saying "attendance is not in line with the level of commitment required as far as I am concerned, therefore I will no longer teach your child"

So after having read the OP... i wonder what the situation is... was it the first time the child was missing for an extended period? Did the coach get enough of a warning... ? Di the coach even get the warning???? A text message is very annoying honestly... I would prefer a proper phone call/voice mail or definitely an email as then, with many students, a coach will have records of who is missing... I also appreciate confirmation... if I haven't replied, parents should double check that all is fine... I may have forgotten etc...

the last thing parents should want to do is to cause a coach to expect a student who is not showing up... it is very aggravating. My students tell me weeks in advance when they have holidays and that way, sometimes I have managed to reduce my workload, combining two days of students into one...

building a great "administrative" relationship with a coach is definitely a win-win situation.

As nice and willing to teach a coach is, this is his/her career and it's a job... and it's how they earn a living... I would like to see any parent react to this :

Let's imagine if your boss calls you monday morning... and say.. well this week, i don't need you 9 to 5.... so please come 11 to 7.... next week... I actually won't need you at all as well, we are closing the office for holidays so sorry, but no money for you... the following week, we are back to the usual 9 to 5 every day but friday, i would like you to come early, so let's say 7 to 11, and then... you can rest as i will need you from 10pm to midnight...

yeah.. you got it... we coaches also have lives and families and responsibilities... already, we are working irregular hours, so please, don't make it worse.
