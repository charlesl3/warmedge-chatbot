I'm an adult skater who skates 2-3 times a week and typically does a lesson every other week. I've been with the same coach for three years now, but he seems to have gone MIA. He hasn't been at the rink for almost a month and isn't answering when I text him. So ... I'm guessing that means I need to find a new coach!

I'm looking to start testing moves in the field in the next couple months. I can do the first three levels of test skills, just never actually started the testing process. Anyone have any advice on how to pick a new coach and make the switch politely? I'm also worried about it being awkward if/when he comes back to the rink and I'm with someone else.

Thanks!

Are there other adult skaters in your area? I would start the process by talking to them if there are! The adults usually know what coaches are good with adults, and which focus on the younger skaters.

Assuming you are paid up with your old coach, you should be okay with contacting other coaches, though I would be upfront with the situation for sure. You may also want to talk with the skating director at your rink. Maybe the coach had a family emergency, maybe he got fired, who knows? Hopefully a conversation with the director could be helpful!

Many of the adult skaters at my rink go back and forth between coaches on a regular basis. Some have said it is because of the schedules, some because they work on different things, some just because they like all of them. The coaches seem to have no problem with it. But I think the coaches view the teaching of adults differently than they do with children who are competing. I rarely see a competitive level child changing coaches (they sometimes have more than one, for different things, but don't switch around like the adults do). I think they see the teaching of the adults as a very casual situation. Many of them adults don't take lessons every single week, or only take 15m lessons, so the coaches may not be worried so much about losing income when adults switch coaches.

I would suggest looking for a coach at your rink who specializes in moves-in-the-field and starting to work with that coach ASAP, with the understanding that this new coach is onlyyour MIF coach, not your "main coach". If your old coach shows up again and has a good enough explanation for you to want to keep him as your coach, you can always pick up lessons with him again.
