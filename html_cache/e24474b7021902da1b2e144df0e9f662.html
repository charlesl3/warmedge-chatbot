Dear GoldenSkate, I think I've found my least favorite jump... I landed the lutz for the first time months ago, but since then it's slowly gotten worse and worse. I think I'm developing a bit of a mental block about the outside edge, and now (as my coach scolds me) I am starting to flutz. Please help me stop this process in its tracks!

The specific issues I can identify are:
  1. shifting weight from my skating leg (which I'm using to lean on the BO edge) onto the picking leg, and
  2. generating rotation.

For reference, right now what I'm doing is entering from back crossovers, switching my arms, picking behind and slightly inside the circle, and drawing in the skating leg to do a "pigeon toe." That's about where it all stops. I feel sort of "blocked" from completing the whole jump, both because I can't get my weight over to the picking foot and because I don't feel like I'm rotating anywhere. My half lutz is ok (and has actually always been my strongest half jump) but doesn't feel like I'm really rotating enough to do the full.

Does anyone have any basic tips that I can try out? My coach has been trying to give me suggestions, such crossing the picking foot more behind me, etc. but I think she's out of ideas.

Sincerely,
Lutz-less

It sounds like you are probably letting your weight get too far forward on the blade of the skating foot (I'll assume you jump CCW and call it your left foot). It's a common issue and almost always results in a flutz. And the less confident about your lutz you are, the more likely you are to keep your weight forward on the blade out of fear.
To fix that problem, you need to try to get your weight on the heel of the skating foot as you reach back to pick, and that means you need to pull your torso back. I used to think of someone pulling me back by my bra strap. Now I think about squeezing my shoulder blades together right at that moment and that generally does the trick.
Two more things to pay attention to:
(1) What is the timing of that scissoring motion with your arms? Are you bringing your right arm back and left arm forward too soon, then just riding the edge and getting stuck? Try keeping the right arm in front and left arm in back until it's time to reach back to pick, then reach back with your right foot and right arm at the same time, making sure the left arm comes to the front.
(2) .Are you on too round an outside edge as you set up the jump? Try for more of a flat diagonal, just barely an outside edge. The deeper the outside edge, the more likely you are to correct at the last second and swerve onto an inside edge as you pick..
As others have suggested, it could also help to try an entry that doesn't give you as much time to over-think it and change edge. I use the mohawk-crossover setup: LFI mohawk to get me onto a RBI edge (right arm still in front), then a left-over-right crossover with a simultaneous arm scissor to get into takeoff position on the LBO edge. The quicker you can pick, the less chance of changing edge.

If you are doing the half lutz as a warmup for the lutz, I'd suggest trying the "quarter lutz" instead It takes off backward and lands forward like the half lutz, but you land forward on the picking toe, not the opposite foot. The benefit is that it trains you to stay over the picking/landing side with your free thigh lifted and closed, just like you need to do on a real lutz. The half lutz can create the wrong kind of muscle memory where you start opening out the free side even on a full lutz.
