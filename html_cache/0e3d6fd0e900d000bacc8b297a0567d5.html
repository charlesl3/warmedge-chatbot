I'm a student,but my timetables are a bit crazy.
I prefer to go to the morning sessions and arrive around 30 min earlier. 20 min for warming up and 10 min for gearing up and and chat with the rest of skaters.
In my home country rink they don't offer us morning sessions, so I have to do with arriving 10-15 min earlier and hurrying everything up.

The days I cannot afford to arrive any earlier I'll try anyway to run around the rink a bit before entering, or jump some rope (the most effective warmup for me) even if I'll be 10 min late.
So my guess would be...try changing your timetable and if that isn't possible then spend a few minutes jumping around before entering the rink. There's also some warming up on ice exercises (usually laps around the rinks, crossrolls, change of edges on one and the other foot, or mixed mohawks exercises, and a very effective exercise that I hate that involves something that is almost like swizzles but jumping while doing them) that my coaches also make us do.

When I know I won't have adequate dedicated warm-up time before a session, I try to improvise ways to sneak in warm-ups discreetly into my daily activities or commute prior to skating.

For example, I take the bus to the rink. Standing, instead of sitting, can help to warm up your core and balance (I try to not hold on to the rails to see if I can balance through all the jolting and bumps.) If I sit, I do discreet leg raises, ankle rolls etc. behind the seats. If you are carrying your skate bag, you can use them as weights while waiting for the bus. People might look at you funny, but whatever. I don't usually carry a my skates because I leave them at the rink, but I'm usually carrying a ton of other stuff I need for my work day, so that also works. I might also do some stationary barre-like movements discreetly while waiting for the bus. If I have a long wait for the next bus, I will walk to the next bus stop.

Its no substitute for a proper warm-up, but in a pinch, anything to get my muscles working is better than nothing.

Here are some warm-up execises I regularly use, which I learnt from off-ice class. I can't promise they don't look weird, but just bite the bullet and accept the higher purpose of doing these warm-ups.

Here goes. They are listed in no particular order of execution. Again, as other posters noted, do these dynamic warm-ups only after you've done some generic jogging or jump rope.

Inch worms: get into a plank position. Walk your feet forward towards your hands as close as you can go, keeping knees straight. You should feel a stretch in your hamstrings. Walk your hands forward back to a plank position. Repeat down the length of the room. Bonus: throw in a push-up or two each time you reach a plank position.

Frankenstein walks: With both hands out in front at shoulder level, kick (in a controlled manner) one leg up to touch the opposite hand, keep leg straight. Step forward, repeat with kicking the other leg. Arm are stationary, like a zombie. Repeat down the length of the room.

Moving quad stretch: from standing, bend your knee reaching your foot towards your butt, hold it with your hands to stretch the quads. Rise onto tiptoes while you hold your stretch. Release the stretch, step forward and repeat other side. Repeat several times down the length of the room. You can modify the stretching motion to stretch your hamstrings (bring your knee to your chest) or gluts (bring your shin horizontally in front of your body at hip level).

Forward lunges: Lunge forward, keeping the back leg straight. Rise up to a tiptoe on the front lunging leg. Repeat other side. Repeat down the length of the room.

Side lunges: Similar to forward lunges, but you lunge sideways. Keep the side leg straight and rise on the lunging leg. Do a half turn to lunge on the other leg, but moving in the same overall direction. Repeat down the length of the room.

Hip circles: draw a circle with your hips, as if you're twirling a hoola hoop. Arm variations optional.

Shoulder/torso twists: keeping hips facing forward, rotate shoulders CCW and CW to and fro. Arm variations optional.

Windmill: standing feet slightly wider than shoulder width, behind forward at waist with back straight and horizontal. With one arm touching the floor and the other arm pointing to the ceiling, rotating from the shoulders and keeping back straight and horizontal, rotate so that the ceiling arm now touches the floor and the floor arm points to the ceiling. Repeat back and forth, as fast as you can. Like a windmill. Make sure your shoulder and upper torso is generating the rotation, not just your arms. Keep a strong straight back to avoid injury.

Sideways torso rotation runs: step sideways to the right, step left with left foot crossing in front of right foot, step step sideways to the right again, step left with left foot crossing behind right foot. Continue down the length of the room, stepping as fast as you can. Hips should rotate each time the left foot crosses in front of or behind the right foot, but shoulders are always parallel to the direction of motion, i.e., lead with right shoulder. Repeat in opposite direction. (Sorry, it's a bit hard to describe this in words, easier to see it being done.)
