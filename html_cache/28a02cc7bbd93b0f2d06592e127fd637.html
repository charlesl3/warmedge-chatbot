Hello! I have an Edea boot related question and was hoping if any fellow Edea skaters could help me out.

I recently switched over to the Edea Concerto boots from Jacksons and I really want to love them. I had my necessary adjustments done- punching aching spots out and widening the boot slightly as my feet are a bit wider and i have a lower arch. Even in spite of these adjustments, they have always made my feet sore whenever I've worn them.

First it was arch pain, which I assumed was because of the breaking in process, and then there was an aching pain between my big toe and the second toe, and in the ball of my feet as if I'm putting too much pressure for too much time on that area. And recently i've started to feel a little bit of aching in my heels, on the flat part of my foot right below the heel. I also feel like I may be developing a bone spur on the top of my foot, but I can't be sure if that was there already or not, so i'm not counting that as part of the issue (although it is alarming).

But, all in all, yikes! The pain does seem to go away after maybe about 30 minutes on the ice, but the aftermath is awful. My feet ache all the time when i'm off the ice. I've tried icing them, soaking in warm water to reduce what I suspect is inflammation or tense muscles, taking advil, but my feet just don't feel great.

Okay! I think that's all the pertinent info... my question! Is this... normal for an Edea break in period? I'm going to try a couple different inserts to see if it helps, but i'm not holding my breath. I did get my Edeas checked by an Edea boot fitter and I am wearing the correct size, so I don't think that's the problem. For those of you that switched out of Edeas... when did you decide that the boot just wasn't for your foot and call it quits?

Thanks in advanced for your help!

I've never worn Edeas but just want to say that it sounds like something is really really off. Having just broken in a new pair of Riedells I can commiserate on the initial uncomfortableness and even pain, but that went away once I took them off my feet. (We're doing fine now.) Perhaps you might see a doctor who could help you figure out if it's being caused by pressure on a particular area of your foot and get your skates readjusted?

Something similar happened to me when I switched from Risport to Edea. I was told by them that they were going to be better for my feet and lighter for jumps. Well, I know it wasn't that I was fit wrong, so it was definitely that Edea did not work for me. I switched to back Risport and then eventually Jackson for my freestyle boots.

I'm not saying this is the case with you, but some of us do not have feet that work with Edea no matter how hard we want to, this is something you could explore.

Good luck to you!

I guess my primary reason was that the pair of Jacksons I had were breaking down and it was time for some new boots. After trying them on though, I do have to note that I really enjoy the way the boots are made to lace up- the "locking" in of the foot is fantastic and I love the ample spacing of the tongue to allow for deep knee bending without feeling unstable. That's definitely a pro. Now if only they'd stop hurting me in other places...

My daughter is SUPER picky about shoes and after her first pair of real skates, which we purchased used and already broken in, we couldn't find a new skate she could tolerate. That's why we went to Edea. She has had zero break-in on any of her 4 pairs of Edeas but she does wear bunga pads and wouldn't skate without them. She's still very sensitive about her feet and ankles -- the placement has to be just right. Still, she will never wear a traditional boot because they all reduce her to tears. I don't think what you're experiencing is normal. I'd probably find a new fitter to see if they can help reshape the boots -- sizing isn't the only concern and not all fitters are equally skilled.

I switched from Jackson to Edea because I kept having heel slipping problems in my Jacksons. Went through about 2-3 weeks of arch pain in my Edeas and getting used to the higher heel, but the arch pain was solved when I replaced the insole with black superfeet (yellow didn't work for me). No problems since then. I also use bunga pads for my ankles but that's just because I like it a bit tighter there than usual but don't want to be pulling at the laces so much.

Seems worrying that you're having so much pain even with them off for a considerable amount of time. Probably as the others' have said you should ask a specialist about it. Or maybe your feet shape simply don't work well with Edeas. If they're the right size I don't think there should be so much pain? I know a skater who's barely started single axels who just got Concertos and was fine with them within a week. Before that he was using low-level Jacksons I believe.
