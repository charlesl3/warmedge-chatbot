You can't wear them and then return them, because you have to buy a separate blade and mount them. You'd essentially be wasting your time.

Why not the Edea Chorus? It's rated for doubles and is a little cheaper. They are still probably lighter than your Jacksons, and you need new skates anyway, but the chorus would last you well into learning your singles and maybe even your doubles. Also, the height for your jumps probably won't be / that/ affected by the weight of the skates at this level. It sounds like the skates you are using are a pretty low-level skate and you may need more support for your landings, which will help you feel confident to jump higher. If weakness is an issue I would focus on off-ice training to get stronger.

I must say, whenever I hear this kind of questions (happens a lot in my rink too) I'm left wondering about the amazing marketing team Edea must have...

Now, it's true that Edea says "you can't overboot in Edea", meaning they're more comfortable at first than other brands... but I don't trust that statement. I've never tried them on so I don't know from personal experience. I do think that getting a boot for triples before even your axel is definitely overbooting.

You'll get better results with a boot suited for your level, it will be easier to bend your knee and to break them in. It will also be fairly cheaper. It will also look better with the people around the rink —doing single toe loops with Ice Flys seems overkill if you ask me.
There are definitely other light boots you can buy and will be better suited for your skills. And lighter in your wallet too, if that's an issue. That being said, if your jumps are tiny right now it's likely due to experience and technique, and not the weight of the boot. You could do with more support to your ankles, too, of course.
As Gilbird said, a boot rated for doubles or axel/doubles will do for now and will last you some time. Having a new blade will also help you —The Glacier come with beginner blades and you'll really feel the difference when you change boots, for the better!

Remember this: The boot doesn't make the skater. Having Ice Flys won't teach you to jump. What is more: following the usual boots progress will help you when you finally get them when you have your doubles
And using that extra money from not getting Ice Flys and getting instead more time on the ice or more private lessons will be even better!

It seems like other figure skate manufacturers are coming out with lighter boots all the time to compete with Edea and latest technology.

I also feel like going from a beginner blade and boot all the way to an Ice Fly and a higher level corresponding blade is a recipe for disaster. With a bigger toe pick you're probably going to have mega-adjustment issues for a little while and then coupling that with the major jump in boot stiffness, even if Ice Flys are supposed to be easy to break in, I feel like you MIGHT hate skating because nothing you can do right now will feel anything close to the same. I'd hate for you to go through that. Maybe it would be fine BUT in a few years (or sooner depending on your training) you are going to need new boots anyway so I think it's better to wait on the Ice Flys (maybe an even better boot will be around by then that everyone wants)!

I'm in the same boat, honestly. I know it's tempting to want a flashy, pretty and allegedly comfortable boot like the Ice Fly, especially when virtually everyone I see is wearing it, but the boot alone won't make me a better skater. Time and training and focus will make me a better skater. I have most of my singles and I wanted the Ice Fly but everyone on here has suggested I do the Chorus or another boot rated for doubles and then if I ever get to a high enough level where I'm looking at the Ice Fly, I can do that. I think it's a wise decision. Maybe you could think of the Ice Fly as a reward for getting all your singles and most of your doubles, and meanwhile put the money towards ice time and training. Or other training equipment like butt pads for all the falling you're going to do when you start your axel. =)

I would recommend speaking with a good skate fitter/professional. Different skates work better for different people. I skated on one brand of skates for years with a lot of foot pain and then when I purchased my new skates the pro looked at my feet and knew right away what would and wouldn't work. I am consequently in love with my Jacksons. I have friends who wear the Ice Fly and love it and I have friends who tried it and hated it- it's a pretty expensive miss if it doesn't work.

That said, I think it's wise to think about what your skill level in the future will be with new skates- in your case it would be okay to get skates slightly stiffer than you need right now but even still that puts you in the range of the Chorus (like Ic3Rabbit said). And Edea's may or may not work for you which is why I recommend talking to a skate shop that sells different brands. You definitely don't want to buy a skate that isn't supportive enough, but you also don't need to buy a skate rated for triples.

Good luck!

I second Seren's suggestion to see a proper fitter at a skate shop to ensure that the Edea is in fact the right brand. Speaking from personal experience, my skater and I had to try a lot of brands to find the one that fit our feet the best. My skater tried Edea and it was NOT a good fit (even though it felt "comfy" in the shop). Unfortunately, the fitter that day was more interested in selling us skates then suggesting we wait for something more appropriate and it was a very expensive mistake.

Perhaps you have already tried other brands, but if not, please be flexible on the brand of skates and go with what fits the best and is appropriate to your level then what is trendy or looks nice. Edeas are great for a lot of people but not for all people, so just keep that in mind. Your feet will thank you later.

I second Ic3Rabbit (again!) on the JW Coronation Ace- it's a great blade. If you like the look of Paramounts I think they do have a blade with a similar profile. If you like the colored blades and they are the right level then I don't see why you can't consider them. If there is someone who has them you can ask that would always be best. The only thing to be careful of with Paramounts or Matrix blades is making sure there is someone near you who can sharpen them. This is getting to be less of an issue but it was definitely a part of why I didn't seriously consider Matrix blades- I would have had to drive 3 hours to Seattle to sharpen them.

As for rhinestones, I know people who put small rhinestones on their skates. As long as they are very securely attached I don't think it would be a problem.
