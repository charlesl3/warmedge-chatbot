Hello! New to this forum and was hoping to get some answers, to my plethora of questions, from more knowledgeable skaters. Having some issues with my feet/skates and looking to see if I should be upgrading my skates. So any advice is super appreciated! TL;DR version at the end.

So, I've been skating for about a year and a half now, started from adult group lessons and am taking private now. I've gone through basic 1-8 and am working now on... freestyle 1-2ish, maybe 3? I'm currently skating in Jackson Glacier 520. I'm semi-worried about continuing to practice jumps in these.

I've recently increased my skating from once a week to two-three times a week, ~two hours each session, and am experiencing some odd nerve issues with the front part of my right ankle. If i push down on a specific spot/area, i feel this electric shock tingly feeling that goes down to the front of my foot, resulting in numbness on the top of my foot between my big toe and second toe. No pain, just numbness. Does this sound like lace bite? Not sure. Haven't brought up this issue with my coach yet because I figured it might just go away on it's own w/icing and rest, but it's been a couple of weeks now and it's uncomfortable. I have been using bunga pads and don't think it's really helping. Anyone else experience this before?

Is this a sign that it is time for new skates? There is a visible crease on my current skates where my ankle bends and on the tongue where the laces are tied, but is that considered normal? And I also feel like I can never get them "tight" enough to create support and when I leave them a little loose, i feel like my feet and ankles can sort of roll side to side in the boot.

I've been looking around at different skate brands and I understand the importance of trying on different skates for different foot types, so I'll look into that, but is there any advice on looking for boots? Any thoughts about Edea chorus/overture skates and overbooting being an issue at my current level? Is it overkill to purchase separate boot and blade at this point? Should my coach be going with me to look at boots? SO MANY QUESTIONS :shocked: haha.

TL;DR- skates never feel supportive enough even when i lace them tight and tightness of skates may be causing nerve issues on front of ankle? Had skates for 1.5 years, time for new skates? Boot recommendations? Too low a level to purchase separate boot and blades?

Thanks so much for taking the time to read this and really, any advice at all would be super super helpful. Thanks!!! :luv17:

I will chime in from experience, the higher heel on Edeas did not trip me up at all. That could be though due to me adding an insert into my jacksons because the heel wasn't narrow enough, and I needed more snugness in it, which led to adding the insert thus raising my heel.

I've been in Reidell (too narrow for me), Jackson (great at first, but were still too narrow even with a custom modification to the width, and too narrow in the heel), and Edea (the Cinderella shoe of skates for me). It's all about what fits your foot best, but if you're having a nerve issue, you best chat with a skate fitter as that could be signs of an improper fit.

Yes, you definitely need new boots! Beware of buying a brand just because other people like them (like Edea, which was too narrow in the ball and instep of my foot, but too wobbly in the ankle). Most of the major boot brands make good quality boots. What makes one brand the right brand for you is the shape of the boot and how well it fits the shape of your foot. Make sure you have a good fitter who is knowledgeable about the shape of different brands of boots. Even better, go to two different places to get fitted and see what brand each one recommends. And whatever you do, make sure you get some gel ankle sleeves to protect your ankles from lacebite!

As a rule, if your boots are creasing, it's definitely time for new boots. If you've had them a year and a half and this is happening you probably need stronger boots. When you go into a skate shop, let them know what's happening and what you're working on and they'll let you know which boots to buy. Don't be afraid to try on a ton of boots until you find ones that fit perfectly, it's a big investment and you want them to be comfortable.
