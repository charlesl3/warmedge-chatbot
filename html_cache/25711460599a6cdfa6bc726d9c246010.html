I cheered for Elvis when I was a kid...it didn't matter that he was Canadian.
I saw Honda skate later, and though I have a great appreciation for him now, when I saw him then, I was NOT impressed at all.

However, National skaters get more local coverage in the news and more exposure, so it's easier to find ways of liking them.

For example, I see very little of Sean Rabbit, and a bit more of Hiroaki Sato (both slightly obscure skaters with big presence on ice) I cheer for Hiroaki. --That's not to say I won't cheer for Sean if I get to know him better.

In the top levels though, liking for me has EVERYTHING to do with style. More than Jumps, spins, spirals or even 'artistry' if I like their style, then it won't matter where they came from.

This has lead me to like most of the American guys, very few of the American girls, Most of the Russian girls, very few of the Russian guys (except Vornov), Most of the Japanese guys, and some of the Japanese Girls, Both the top French girls etc, etc.

noskates said:
Why is that my loss? Tonya was not graceful. I personally never enjoyed her skating. She looked heavy to me on the ice and that, for me, detracted from the gracefulness of her skating. That's not to say she wasn't athletic.

Seriously I don't understand what the issue here is other than someone or some are miffed because some of us prefer a figure skater that is lithe and graceful. There are skaters that are not lithe and graceful and they still win medals. The argument that Johnny Weir is Johnny Weir also makes no sense because he was trying to stay trim. But the fact that someone with a certain body type is able to rotate faster and tighter is sort of fact, isn't it?

Back to the thread topic - I definitely prefer someone that is flexible, has long lines, points their toe in a spiral, and looks like they're in control and not about to lose their balance. And I absolutely do not feel that that opinion is foolish or harmful. It's a personal choice and seemingly the personal choice of many, many coaches.
Click to expand...

The problem is that body type, weight, and appearance, are extremely sensitive topics, and comments implying that only thin, beautiful, long legged women can be lithe or graceful, or that devalue achievement or artistry at the expense of looks, don't sit well with many people.

I'm one of those who can never be blinded by nationalism, as my country's "golden times" in figure skating are long gone and are not likely to come back, ever. But I don't fret on that, I enjoy figure skating no matter what.
As for the best spirals, I root for Sasha, Michelle, Nancy, Mao, Ashley, Shizuka, Adelina, Carolina, Kristi, Yuna, ... in no particular order. There are really a lot of skaters with great spirals. The positions are nice to have imho, but the speed, edge and expression are more important to me. But I'm glad that the spiral sequence is gone, because all the skaters going into awkward positions created by CoP demands didn't do it for me ... spirals are not only about flexibility.

Good thread here.

I've been watching some "classic" skating videos recently of Sasha and Michelle and those two have my favorite spirals. Sasha for her balletic pose and extension, and Michelle for her emotion - she always seemed like the spiral was her favorite thing to do on the ice. Nicole Bobek also had a great position. Both of the Hughes sisters (Emily and Sarah) had lovely extension, especially Emily. Always thought Emily was underrated, too.

Of the more current crop, my favorite spiral is Mao's - love her use of it at the end of her Rach2 program. I also like Ashley's Charlotte spiral. Mirai has a lovely spiral but she doesn't hold it long enough anymore due to the IJS rules.
