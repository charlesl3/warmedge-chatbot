Soo a few months ago, ive purchased the Jackson Ultima Mystique for Men and at that time, At that time, being a beginner and all, i didnt knew enough about sizing. Ended up getting the perfect length but not the width so my feet are wide, the boots were narrow (The boots were shipped from Canada, and the vendor who sold it to me didnt ask me about the width size of the boot).

I dont have enough money to keep upgrading boots every few months are they are pricey when getting shipped.

Note that there are no dealers where i live (United Arab Emirates) so there is no one here who could assist me with sizing etc. but ive been relying on the online sizing charts and how to size your feet (tracing a the outline of your feet)

Im contemplating on getting the ICE FLY or Piano and hope it could last me for 1+ year. But im having second opinions. Kindly need your advice and help here.

First off, it is totally normal for your boots to fit your feet differently due to the fact that our feet are not perfectly symmetrical. If you have wide feet, then I would say and recommend Jacksons. However, I will not deter you from getting Edea if you so chose and if the fit is right because I wear Edea now.

When you measure your feet for width the best method is to use a tape clothe for an accurate reading, as well as to measure your foot when it is pressed on the ground; also, measure at the widest point. Lastly, compare your measurements with actual brand specific measurements becuase eaxh brand typically vary between each other. Example, my left foot is about 24.5 cm and my right is about 25.0 cm at the widest point. My measurements required me to either bear with pain, get custom boots, or to have a lucky fit. My one skate tech happened to have an Edea boot in an E width in my measured length size: 245 E. Perfect match, slight pain here and there due to the build of the Edea boots, but I know I will never get another brand; the C width in Edea felt way too snug for comfort, but more roomy compared to my C width in Jacksons.

Now, for the decision to get a higher end boot is up to you and your finances. However, if you aren't jumping you won't need to get, due to your suggestion, Edea Piano or Edea Ice Fly that are meant for triples and quads; with what you say you will be learning, you really don't need to have higher end boots like that and the money you would be using for the higher boots could be better used for lessons or ice times/public skates to hone your basic skills. If you can find someone that knows how to stretch or punch your boots, then you could possibly stretch or punch your boots to fit your foot better and keep your current boots to save money because they should work for doing edges, crossovers, and backward gliding. Also, Edea Piano do not come in E width, so if you have wide feet like me then Piano are out.

Most skaters go through various brands, boots and blades, until they find the right combo. So, I wish you luck!

Question: if there is no dealer in your area then who will mount the blades? Or, do you mean Edea dealer? I'm asking because high end boots, like your suggestions, do not come with the blades mounted and it is best to have a skate tech that knows Edea products.

If the Edea fit works for you, it would probably be a great brand to go with - but to save you money, I just want to echo what zjamic said: the Ice Fly/Piano is far more boot that you’ll need. Booting up a level can be a wise move for taller/heavier adult skaters - but I think the Overtures would last you a good while, and if you’re taller/heavier I’d consider the Chorus (rated for doubles) at the max. Put the money you save on boots towards a really good set of blades - they can be expensive too, and you’ll be ready for a blade upgrade far sooner than a boot upgrade (I’m still in my GAM Concepts which are rated for singles, but I upgraded the blades from the stock Ultima Mirages to MK Professionals and it made a world of difference for my turns and spins).

From what you said you are currently learning, I don't think you'd need to go above Edea Overture or Chorus. If you are still growing, much less.

Overture is the last model that's not heat-moldable from Edea, Chorus is the first that can be heat-molded, from what I remember. I got Edea Chorus last year, because realistically they will keep up with everything I will ever do: maximum double jumps. I'm 29 now and learning single jumps. (with those ridiculous 3 months of training every year, since we can only use the outdoor rinks... yeah. not all that much progress in my future^^)

You will have to replace boots after several hundred hours of skating, as they will break down. over-booting is throwing away money in that regard: if in those several hundred hours of skating, it is unrealistic that you will need the stability of the expensieve boot, you will STILL break it down after enough use. With kids that still grow, this is even more prominent, because the boots won't fit when kids reach the pooint where they might need the added stability the more expensieve boots give.

My group lesson coach uses Edea Chorus as well. Many of our coaches were like "don't buy ice flys, you won't need them". And they are right. a good boot and decent blades (I also got MK professionals, as recommended by my coaches and the local skating fitter who knows his stuff) are necessary to learn well, but always keep in mind what you will probably be learning in the lifespan of that boot and blade. (a blade can only be sharpened for up to 10-12 times max until it's done, I think? one sharpening every ~30-40 skating hours is what I was recommended? you can do the math from that onward...) you save money by not over-booting. becuase, as said before: even a more "robust" boot will break down from the prolonged use.

As the Chorus can be heat-molded it might be easier to fit, even without a knowledgeable fitter by your side. I got a recommendation for Edea because I have narrow feet, but there are different widths for Edea boots. A friend of mine has Edea Overtures, she skates at basically the same level I do, and is very happy with her skates. so... Good luck finding your perfect fit!
